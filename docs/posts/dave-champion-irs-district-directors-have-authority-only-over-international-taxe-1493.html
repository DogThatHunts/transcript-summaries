The argument against domestic income tax authority

Dave Champion asserts that the federal income tax is a "scam" that does not legally apply to the ordinary domestic earnings of U.S. citizens. He argues that while the public believes the IRS has sweeping domestic authority, a close reading of statutory delegation, Treasury orders, and federal regulations reveals that IRS District Directors only possess legal authority over customs duties and international tax matters.

The origin of internal revenue districts

According to Champion, Internal Revenue Districts were mandated by Section 7621 of the Tax Code, which directed the President to establish them. He notes that the statute itself does not define what these districts are. Under Executive Order 10289, the President delegated this authority to the Secretary of the Treasury, who subsequently issued Treasury Order 150-01 to establish the districts. Champion claims that the Treasury Department historically drafted this legislation to mislead the public, taking advantage of a Congress that he asserts lacks understanding of tax law.

Customs duties and Title 19 regulations

Champion argues that the implementing regulations for Executive Order 10289 are housed under Title 19 of the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR), Part 101, which governs "Customs Duties." Because there is no parallel implementing regulation under Title 26 (Internal Revenue), Champion contends that the physical jurisdiction of Internal Revenue Districts is legally confined to customs ports of entry, service ports, and customs stations, rather than broader domestic geographic areas.

International limits on district directors

To address income tax authority, Champion points to 26 CFR § 601.101, which outlines the duties of the "Director, Foreign Operations District" (later renamed the "Assistant Commissioner International"). The regulation states that this office administers tax laws for U.S. citizens abroad, foreign taxpayers with U.S. income, and those withholding taxes for non-resident aliens. Champion highlights a provision stating that references to a "district director" include the foreign operations director. He argues this means any income tax authority held by a District Director is strictly limited to these foreign and international categories.

The Internal Revenue Manual and the Wesley Snipes case

Champion references Part 1100 of the Internal Revenue Manual (IRM), which covers "Organization and Staffing." He claims this document shows that the IRS's Examination, Collection, and Criminal Investigation divisions exist solely under the authority of the Assistant Commissioner International. Consequently, he argues, the IRS has no active division with legal jurisdiction over the domestic earnings of U.S. citizens.

To support this, Champion discusses the tax prosecution of actor Wesley Snipes. He asserts that Snipes was convicted only because his legal team failed to introduce IRM Part 1100 into the administrative record. Champion claims that placing this manual into the record legally binds the IRS to its own jurisdictional definitions, which would have forced the government to drop the case.
