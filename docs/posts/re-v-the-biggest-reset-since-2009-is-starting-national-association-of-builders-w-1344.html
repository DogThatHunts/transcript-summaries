The biggest reset since 2009 is starting

Overview

RE-V argues that the U.S. housing market is undergoing a major price correction comparable to the 2008–2009 downturn. Home builders have cut new-build prices by 16% from their 2022 peak (from $460,000 to $387,000 median), approaching the 22% cuts seen during the 2007–2010 crisis. For the first time in U.S. history, new homes are now cheaper than existing homes—5.5% cheaper in March 2026, versus the historical norm of 15% more expensive.   5. Canada offers a preview of U.S. dynamics: Canadian home prices have fallen nearly 20% from 2022 peak, with Toronto down 25%. Because Canadian mortgages reset every five years (versus 30-year U.S. fixed rates), sellers adjust faster to higher rates—a pattern the U.S. will likely follow as mortgage holders transition.

The builder price correction in detail

RE-V presents data showing that builders have cut prices by 16% from October 2022 ($460,000 median) to 2026 ($387,000 median). This drawdown is nearly as steep as the 22% cut from 2007 to 2010, yet most homebuyers and policymakers remain unaware of the correction underway. The speaker argues this is an important signal that a real price correction is occurring, not just a temporary dip.

However, the correction has been concentrated in new builds, which represent only 15% of annual sales. Existing homes—85% of the market—have largely held their prices, creating an unusual dynamic where new homes are now cheaper than existing homes. This price inversion is historically unprecedented and signals that resale prices will eventually have to adjust downward.

Why resale prices haven't fallen yet

The primary reason existing homeowners have not cut prices is the "mortgage rate lock-in" effect. Roughly 50% of homeowners with mortgages have rates below 4%, and many view keeping their home off the market and preserving their low rate as preferable to selling at a 20% discount. As RE-V states, many owners "would rather keep that 3% rate or 3.5% rate or 4% rate" than liquidate at market-clearing prices.

This situation has an expiration date. As of 2026, more homeowners have mortgages above 6% than below 3%, and in states like Texas, Tennessee, and Florida, there are nearly double as many 6%+ rate owners as sub-3% owners. As these higher-rate owners face payment pressure and foreclosure risk, they will be forced to cut prices, triggering the broader resale market correction.

Canada as a leading indicator

RE-V highlights Canada's housing market as a potential preview of U.S. dynamics. Canadian home prices are down nearly 20% from 2022, with Toronto down 25%—the largest correction in North America outside Austin. As a result, Canadian sellers are more inclined to cut prices and liquidate properties when their payments rise. RE-V suggests this pattern will eventually play out in the U.S. as more mortgage holders transition to higher rates and face payment shock.
