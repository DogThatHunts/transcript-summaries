The case against the fossil fuel theory

Dave Champion argues that the widely accepted "fossil fuel" model—which posits that petroleum is formed from compressed ancient organic matter—is mathematically impossible. He asserts that the volume of crude oil extracted over the last 130 years vastly exceeds the physical amount of flora and fauna that could have ever existed and transformed into oil. Champion notes that the theory was first hypothesized in 1857 without scientific backing and claims it was later popularized by John D. Rockefeller around 1890 to manufacture an illusion of scarcity, thereby driving up oil prices.

The abiotic alternative

As an alternative, Champion promotes the "abiotic theory" of oil production. Under this hypothesis, the Earth's core continuously generates a byproduct that is pushed outward toward the surface by centrifugal force from the planet's rotation. This substance migrates through subterranean cracks and pools in deep cavities where humans can drill for it. Champion argues that because this process is continuous, oil is a self-replenishing resource rather than a finite fossil fuel, though he concedes that the exact rate of replenishment remains unknown.

Establishment narratives and manufactured scarcity

According to Champion, the global "establishment"—comprising the oil industry, politicians, and academic institutions—actively suppresses the abiotic theory. He claims that acknowledging a self-replenishing source of oil would undermine the narrative of scarcity, which is critical for maintaining high industry profitability.
