The core claim: why Champion asserts the income tax is a scam

Dave Champion, author of Income Tax: Shattering the Mist, argues that the United States government has executed one of the most successful propaganda campaigns in history by convincing 98% of the population that ordinary, hardworking Americans are legally required to pay income tax. He argues that the government's entire tax enforcement apparatus relies on public ignorance and intimidation rather than actual statutory authority.

The three legal classes of income tax

According to Champion, a strict reading of federal tax statutes, regulations, and Treasury decisions since 1913 reveals that Congress has only imposed the income tax on three specific classes of entities:

  1. Non-resident aliens with U.S.-source income.
  2. Foreign corporations with U.S.-source income.
  3. U.S. persons (defined as citizens, residents, or domestic corporations) who have custody and control of U.S.-source income belonging to a foreign person or corporation.

Champion explains that these "U.S. persons" are legally required to act as withholding agents, collecting the tax before the foreign entity's income is sent offshore and out of U.S. jurisdiction. He asserts that ordinary domestic citizens earning a living inside the United States do not fall into any of these categories and are therefore not subject to the tax.

Instead, Champion asserts that the secret is guarded by long-serving, high-ranking career officials, such as the IRS Chief Tax Compliance Officer (whom he identifies as Edward Killen) and a select few senior attorneys within the IRS Chief Counsel's office, the Treasury's Office of Tax Policy, and the Department of Justice's Tax Division. The "kryptonite" legal defense and due process

Champion points to the landmark Supreme Court case Brushaber v. Union Pacific Railroad Co. to argue that the income tax is legally classified as an excise tax. Because an excise tax is a levy on a government-granted privilege, and because the Supreme Court has repeatedly ruled that earning a living is an unalienable right, Champion argues that the government cannot legally tax ordinary labor as an excise.

He claims that if the IRS enforces an excise tax on ordinary citizens who are not exercising a government privilege, it unconstitutionally converts the tax into a direct tax without apportionment. To prevent a federal court from declaring the entire tax system unconstitutional, Champion claims that senior IRS enforcement officers will immediately halt enforcement actions against any citizen who correctly uses administrative due process to establish that they do not belong to the three taxable classes. This administrative retreat is what Champion refers to as "kryptonite" against the IRS.
