Bilyeu | AGI Is Here — And Society Isn’t Ready | Peter Diamandis

Peter Diamandis and Tom Bilyeu explore how the arrival of artificial general intelligence will upend employment, spark near-term turbulence, and ultimately drive radical abundance across medicine, energy, and human capability.

In this in-depth conversation, entrepreneur and futurist Peter Diamandis joins host Tom Bilyeu to discuss the imminent arrival of artificial general intelligence (AGI) and society’s lack of preparation for the fallout. Diamandis argues that the traditional post-industrial social contract—graduating from high school, attending university, and securing a corporate job—is permanently broken as companies reduce headcounts and lean on automated intelligence. While Bilyeu cautions about severe social unrest, economic displacement, and a widespread crisis of meaning, Diamandis offers a techno-optimist roadmap. He predicts a turbulent two-to-eight-year transition characterized by potential universal basic income rollouts, rapidly falling marginal costs for energy, healthcare, and education, and breakthroughs in longevity and robotics, urging individuals to adopt a creator mindset rooted in agency, purpose, and relentless curiosity.

Key insights

  • The traditional educational and career pipeline is obsolete: The institutional path from university degrees to secure corporate jobs is collapsing, evidenced by rising underemployment among young adults aged 22 to 28 alongside an explosion in solopreneurship.
  • Labor and energy demonetization: As autonomous vehicles, humanoid robotics (such as Tesla Optimus), and solar energy scale, the marginal cost of labor and foundational goods will plummet toward zero, shifting the economic paradigm from scarcity to universal high income.
  • Algorithmic resolution of hard sciences: Diamandis highlights predictions from leaders like Demis Hassabis and Dario Amodei, suggesting AI will rapidly accelerate mathematics, physics, and drug discovery, potentially curing major diseases and vastly extending human healthspan within a decade.
  • Near-term social turbulence and safety nets: Both speakers anticipate significant friction—including public backlash against data centers, youth resentment, and potential unrest—likely prompting governments to issue substantial universal basic income checks funded by money printing or tech-firm dividends.
  • The divergence of human trajectories: Humanity faces crucial cultural forks, dividing those who passively consume synthetic media from creators who harness AI tools, brain-computer interfaces (BCIs), and radical life extension to expand their capabilities.

The broken social contract and the two-to-eight-year turbulence

Diamandis asserts that AGI benchmarks have already been crossed or will fall within the next two years. The immediate casualty is the post-industrial economic structure: corporate enterprises are poised to downsize traditional workforces by up to 80%, relying instead on AI models to achieve greater operational efficiency. While prior revolutions eventually generated net-new employment, the unprecedented velocity of AI adoption creates immediate friction. Diamandis notes a growing cohort of unemployed or underemployed individuals between the ages of 22 and 28 who find entry-level corporate doorways closed.

Bilyeu emphasizes that technological transitions historically produce deep despair for generations unable to pivot quickly. With young men increasingly unable to afford housing, marriage, or basic milestones, both speakers predict an acute risk of populism, anti-AI extremism, and domestic attacks on compute infrastructure. To avoid widespread civil breakdown during this transitional chasm, Diamandis expects governments will inevitably deploy monthly stimulus checks—potentially around $3,000 per recipient—modeled after pandemic-era relief or state-level dividend programs like the Alaska Permanent Fund.

Demonetization, humanoid robotics, and universal high income

Despite the near-term shocks, Diamandis maintains that the long-term deflationary pressure of technology will drastically reduce living expenses. Pointing to Elon Musk’s concept of universal high income (UHI), he explains that abundance is driven by collapsing input costs: intelligence is becoming an on-demand commodity, while solar energy and robotic labor eliminate expensive human hours from supply chains.

When general-purpose humanoid robots reach mass manufacturing—projected at capital costs near $20,000 or lease rates of $10 per day—the baseline labor cost for construction, manufacturing, and routine services approaches zero. Paired with autonomous fleets from companies like Waymo and Tesla, consumer transportation costs could drop fivefold. Essential services such as medical diagnostics and hyper-personalized tutoring will be delivered almost entirely by software, decoupling high living standards from high monetary incomes.

Solving science, curing disease, and longevity escape velocity

Diamandis argues that the most profound and underreported AI impact will occur across the hard sciences. Deep learning models have moved beyond existing training data to explore novel mathematical territory through self-play and reasoning architectures. As these capabilities cascade into chemistry, biology, and materials science, discovery cycles that once took decades will collapse into days.

Citing Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis, Diamandis highlights the ambition to solve or treat virtually all major human diseases—including cancer, inflammatory conditions, and cardiovascular illnesses—within the next ten years. He also cites Dario Amodei’s forecast of doubling human lifespans and Ray Kurzweil’s timeline predicting longevity escape velocity by roughly 2033. If intelligence can simulate complex biological outcomes faster than physical wet labs, preventive medicine and individualized therapeutics will democratize globally.

Brain-computer interfaces and the next stage of human evolution

Looking beyond software models, Diamandis and Bilyeu examine the physical convergence of human biology and machine intelligence. Diamandis views humanity as an evolutionary biological "boot disk" designed to seed synthetic intelligence, which will inevitably spread compute into orbital infrastructure and Dyson-style solar arrays. To keep pace with superintelligent systems, humans will increasingly merge with technology via brain-computer interfaces (BCIs).

Diamandis outlines emerging neural technologies, ranging from Max Hodak’s work at Science Corp—developing a third brain hemisphere using neural stem cells to bridge biological neocortex with external chips—to non-invasive sonogenetic and laser platforms that read and write neural states via ultrasound. While Bilyeu cautions that transhumanism provokes deep psychological discomfort regarding what it means to be human, Diamandis views high-bandwidth cortical connections as a natural evolutionary step enabling cloud-level cognition and memory extension by the mid-2030s.

Reimagining education and cultivating an abundance mindset

The conversation turns to institutional reform, with Diamandis heavily criticizing contemporary primary and secondary schooling for training children for 20th-century assembly lines. Citing internal survey data showing that 70% of surveyed parents, teachers, and students believe current curricula fail to prepare youth for the future, he argues that education must abandon rote memorization in favor of adaptability, agency, and technological fluency.

To counter cultural narratives centered on dystopian science fiction, Diamandis details initiatives like the Future Vision XPRIZE, the Gemini XPRIZE hackathon, and the Abundance XPRIZE, which incentivize creators to build functional businesses or portray hopeful futures. He concludes that survival in an AI-dominated economy hinges on mindset: individuals must reject passive consumerism, ground themselves in deep personal purpose, and aggressively use accessible foundation models to launch independent enterprises.
