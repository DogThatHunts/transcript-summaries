How to get to the unified field and transcendental meditation

The scientific journey inward

SecretWithin begins by mapping the historical arc of scientific discovery: from observing surfaces three centuries ago to probing ever-deeper layers of matter. Scientists discovered molecules, then atoms, then subatomic particles—electrons, neutrons, protons. As they went deeper, the four fundamental forces collapsed into three, then two. About thirty years ago, quantum physics discovered what SecretWithin calls the unified field: the unity underlying all particles and forces of creation. Crucially, SecretWithin argues that everything manifests from this field of "no thing"—an unmanifest, transcendent source from which all things emerge. Scientists know this field exists but cannot access it through conventional means, since it is unmanifest and cannot be walked into.

The technique: transcendental meditation

SecretWithin teaches that any person can access the unified field through Transcendental Meditation, a technique distinct from concentration or contemplation (which "keep you right on the surface"). The practice works through a mantra—a specific sound, vibration, or thought given by a teacher (SecretWithin credits Maharishi with this approach). The mantra acts as "the key that opens the door," turning the mind and awareness inward. Because each deeper level of mind and intellect contains more happiness, the meditator naturally dives deeper without effort. At the borderline of intellect, one transcends—goes beyond the field of relativity and duality—to experience "pure, unbounded, infinite consciousness."

The nature of transcendental consciousness

SecretWithin describes the unified field, or transcendental consciousness, as possessing infinite qualities: creativity, intelligence, energy, love, power, bliss, and dynamic peace. This consciousness is eternal, immutable, and immortal—"it is and it will be forever." SecretWithin asserts that any human being can experience this "easily and effortlessly" through Transcendental Meditation. Once experienced, this level becomes enlivened within the individual and grows permanently.

Expansion of consciousness and the recession of negativity

SecretWithin uses the metaphor of an expanding ball of consciousness. Everyone begins with some measure of consciousness—"the I am-ness of life." When one experiences the unified field and enlivens it, this ball expands, and its qualities (creativity, intelligence, love, energy, power, bliss, dynamic peace) expand with it. As consciousness grows, negativity recedes as a side effect. SecretWithin lists what lifts away: tension, stress, anxiety, sorrow, depression, anger, hate, fear. These cannot coexist with "the light of this" unified field. SecretWithin emphasizes that this recession is effortless—negativity "lifts away without trying."

SecretWithin distinguishes between destructive anger (bitter, selfish, poisonous to oneself and others) and "righteous anger" (being strongly for or against something), noting that the former is a form of mind control that blocks creative flow and work capacity. Sorrow, held for years, also lifts away when one gains access to transcendence.

Application in schools and the power of group practice

SecretWithin advocates introducing Transcendental Meditation in schools to address widespread stress, anxiety, depression, and pharmaceutical dependency. The technique is described as easy and not requiring concentration or contemplation.

SecretWithin then introduces the concept of group coherence: when many people practice advanced techniques together, the effect becomes "quadratically more powerful" than the same number scattered. He cites the "square root of 1% of a population" as a threshold—for France, 750 to 1,000 people. SecretWithin references physicist Dr. John Hagelin's analogy of loudspeakers: two separated speakers produce one volume; placed together, they equal four speakers; three together equal nine. This is "constructive interference," a real physical phenomenon. SecretWithin compares a group generating dynamic peace to a power plant generating electricity for a community.

The radiation of peace and the end of enmity

SecretWithin argues that humans, like light bulbs, radiate their inner state. A room where a bad argument occurred retains that energy; a room filled with bliss radiates beauty. A group practicing together radiates peace, and "negativity goes, enmity and the enemy goes, without trying." SecretWithin claims that as this feeling grows, one reaches a state where "you couldn't hurt another human being. It would be like hurting yourself." He contrasts this vision of real peace with the current world, where violence persists "in the name of peace," calling it "pretty much an absurd joke" and "the old way." The new way, he argues, leads to meeting friends rather than enemies, as "the light of unity destroys the enmity in an enemy."
