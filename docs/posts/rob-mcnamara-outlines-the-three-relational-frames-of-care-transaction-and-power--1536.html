Rob McNamara outlines the three relational frames of care, transaction, and power, explaining how to diagnose the true dynamics of any relationship.

In this episode of A System of Wrong, Rob McNamara introduces a framework developed by Forrest Landry to understand the underlying dynamics of any relationship. Every connection—whether with a person, institution, or platform—operates under one of three frames: care, transaction, or power. By analyzing how decisions are made, how value flows, and whether score is kept, individuals can identify the true nature of their interactions and spot destructive mismatches.

Key takeaways

  • The three relational frames: Relationships are governed by distinct central questions: Care asks "What do you need?", Transaction asks "What is it worth?", and Power asks "Who decides?"
  • Diagnosing the active frame: McNamara notes that people's words are unreliable indicators of a relationship's true frame; instead, the frame is revealed by what the relationship actually does.
  • The score-keeping test: Score-keeping is strictly forbidden in care, an obligation in transactions (the ledger), and entirely irrelevant in power dynamics where outcomes are compelled.
  • The flow of value: Value flows toward the need in a care frame, toward a balance in a transaction, and toward the party with capacity in a power frame.
  • The danger of mismatches: When the tests for these frames disagree, a mismatch occurs, which McNamara flags as the point where relationships typically break down.
