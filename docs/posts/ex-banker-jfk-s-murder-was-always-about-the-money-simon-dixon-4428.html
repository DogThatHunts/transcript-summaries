The petrodollar and the gold crisis

Simon Dixon outlines how the petrodollar system emerged as a response to a crisis in the U.S. gold reserves. After the Korean and Vietnam Wars, the U.S. government's gold reserves—accumulated from colonial and wartime holdings—were held on two balance sheets: the U.S. Treasury and the Federal Reserve. This created a double bookkeeping system, with the gold effectively levered through derivatives markets.

Dixon notes that if all countries had demanded their gold back, the physical supply would have been insufficient due to this financial leverage. China, for instance, holds its gold and silver without participating in the hyper-financialized dollar system, suggesting a contrast between national sovereignty and global financial structures.

These actions, Dixon argues, threatened powerful financial and military interests.
