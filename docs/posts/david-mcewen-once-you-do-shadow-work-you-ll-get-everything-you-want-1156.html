David McEwen | Once you do shadow work, you'll get everything you want

Spiritual mentor David McEwen explains how releasing repressed emotions raises your baseline vibration to effortlessly attract abundance, offering three practical techniques to clear your energetic anchors.

The mechanics of the shadow and energetic anchors

David McEwen explains that when you avoid doing shadow work, your shadow absorbs your energy, leaving your internal battery constantly drained. This depletion directly hinders your ability to manifest financial success, healthy relationships, or general happiness.

McEwen uses the analogy of floating in an ocean: humans are naturally buoyant and want to rise to the surface, but unresolved shadow material acts as "energetic anchors" dragging us down into the depths. These depths correspond to the lower levels of consciousness on the Hawkins Scale of Consciousness (developed by Dr. David R. Hawkins), which include guilt, shame, fear, apathy, anger, and pride (any state vibrating below the level of courage at 200). Operating from these dense states limits your life output to only what those low frequencies can produce. By systematically releasing these anchors, you naturally float back up to higher levels of consciousness like joy, peace, willingness, acceptance, and abundance.

The scale of consciousness: we get what we are

A fundamental principle of reality creation, according to McEwen, is that "you don't get what you want; you get what you are." Wanting or longing for something from a place of lack actually vibrates at the low level of "desire," which sits just above fear on the Hawkins scale.

Instead, physical reality acts as a mirror to the frequency you frequent most often. When you clear your energetic anchors, maintaining a high level of consciousness (such as gratitude or love) becomes effortless because you are no longer being actively pulled down. McEwen claims that when his clients clear these blocks, they experience rapid, serendipitous material changes, such as unexpected financial windfalls or new relationships, because their baseline vibration has shifted.

Technique 1: the condensed letting go process

Based on Dr. David R. Hawkins' book Letting Go, McEwen outlines a practical, step-by-step emotional release method that can be practiced in two formats:

The short session (responsive)

This 90-second to 3-minute process is used in the moment you feel triggered or notice yourself slipping into a lower emotional state: 1. Wake up and become aware: Catch yourself before spiraling into autopilot. 2. Locate and identify: Feel where the emotion is physically stored in your body (e.g., gut, chest, throat) and name the pure emotion (e.g., frustration, sadness) rather than a mental descriptor like "stress." 3. Detach: Disidentify from the feeling. Remind yourself that you are not the emotion; you are the observer experiencing it (e.g., "My name is Dave, and I am simply experiencing the ride of frustration"). 4. Allow and observe: Turn your conscious spotlight onto the physical sensation. Watch it move and run its course without adding any mental commentary, stories, or blame. Unresisted emotions typically dissolve in under 90 seconds.

The long session (scheduled)

For deeper, multi-layered emotional blocks (the "iceberg"), schedule a 20-to-30-minute session: * Begin by entering a relaxed state using 5 to 10 deep diaphragmatic breaths or passive meditation. * Scan your body for tension or emotional anchors. * Apply the same locate, detach, and observe steps. * Be prepared for emotions to transition as they clear (e.g., frustration may dissolve to reveal underlying sadness, which then reveals anger). Clear each layer as it arises.

Technique 2: ho'oponopono meditation

The second tool is a traditional Hawaiian reconciliation and cleansing practice. McEwen recommends focusing your mind on a specific energetic block, person, or situation you wish to clear, and repeating the following four-phrase mantra slowly and methodically: * "I'm sorry." * "Please forgive me." * "Thank you." * "I love you."

This can be done silently or out loud (which McEwen prefers for its physical vocal resonance) for 10 to 20 minutes, often accompanied by binaural beats. He notes that clients frequently experience profound emotional releases and tears of relief during this meditation.

Technique 3: TFT pinky-tapping for forgiveness

Holding grudges or refusing to forgive creates toxic energetic cords that drain your creative power. McEwen emphasizes that forgiveness is not about condoning another person's behavior; it is about reclaiming your own energy.

Using a simplified technique from Thought Field Therapy (TFT), you can stimulate the heart meridian to facilitate deep forgiveness of others or yourself: 1. Continuously tap the side of your pinky finger (the meridian point associated with the heart). 2. State your forgiveness script aloud: "I forgive [Name/Myself] for [state the situation in a sentence or two] because [provide a reason of compassion or understanding]." 3. For example: "I forgive my mom for making me feel unsupported because she didn't know any better and lacked support herself as a child."

McEwen notes that physical reactions during any of these exercises—such as yawning, crying, coughing, or shivering—are positive signs that your physical body is successfully releasing the trapped emotional energy.
