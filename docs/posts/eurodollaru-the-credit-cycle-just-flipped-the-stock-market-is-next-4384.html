EurodollarU | The Credit Cycle Just Flipped (The Stock Market Is Next)

The credit cycle is shifting from junk debt to investment-grade spreads, signaling a growing threat to stock markets.

Did the credit cycle just completely flip?

EurodollarU explores whether the credit cycle has flipped, with stocks now at risk.

Key insights

  • Credit stress is moving up the quality ladder from weak private credit borrowers to investment-grade debt.
  • Investment-grade spreads are rising rapidly, approaching levels seen during the 2025 tariff chaos.
  • The market is no longer treating junk debt and investment-grade debt as separate economies.
  • A growing disconnect between junk and investment-grade spreads must close—one way or the other.
  • If spreads converge, equity markets will face significant pressure.

The credit cycle is changing

  • Early in the cycle, only the weakest borrowers—those with excessive leverage, poor cash flow, and limited access to financing—were affected.
  • As economic conditions deteriorate, lenders demand higher compensation, even from stable, investment-grade firms.
  • This shift shows the credit cycle is no longer confined to vulnerable companies.

Oil and inflation: a misleading signal

  • Higher oil prices raise short-term inflation but may lead to lower long-term inflation due to reduced demand.
  • The market is pricing in a disinflationary sequence: energy shocks raise prices now, but damage demand later.
  • This is reflected in Treasury market signals:
  • Five-year TIPS break-evens have risen.
  • 10-year break-evens have fallen, indicating an inverted inflation curve.
  • The five-year forward inflation rate dropped sharply—by 15 basis points in one day—since August 2024.

Evidence of systemic stress

  • CCC spreads have surged to over 1,340 basis points since September.
  • Investment-grade spreads remained tight until now, creating a historically extreme gap.
  • The divergence is unsustainable: either junk spreads are overblown or investment-grade spreads must widen.
  • Recent data shows the second possibility is becoming more likely.

Market behavior signals danger

  • Arena Capital Management lost 6.9% in October after a 15.7% drop in September.
  • Sona Asset Management lost 3.1% over two weeks, erasing 2026 gains.
  • These are large funds with no prior year-end losses.
  • The losses occurred as vulnerable firms—especially in telecom and leveraged sectors—faced sudden stress.

The self-reinforcing credit spiral

  • Falling prices create losses.
  • Losses force risk reduction: selling assets or buying protection.
  • This widens spreads, causing further losses for investors in similar positions.
  • The process becomes self-reinforcing.

Who is exposed?

  • The stress is now spreading beyond junk debt.
  • Investment-grade spreads widening expose:
  • Insurance companies
  • Pension funds
  • Banks
  • Mutual funds
  • ETFs
  • Many use leverage or have volatility limits that trigger cuts in exposure.
  • This creates a potential credit market VAR shock.

Bank capital signals growing risk

  • Investors are demanding higher spreads for AT1 bonds.
  • New AT1 bonds from Belfias Bank were priced below face value.
  • Orders for Banco BPM’s AT1 transaction shrank during sales.
  • This shows investors are no longer willing to overlook risk based on a company’s name.

The link to equities

  • Investment-grade spreads have a strong correlation with the VIX.
  • When spreads widen, the VIX rises and stock indexes fall.
  • Investment-grade debt reflects the financing conditions of large, systemically important firms—same firms in major equity indexes.
  • Stock investors focus on long-term growth; bondholders evaluate near-term cash flow and risk.
  • As credit stress grows, even strong firms face second looks.

The warning for stocks

  • Stocks bear a passing resemblance to triple C spreads, but the real risk comes from investment-grade spreads.
  • If investment-grade spreads keep widening—especially if they converge with junk spreads—
  • The VIX will rise.
  • Equity indexes will be significantly impacted.
  • This would mark the full arrival of the credit cycle.

Final assessment

  • The credit cycle has not yet reached crisis levels.
  • Investment-grade spreads are not at crisis levels, and markets could stabilize.
  • However, the structure of the cycle has changed.
  • Stress is no longer confined to private credit or triple C borrowers.
  • The full system is now being re-priced.
