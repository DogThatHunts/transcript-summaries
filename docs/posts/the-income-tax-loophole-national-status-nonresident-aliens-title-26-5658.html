Daniel Mintz introduces the concept of legal dialectics—or equivocation—where ambiguous language and double meanings are used within legal codes to confuse the public. Daniel Mintz highlights a core legal distinction between the "United States" and the "United States of America."

According to Daniel Mintz, Title 28 U.S.C. § 3002(15)(A) explicitly defines the "United States" as a federal corporation established in 1871. In contrast, the "United States of America" refers to the constitutional union of states. Title 26 analysis and nonresident alien status

Focusing on Title 26 of the Internal Revenue Code, Daniel Mintz examines Section 1.1-1, which imposes an income tax on every individual who is a citizen or resident of the United States. The statute notes that nonresident alien individuals are taxed only to the extent provided in Sections 871(b) and 877(b).

Daniel Mintz breaks down these sections to argue that nonresident aliens face tax liability under very limited conditions:

  • Section 877(b): Governs tax obligations for individuals who lost U.S. citizenship within the preceding ten years (expatriation tax).
  • Section 871(b) and Section 872(a): Specifies that gross income for a nonresident alien includes only income derived from sources within the federal corporation or connected with a trade or business inside the federal corporation.

Daniel Mintz concludes that for nationals who do not work for the federal government or operate inside Washington, D.C., there is no statutory filing requirement or tax liability for federal, state, or local personal income taxes.
