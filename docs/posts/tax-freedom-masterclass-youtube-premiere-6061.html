Core argument: the tax applies only to the District of Columbia

  • Hughes reads 26 U.S.C. § 7701, which defines "United States" for federal income tax purposes as including only the District of Columbia, and "State" as including only the District of Columbia.
  • He cites the Supreme Court principle that an explicit statutory definition excludes unstated meanings; therefore the 50 states are excluded from the tax's reach.
  • He contrasts this with a petroleum-tax subsection (26 U.S.C. § 4612) where "United States" explicitly lists the 50 states, arguing Congress knows how to include the 50 states when it intends to.

American nationals vs. U.S. citizens

  • Hughes distinguishes "American nationals" (born in the 50 states or naturalized) from "U.S. citizens" (residents of D.C. or territories).
  • Under 26 U.S.C. § 7701(b)(1)(B), American nationals are "non-resident aliens" for tax purposes.
  • Non-resident aliens may elect to be treated as U.S. residents (i.e., taxpayers) under 26 U.S.C. § 6013(g); checking the filing-status boxes on Form 1040 constitutes that election.

The system is voluntary
