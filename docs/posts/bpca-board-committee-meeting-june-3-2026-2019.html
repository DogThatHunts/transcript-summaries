BPCA Board Committee Meeting – June 3, 2026

The Battery Park City Authority board navigates intense community feedback on construction impacts while approving key settlements, multi-million dollar contracts, and major coastal resiliency updates.

The Battery Park City Authority (BPCA) Board Committee met on June 3, 2026, to address public concerns regarding construction disruptions, review major coastal resiliency milestones, and approve several key administrative and financial resolutions. Local residents voiced frustrations over neighborhood navigability, tree removals, and environmental monitoring transparency. In response, BPCA leadership highlighted progress on the South and Northwest Resiliency Projects, including the reopening of pedestrian paths connecting Battery Park City to the Battery, and detailed an extensive summer programming lineup at Wagner Park. The board also approved a $3.1 million global settlement with Pasilko Bove to resolve South Resiliency delay claims, authorized a $1.15 million work order increase for the Wagner Park pavilion, and finalized several service contracts spanning insurance, investment advisory, and habitat monitoring.

Key insights

  • Resiliency project delays and recovery: The Northwest Resiliency Project is currently tracking 56 days behind schedule (an improvement from 73 days), with a large portion of the delay attributed to difficulties in securing necessary access agreements and easements.
  • South Resiliency budget and settlements: The South Resiliency Project's budget overage has been reduced from $10 million to $6.69 million. To help close out the project, the board approved a $3.1 million global settlement with contractor Pasilko Bove (PBJV) to resolve $5.2 million in disputed delay and impact claims.
  • Tree canopy replacement strategy: Addressing intense community concern over the planned removal of roughly 500 mature trees, BPCA's Ryan (Parks Operations) clarified that the authority will plant 525 diverse, nursery-selected trees (ranging from 10 to 16 feet tall) to establish a more resilient urban ecosystem.
  • Transparency and environmental monitoring concerns: Resident Mark Leibman criticized the authority's four-month delay in releasing independent air monitoring reports, urging the BPCA to provide plain-English summaries and expand public disclosures to include sound, vibration, and water quality data.
  • Organizational transitions: BPCA announced key leadership appointments, welcoming Stacey Flanagan as Chief Operating Officer and Kian Gourley as Director of Public Safety, while preparing for a major office relocation to the building's 15th floor in September.

Public comments and community concerns

Several local residents raised concerns regarding the ongoing disruption from the resiliency construction projects, neighborhood maintenance, and financial transparency:

  • Jonathan Johnson reported issues with overflowing garbage cans, double-parked commercial vehicles, and a lack of safety standards on Albany Street, where large trucks back down without traffic directors. He also expressed concern over rising land leases and falling rents ahead of the 2040 land valuation "reckoning day."
  • Mary Ann criticized BPCA's responsiveness, citing unaddressed requests for a knife grinder at the farmers market (displaced by Wagner Park work), class space for senior chair yoga, and clear pedestrian maps for Esplanade construction zones. She noted that 73% of her condo's monthly common charges now go directly to the authority.
  • Felipe Serra challenged the planned removal of 80% of the trees on the South Esplanade. He argued that the design is applying a conservative interpretation of USACE (Army Corps) 15-foot clear zone guidelines, even though the project is not a USACE project. He requested a "retention first" alternative to evaluate trees individually and called for an independent design review.
  • Mark Leibman urged the authority to hold a design competition for Albany Street and Rector Place. He criticized the four-month delay in releasing independent air monitoring reports and requested that future updates be in plain English. He also urged the BPCA to disclose sound and vibration data, perform water quality testing following a recent fire hydrant incident, and improve response times to public inquiries.

Resiliency project updates and tree management

BPCA leadership provided progress reports on the major coastal resiliency portfolios:

  • South Resiliency Project: Angela Pinsky reported that Wagner Park is 99% complete, with punch list work underway. Pedestrian connections between Battery Park City and the Battery (at the Verrazano statue and Pier A) reopened over Memorial Day weekend. Substantial completion of Pier A Plaza is targeted for July 2, 2026, in time for July 4th celebrations.
  • Northwest Resiliency Project: Heather and Rami reported active construction in Reach 7 (flood barrier installation), Rector Tidegate, and Vesey Street. The Albany Street Tidegate is expected to wrap up by the end of June. Access along Brookfield Place will close on June 6, having been kept open temporarily for July 4th crowd control. The project is currently 56 days behind schedule due to easement and access agreement delays.
  • Tree canopy replacement: In response to public feedback, Ryan (Parks Operations) detailed the replanting strategy. The project will remove approximately 500 trees (including 40 in poor health) and plant 525 new trees, representing 68 diverse species (including river birch, tupelo, white oak, and magnolia). The new trees will be nursery-selected, measuring 3 to 8 inches in caliper and 10 to 16 feet in height, designed for high resistance to disease, salinity, and drought.

Financial reports and diversity utilization

  • Investment portfolio: Pam reported that the authority's investment portfolio stands at $1.2 billion, with 90% concentrated in U.S. Treasuries. The investment committee is diversifying holdings into commercial paper and municipal securities.
  • MWBE utilization: Janelia reported that during Q4 of FY25-26, the authority recorded $48 million in eligible spend, with $9.6 million (20%) paid to MWBE firms. For the South Resiliency Project, MWBE/SDVOB utilization reached 24% ($55.9 million), exceeding the 18% goal. For the Northwest Resiliency Project, MWBE/SDVOB commitments stand at 32% ($565 million) of the $1.77 billion contract value, against a 36% goal.

Board resolutions and contract approvals

The board voted to approve several key resolutions and administrative contracts:

  • Pasilko Bove (PBJV) settlement: Approved a $3.1 million global settlement with PBJV to resolve $5.2 million in disputed delay and impact claims on the South Resiliency Project.
  • EW Howell contract amendment: Approved a $1.15 million work order allowance increase for Package 3 (Wagner Park pavilion) to cover field changes and acceleration costs.
  • Ramboll Americas contract: Approved a 39-month contract (with three one-year renewal options) not to exceed $930,583 for state-mandated five-year habitat monitoring at Pier A Inlet.
  • PFM Asset Management contract: Approved a three-year contract (with two one-year renewals) not to exceed $2 million for investment advisory services.
  • Municipal advisors panel: Approved a pre-qualified panel consisting of Acacia Financial Group, CSG Advisors, and Mahanti Gargiulo for a three-year term, with a $1 million cap on advisory services.
  • Bond fund reallocation: Authorized the reallocation of unutilized bond funds ($1 million from 2013 bonds and $6.94 million from 2019 bonds) to general infrastructure.
  • 2026-2027 Insurance program: Authorized the binding of the annual insurance program for a premium not to exceed $6,694,035, representing a 5.6% year-over-year increase driven by the addition of the $90 million Wagner Park Pavilion.
  • Risa Heller Communications amendment: Approved a 12-month contract extension to July 31, 2027, increasing the contract by $180,000 ($15,000 per month) for public relations and newsletter support.
  • Shoreline Graphics contract: Approved a three-year printing and mailing services contract (with two one-year extensions) not to exceed $375,000.
  • ESP Security contract: Approved a three-year contract (with two one-year extensions) not to exceed $744,750 for school crossing guard services.
  • Aqua Treat contract: Approved a three-year contract not to exceed $50,000 for HVAC chemical treatment services.
