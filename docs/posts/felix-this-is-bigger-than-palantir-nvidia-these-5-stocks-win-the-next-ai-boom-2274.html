Timing and market patterns

Felix teaches that four repeating patterns govern growth stocks: base (sideways consolidation), climbing (rapid ascent), tired (topping out, often short-lived), and collapse (faster descent than ascent—mountains take longer to climb than to run down).

The mistake retail investors make: they buy late in the climbing phase, then hold or average down as the stock collapses. This is "soul-destroying." Felix's goal is to buy approximately at the start of the climb and sell approximately at the top of the tired phase—not perfectly, but avoiding catastrophic losses.

He illustrates with Oklo: it went up 160% since his last video, but if you held it all the way down, you'd be negative. The key is recognizing when everyone who wants to sell has sold, at which point a new sideways base forms and the next opportunity emerges.

Three disciplines to avoid disaster

  1. Timing. Buy only during the upward phase, not late in the climb or during the collapse. Felix emphasizes this is not about perfect timing (which is "ludicrous"), but about avoiding getting things terribly wrong through good risk management.

  2. Position sizing. If a stock goes up 5x and you have 2% of your portfolio in it, you now have 10%. But if you put 30% into a high-risk play and it goes to zero, your retirement is delayed by years. Position small, even smaller than you think.

  3. Exit rules. Write your exit rule before you enter. Retail investors fail because they buy on emotion and sell on emotion—or freeze entirely. A written plan prevents panic.
