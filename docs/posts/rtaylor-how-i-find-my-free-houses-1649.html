The low-equity opportunity

Many sellers face a brutal choice: lose $8,000–$15,000 on a traditional sale (after realtor commissions), or accept a $0-down takeover and lose only closing costs (~$6,000). He presents a hypothetical: if a seller owes $300,000 and lists at $300,000, they'll receive an offer at $275,000 (buyer's market), pay 6% realtor commission, and walk away with $258,000—a $42,000 loss. Accepting a $0-down takeover with $6,000 closing costs is preferable.

The mechanics: subject-to and trust structures

RTaylor emphasizes that debt and ownership are separate. A parent can own a house while the child's name is on the mortgage (or vice versa). He uses trusts to separate ownership from debt:

  • The seller transfers the property into a trust.
  • RTaylor buys the controlling interest of the trust (e.g., 90%) while the seller retains 10%.
  • RTaylor becomes the executor and decision-maker.
  • The original mortgage remains in the seller's name.
  • The seller stays on the deed to avoid violating the due-on-sale clause.

This structure allows him to own the property, claim depreciation (accelerated depreciation lets him claim 27.5 years of deductions upfront), and reduce his tax bill. He claims that after making $3 million in 2024, he paid no federal taxes due to real estate deductions.

Alternatively, he uses "wraps" (wraparound mortgages) where the seller holds title and RTaylor controls the property via a master lease agreement. Wraps close in 3–4 days with an attorney; trusts go through a title company (Gold Key TC is his recommended service).

Loan assumptions and alternatives

Not all loans are assumable. Conventional loans typically are not; FHA and VA loans usually are. For non-assumable loans, RTaylor uses trusts. He owns multiple properties with VA and conventional loans held in trusts, verified by public records (e.g., 7605 Mergenzer, Tuscaloosa, Alabama; 1347 Harvard Avenue, Memphis, Tennessee).

For smaller markets with fewer opportunities, he recommends asking sellers to assume the loan for $0 down and to pay closing costs. This clears the seller from the property entirely and may be more palatable than creative financing.
