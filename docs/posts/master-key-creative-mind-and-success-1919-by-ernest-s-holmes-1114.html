Master Key | Creative Mind and Success (1919) by Ernest S. Holmes

A foundational New Thought text exploring how individual consciousness acts as a creative conduit for the infinite mind to shape health, wealth, and human relationships.

The creative power of the one mind

The nature of truth and consciousness

Ernest S. Holmes establishes that life is a singular, conscious, and spiritual reality. Because humans possess consciousness and the ability to think, they must originate from this primary life force. He asserts that the physical world is not an illusion but a divine reality created by God out of Himself through self-knowing or thinking. Man exists as an individual "center of God in God," differing from the universal spirit in degree but not in quality. Holmes explains that free will is essential to this individuality; without the power of choice, humans would merely be automatons.

The law of cause and effect in human conditions

According to Holmes, circumstances and conditions are never primary causes; they are always effects held in place by the mind. He argues that individuals set the law of cause and effect in motion through their thoughts, meaning each person is the silent cause of their own success or failure. Ignorance of this mental law (sometimes called karma) binds people, while understanding it brings freedom.

To illustrate the creative power of unconscious thought, Holmes shares a contested anecdote of a female patient who, after having a 50-pound water growth surgically removed, saw it return within days. He claims she unconsciously created the growth by drawing elements from the air through her thought patterns. Holmes also asserts that Jesus understood these mental laws perfectly, allowing him to perform what others called miracles, such as calling forth bread from the "ethers of life." He emphasizes that Jesus was not God, but rather a manifestation of God, a status he claims is shared by all people.

The mechanics of mental attraction and equivalence

Holmes explains that individuals are surrounded by a personal "thought atmosphere" or aura that acts as a magnet, attracting conditions that match their inner mental states. He introduces the concept of the "mental equivalent," asserting that individuals cannot manifest success, health, or wealth beyond their internal capacity to embody and believe in those concepts.

Many people fail to demonstrate their desires because they merely wish or long for things. Holmes insists that the law of mind requires an "already-having-received" attitude, providing a complete mental mold into which the creative spirit can pour itself. He advises keeping plans and desires entirely secret. Sharing them with others can introduce doubt and negative suggestions, which divides and weakens the "single stream of thought" necessary for manifestation.

Practical application: business, money, and activity

Holmes refutes the idea that money is inherently evil, defining it instead as a spiritual idea and a good expression of life. To acquire wealth, individuals must build a "money consciousness" or "million dollar consciousness" by daily affirming their unity with infinite supply.

He emphasizes that the universe is defined by constant motion and activity. Therefore, physical action must accompany active thinking. He suggests that a business owner must physically move goods and maintain an alert, active presence to project an atmosphere of success. To break stagnant mental habits, Holmes suggests making physical changes in one's environment, such as moving furniture around or changing daily routines to stimulate mental change.

The practice of mental treatment and spiritual guidance

Holmes defines a "treatment" as a mental process where the practitioner completely convinces themselves of a desired reality, resting in the quiet conviction that the universal mind will execute it. This does not require unusual physical sensations, emotional thrills, or psychic experiences. He outlines a method for receiving guidance from the infinite: sitting in silence, visualizing the desired outcome, and waiting for an intuitive idea to take form.

To demonstrate this, Holmes shares an anecdote of a businessman whose partners' firm was on the brink of bankruptcy. After spending three days in deep meditation claiming supreme guidance, the man formulated a complete business strategy that successfully restored the company's prosperity.

Finally, Holmes discusses transcending past mistakes or negative "karma." He asserts that when an individual changes their mental attitude and stops acting in error, the flow of the law is reversed, effectively blotting out past consequences without breaking the law itself.
