SecretWithin | The 30 Day Challenge| Earl Nightingale| Pay The Price.

A 30‑day challenge based on Earl Nightingale’s ‘Pay the Price’, guiding you to define a single goal, master your thoughts, and serve others to unlock lasting success.

Overview

The narrator (SecretWithin) introduces Earl Nightingale’s 1956 recording Pay the Price and urges listeners to undertake a 30‑day test that will, if followed, "completely change your life for the better." The core message is that success requires paying a price – effort, thought‑control, courage, and service – and that the results are proportional to the effort invested.

Core principles

  • Thought becomes reality – "we literally become what we think about" (SecretWithin). Controlling thoughts is presented as the first step to controlling one’s life.
  • Equal‑and‑opposite reaction – the Newtonian law is applied to human effort: every action demands an equal reaction, i.e., you must "pay the price" for any achievement.
  • Four practical pillars – become what you think, use imagination, exercise courage, and save at least 10 % of earnings (SecretWithin).
  • Service before profit – money follows success, and success follows service; the narrator repeatedly stresses that you must be of service first.
  • Persistence as faith – persisting with the test is described as an act of faith; failure is framed as a cue to restart the 30‑day cycle.

The 30‑day test

  1. Write a single, clearly defined goal on a card (e.g., more money, a home, a job promotion). Keep it private but visible.
  2. Read the goal each morning and night, visualising it positively and replacing any fearful thought with the goal image.
  3. Commit to a cheerful, positive outlook and give more than you have ever given before – in work, family, or personal effort.
  4. Save at least 10 % of what you earn during the test.
  5. Memorise and recite the three biblical promises – "Ask, and it shall be given you; Seek, and ye shall find; Knock, and it shall be opened unto you" – placing them on the back of the goal card.
  6. Read inspirational books (the Bible, Wake Up and Live, The Magic of Believing, Think and Grow Rich) to keep motivation high.
  7. If negative thoughts arise, consciously replace them with the goal image; if you feel overwhelmed, restart the 30‑day cycle.

Practical advice & recommended reading

  • Treat the test as a habit‑forming exercise; new habits are hard to form but last a lifetime.
  • Involve family members by sharing the recording periodically.
  • Remember the law of service: "No man can get rich himself unless he enriches others."
  • Follow the six steps outlined by Dr. David Harold Fink, MD: set a goal, stop self‑criticism, focus on reasons you can succeed, trace limiting attitudes to childhood, rewrite your self‑image, and act the part of the successful person.

Final reflections

The narrator concludes that the 30‑day test is a gateway to an "abundance" mindset, promising both financial wealth and inner peace to those who persist. Success is framed not as a result of chasing money, but as the natural outcome of disciplined thought, courageous action, and consistent service.
