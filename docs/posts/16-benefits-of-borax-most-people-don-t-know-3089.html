16 Benefits of Borax Most People Don’t Know

Discover 16 surprising ways a $7 box of Borax can slash household costs and solve everyday problems.

Overview

Borax, a cheap laundry booster on shelves since the 1800s, can tackle odors, stains, pests, and minor repairs around the home. The video walks through sixteen practical tricks that turn a single $7 box into a multi‑purpose cleaner, saver, and DIY remedy.

16. trash‑can shield

  • Clean the bin thoroughly.
  • Sprinkle about a half‑cup of dry Borax on the bottom before inserting a new bag.
  • The powder absorbs odors and discourages flies and ants for weeks.

15. fix porcelain‑sink scuff marks

  • Dampen a sponge and dust it with Borax.
  • Gently buff the gray metal deposits left by cookware.
  • The glaze remains untouched while the marks lift away.

14. Clean window screens

  • Mix Borax with water to form a mild solution.
  • Dip a soft brush into the solution and scrub the mesh.
  • Rinse; the grime lifts out of the tiny holes instead of being pushed around.

13. save car floor mats in winter

  • Dissolve Borax in water and wash the mats.
  • The solution neutralises road‑salt before it bonds to fibers, preventing white rings.
  • One extra wash step keeps mats looking newer longer.

12. moth‑proof storage bins

  • Lightly dust the bottom of each bin with Borax before storing winter woolens.
  • Museums use the same method to protect historic textiles without harsh chemicals.

11. remove rust stains from clothing

  • Make a paste of equal parts Borax and lemon juice.
  • Apply to the stain, let sit 30 minutes, then scrub and rinse.
  • Repeat if necessary; the citric acid breaks down iron while Borax lifts it out.

10. revive a shrunken wool sweater

  • Dissolve 1 Tbsp Borax in a gallon of cold water.
  • Submerge the sweater, gently stretch it back to shape, and soak for 30 minutes.
  • Lay flat to dry stretched; the fibers relax enough to reshape.

9. preserve flowers permanently

  • Fill a container with dry Borax and place flowers face‑up inside.
  • Pour more Borax over them until fully covered.
  • After 2–3 weeks, remove; the flowers are dry, retain colour, and can last indefinitely.

8. kill weeds in sidewalk and patio cracks

  • Pour a teaspoon of dry Borax directly into each crack where weeds grow.
  • The high boron level kills roots; use only where you want nothing to grow.
  • One treatment usually lasts the whole season.

7. protect outdoor wood against rot and insects

  • Dissolve 1 cup Borax in a gallon of hot water.
  • Brush the solution onto bare, unpainted wood (fence posts, deck boards, shed siding).
  • Let it soak and dry; reapply yearly for continued protection.

6. get rid of silverfish

  • Lightly dust a thin layer of Borax along the backs of shelves, closet corners, and under cabinets.
  • Silverfish walk through the dust and die; keep the layer thin so pets and children aren’t exposed.

5. get rid of cockroaches

  • Apply a very thin dusting of Borax along baseboards, under appliances, and other travel routes.
  • Cockroaches avoid thick layers; a light coating works over 2–3 weeks to eliminate the population.

4. kill flea eggs in carpet

  • Vacuum first to remove adult fleas.
  • Sprinkle Borax over the carpet, especially where pets rest, and work it deep with a broom.
  • The mineral dehydrates eggs and larvae; keep children and pets out until the carpet is thoroughly vacuumed afterward.

3. deep clean your toilet overnight

  • Pour 1 cup of Borax into the bowl before bed; do not flush.
  • While you sleep, the Borax breaks down mineral scale and hard‑water rings.
  • In the morning, brush and flush; the ring disappears without harsh chemicals.
