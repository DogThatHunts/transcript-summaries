The case for food restrictions

Dave Champion, a self-proclaimed libertarian and author of Body Science, advocates for federal government restrictions on food purchases made through taxpayer-funded assistance programs such as SNAP, WIC, and others. He clarifies that these restrictions would only apply when using taxpayer money, not to individuals' personal purchases. Champion asserts that people relying on food assistance often also receive taxpayer-funded medical care (Medicaid/Medicare), creating a direct link between food choices and public health expenditure.

The government's data blackout

Champion claims an "establishment blackout" prevents public access to data linking food assistance purchases with chronic diseases. He argues that the government possesses all necessary information—social security numbers for both food assistance and medical programs, and detailed purchase records—which could be anonymized and analyzed. He suggests this data is withheld to protect "trillion-dollar industries" that benefit from the current food system and the resulting health issues. He also criticizes the U.S. government's food pyramid, stating its purpose is agricultural promotion, not human health, and suggests reversing its recommendations.

Inadequate state-level restrictions

Champion highlights that states attempting to restrict food assistance purchases must obtain waivers from the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services. He points out that approved restrictions are minimal, typically limited to soft drinks, energy drinks, and candy. He illustrates the absurdity of these rules, noting that a recipient might be prohibited from buying a diet soda with zero carbs but allowed to purchase high-sugar juices or pasta, which contains significantly more carbohydrates.

The dangers of high carbohydrates

According to Champion, high carbohydrate consumption leads to elevated insulin levels, which in turn causes compromised mitochondrial function and metabolic dysfunction—the root cause of most chronic diseases in America. He emphasizes that these diseases develop gradually over years of a high-carbohydrate diet, making it a matter of "when," not "if," for those consistently consuming such foods. He shares his personal experience of consuming minimal carbohydrates (3-5 grams daily) and maintaining perfect health at 66, despite a family history of cancer, diabetes, and atherosclerosis.

Proposed permitted food list

Champion proposes a "short list" of items that should be permitted for purchase with food assistance funds, focusing on low-carb, whole foods: * Any items rendered from a cow or pig (including bacon and spam). * Chicken (including cooked versions). * Fish of any kind (including canned). * Any green vegetable (excluding artichokes and Brussels sprouts due to higher carb content). * Specific sugar-free beverages that provide electrolytes. He clarifies that individuals would still be free to purchase other foods like potatoes and bread with their own money. Champion dismisses critics of his dietary views, equating them to those who believe in the safety of mRNA injections and masks, suggesting they lack understanding of physiology.
