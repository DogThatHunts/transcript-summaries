Dave Champion on Media Lies and the danger of health disinformation

Dave Champion, author of Body Science, argues that media lies—especially those about nutrition—can lead to physical harm and that the public must be vigilant against disinformation.

Key insights

  • The Mayo Clinic does not claim that low-carb diets cause long-term health problems; it only notes short-term side effects like constipation and headaches.
  • Champion corrects the claim that ketosis causes nausea, fatigue, or foul breath—these are not symptoms of ketosis, which instead produces a surge in energy and well-being.
  • Ketosis is a natural, healthy physiological state that has existed in humans for millions of years, distinct from ketoacidosis, a dangerous condition linked to type 1 diabetes.
  • Livestrong’s article, written by registered nurse Krista Sheehan, misrepresents scientific facts and fails to distinguish between ketosis and ketoacidosis.
  • Champion asserts that millions of people have improved health on ketogenic diets, reversing chronic diseases like type 2 diabetes and heart disease.

The Livestrong article and its flaws

  • The article claims a low-carb diet causes headaches, dizziness, fatigue, nausea, diarrhea, and constipation.
  • It falsely states that consuming less than 20 grams of carbs per day leads to ketosis, which is life-threatening.
  • Champion points out that no one in ketosis experiences nausea, headaches, or fatigue—these are instead transient effects from the transition, not ongoing symptoms.
  • The claim that ketosis causes the body to 'destroy fat stores' is scientifically incorrect; fat stores are mobilized to produce energy, not destroyed.
  • The article conflates ketosis (a healthy metabolic state) with ketoacidosis (a life-threatening disease), a confusion that stems from a shared acronym but is fundamentally wrong.

Champion’s critique of the Media and the establishment

  • Champion argues that the U.S. government, big pharma, and big food have a financial interest in suppressing knowledge of ketosis’s health benefits.
  • He suggests that media outlets like Livestrong may be complicit in spreading disinformation to discourage public adoption of ketogenic diets.
  • He questions whether nurse Krista Sheehan, a registered nurse, has the scientific literacy to differentiate between a healthy metabolic state and a pathological one.
  • He implies that Livestrong’s editorial standards are not upheld, as no research or fact-checking appears to have been done on the article.

Real-World health outcomes of Ketosis

  • People in ketosis report improved energy, mental clarity, and reduced symptoms of chronic diseases.
  • Ketosis has been linked to the reversal of insulin resistance, hypertension, type 2 diabetes, heart disease, autoimmune disorders, and mental illnesses like depression.
  • Champion shares his personal experience: after cutting carbs to 30 grams per day, he felt a dramatic improvement in energy and well-being within days.

The broader disinformation campaign

  • Champion claims that for 60 years, a coordinated disinformation campaign by government and industry has led the U.S. population to adopt unhealthy diets and lifestyles.
  • This campaign, he argues, has made Americans the sickest population in human history.
