Chase Hughes outlines a four-step method to defang and escape a manipulative frame during an interaction. By depersonalizing the behavior and treating it as a common pattern, you can neutralize its influence without causing immediate defensiveness. The process culminates in offering the other person an exit ramp before introducing a new frame.

Key takeaways

  • Distance the frame from the person: Avoid accusing the individual directly. Instead of pointing fingers, frame the behavior as a common pattern that naturally occurs rather than something they are uniquely doing.
  • Name the structure: Identify and label the underlying structure of the interaction, focusing on the frame, the behavior, and the resulting outcome.
  • Externalize the consequences: Let the natural patterns of the behavior do the arguing for you, rather than engaging in a direct personal conflict.
  • Offer an exit ramp: Provide the person with a way out of the manipulative frame before attempting to establish a new frame or direction for the conversation.
