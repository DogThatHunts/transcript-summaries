What Happens if you Take Creatine Every Day for 30 Days (After 55)

  • Creatine acts as a muscle's backup battery, helping power short bursts of effort like standing up from a chair or catching yourself.
  • About 95% of body creatine is stored in muscles, and after 55, muscle loss reduces storage space, especially as meat intake often decreases.
  • In the first few weeks, creatine pulls water into muscle cells, causing a slight weight gain—about one to two pounds—that is not fat or puffiness.
  • A 2017 study found that creatine users who combined it with strength training gained more lean muscle and strength than those who trained without it.
  • A 2023 review from the University of Liverpool found early evidence of memory benefits in adults aged 66 to 76, but this is still considered a bonus, not a primary benefit.
  • Creatine breaks down into creatinine, which is measured in blood tests to assess kidney function. This can make kidney health appear worse on paper, so it’s important to discuss creatine use with a doctor before blood work.
  • A 2025 trial found no effect of creatine on hair loss, and a 2023 Canadian trial found no change in bone density, despite a small hint of improved hip shape.
  • The proven tools for bone health remain strength training, calcium, vitamin D, and, when recommended, medication.
  • To take creatine: use plain creatine monohydrate, 3 to 5 grams daily (about one level teaspoon), take it with food or drinks, pair it with strength training and protein intake, and ensure meals contain at least 25 to 30 grams of protein.
  • People over 60 or postmenopausal women may benefit most due to anabolic resistance—older muscle needs stronger stimulation to grow.
  • Always consult a doctor before starting creatine if you have kidney disease, diabetes, high blood pressure, or are on multiple medications.
