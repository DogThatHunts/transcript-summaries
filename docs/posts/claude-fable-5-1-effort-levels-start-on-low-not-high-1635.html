Claude Fable 5.1 effort levels: Start on low, not high

In this episode, Nate B. Jones explores the practical capabilities of Anthropic's Fable 5.1, focusing on how its different effort settings perform across complex knowledge work, writing, and 3D visual generation tasks. By comparing Fable 5.1 (on both "low" and "extra" settings) against Fable 5 and OpenAI's GPT 5.6 Sol, Jones demonstrates why starting with lower effort levels is often the most strategic and token-efficient approach.

The challenge of automating knowledge work

Jones argues that knowledge work—the messy process of iterating through documents, spreadsheets, and presentations to find clarity—is uniquely difficult for AI models. Unlike software engineering, where tools like Claude Code can rely on compiler errors and test suites to verify success, knowledge work has no automated "done" state. Because verification is subjective and manual, models must be evaluated on how easily a human analyst can audit and build upon their drafts.

Financial modeling: Low vs. extra effort

To test the models on a realistic business task, Jones prompted them to research the acquisition of GoPro by Starman, build a post-acquisition discounted cash flow (DCF) model in Excel, and generate an executive PowerPoint deck.

  • Fable 5.1 (Low effort): Produced a highly functional seven-sheet workbook and a 13-slide deck. The formulas worked and successfully calculated valuation changes across different scenarios. However, it lacked dedicated sources or formula-checking sheets, making the underlying reasoning harder to audit.
  • Fable 5.1 (Extra effort): Delivered a more sophisticated nine-sheet workbook and a 15-slide deck. It treated the two companies as separate entities, factored in deal-closing probabilities, utilized a weighted average cost of capital (WACC), performed an exit multiple check, and linked 26 distinct sources.
  • GPT 5.6 Sol (Extra high effort): Created a compact ten-sheet workbook and a ten-slide deck. While its PowerPoint design was less visually polished than Anthropic's, Sol excelled at auditability, providing dedicated sources and check sheets with explicit pass/fail results for formulas.

Jones suggests a multi-pass workflow: use Fable 5.1 on "low" to generate a rapid first draft, use Sol to verify and clean up the spreadsheet structure, and then use Fable 5.1 on "extra" to polish the final presentation.

Writing and narrative structure comparison

Jones evaluated the models' editorial precision by asking them to explain Toyota's entry and success in the U.S. automotive market in exactly 100 words:

  • Fable 5: Struggled with word constraints, relying on florid, metaphorical language (e.g., "thirsty Detroit models") rather than direct facts.
  • Fable 5.1: Demonstrated superior editorial judgment. It established a clear causal chain, referencing historical milestones like the failed Toyota Crown, the Corolla, the oil crisis, and U.S. manufacturing shifts without unnecessary stylistic fluff.
  • GPT 5.6 Sol: Opted for a smoother, high-level narrative with fewer specific dates and milestones, making it highly suitable for general audiences.

Generating 3D architectural films with blender

To see if these patterns carried over to visual tasks, Jones provided a Seattle property address and instructed the models to write code to generate a cinematic architectural walkthrough in Blender.

Fable 5.1 successfully built a 3D model of the house, designed the terrain and lighting, planned complex camera paths, and rendered a cohesive 37-second video entirely from code. While the assets were somewhat stylized, it far surpassed Fable 5 (which produced rougher edges and clipping errors) and Sol (which had lower-quality interior designs).

Token efficiency and pricing dynamics

While Fable 5.1 maintains the same base API pricing as Fable 5 ($10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens), its prompt caching costs have dropped significantly to $0.25 per million tokens. Anthropic estimates that typical workloads cost 25% less, and highly agentic, cache-heavy workflows cost up to 45% less. Jones notes that because Anthropic's subscription limits remain tight, running Fable 5.1 on "low" is an excellent way to stretch usage limits without sacrificing draft quality.
