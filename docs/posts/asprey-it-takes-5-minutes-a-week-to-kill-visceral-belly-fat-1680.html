Visceral fat, heart fat, and the invisible health crisis

The four fat compartments that matter

O'Mara identifies four critical biomarkers visible on MRI:

  1. Visceral fat (inflammatory fat around organs) — you want this as low as possible
  2. Heart fat (pericardial fat) — associated with cardiovascular disease
  3. Muscle fat (intramuscular fat) — a sign of metabolic dysfunction and diabetes risk
  4. Superficial subcutaneous fat (the layer just under skin) — this is the good fat you actually want

The distinction between superficial and deep subcutaneous fat is crucial. A fascia membrane called Scarpa's fascia separates them. Deep subcutaneous fat behaves like visceral fat—it is inflammatory. Superficial subcutaneous fat, by contrast, is protective and associated with better appearance, longevity, and reduced mortality from heart disease, stroke, obesity, diabetes, cancer, and neurodegenerative conditions.
