You Don't Want a High-Performance Team (Build this Instead)

A high-performance team isn’t one that finishes tasks on time without being chased—it’s one that drives business growth. When a great employee performs well one quarter and then falters after a promotion, it signals a deeper problem: the wrong person was promoted.

  • Step one: Before promoting anyone, ask three team members—separately—"When something breaks, who do you go to first?" If the same person keeps coming up, that person is likely the right fit. If it’s your top seller, that’s a sign the team already trusts them.
  • Step two: After promotion, avoid two common mistakes: sending the new manager to a training session with no follow-through, and avoiding management by fearing micromanagement. Management is about agreeing on key metrics, checking them weekly, and coaching—not lecturing.
  • Step three: Replace reports with a weekly, out-loud scorecard. The scorecard has three parts: a number owned by one person against a pre-agreed goal, checked weekly (not monthly), and said out loud by the person whose number it is—leaders go first. This builds accountability and shows the promoted person what leadership looks like.
