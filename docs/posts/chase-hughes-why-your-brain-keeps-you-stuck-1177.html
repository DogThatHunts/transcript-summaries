Chase Hughes explains how our brain's primary function is prediction, not healing, especially after unresolved emotional events. This leads to the development of avoidance mechanisms that shape our perceived "comfort zone," which he argues is actually a perimeter of things our nervous system couldn't fully process.

  • The brain's primary job is prediction: Chase Hughes states that the brain's main function is to predict what will happen next, not necessarily to heal past emotional events.
  • Unresolved events trigger prevention: If an emotional event is unresolved, the brain scans for similar conditions to prevent recurrence, building an "architecture" of avoidance.
  • "Comfort zone" as an avoidance perimeter: What we perceive as a comfort zone is, in fact, a perimeter built by the nervous system around unprocessed events to avoid perceived threats.
  • Influential research: Hughes references Peter Levin's "somatic experiencing" and Bessel van der Kolk's confirming research on these concepts.
