The national picture: demand has evaporated

According to an ABC News report cited by RE-V, the US housing market is in deep trouble. The immigration factor: H-1B visa restrictions reshape demand

RE-V attributes part of Frisco's collapse to a dramatic shift in immigration policy. The area was historically a "hotbed for high-end educated skilled immigrants" on H-1B temporary work visas who purchased homes using government-backed FHA mortgages with as little as 5% down. The Trump administration halted this practice, eliminating a major source of buyer demand. RE-V notes the US is now at "almost a 50 year low on immigration," and argues this policy change—while potentially sound—has accelerated the housing downturn in visa-dependent tech and professional hubs.

Texas's building paradox: supply meets collapsed demand

Unlike California and New York, which restrict development, Texas permits construction freely. RE-V observes that Frisco and surrounding areas continue aggressive building despite plummeting values: "They just build these towns pretty much out of nowhere." This creates a paradox—more supply hitting the market at precisely the moment when California migration has slowed, immigrant buyers have vanished, and overall demand has cratered.

RE-V frames this as ultimately positive: Texas's willingness to build is driving affordability. Austin home prices are down 27% and rents down 21% from peak, making it "one of the more affordable areas in America to rent an apartment." This contrasts sharply with California, which "only permit[s] half the homes that Texas does, despite the fact that California's population is bigger."

Buyer boycott: why price cuts aren't sparking demand

A critical observation: prices are falling in Frisco and across Texas, yet buyers are not returning. Sales remain depressed. RE-V notes "Texas's migration is still down 70% from its pandemic peak" and argues there is a "buyer boycott" underway. The logic is straightforward—as long as demand stays suppressed, sellers must continue cutting prices to move inventory. RE-V expects this cycle to persist.

Valuation still stretched despite declines

RE-V estimates homes in Collin County and parts of Frisco remain overvalued by 8–10%, suggesting another 10% correction is plausible. If that occurs, total declines from peak could reach 20–30%—a scenario RE-V notes would have been "unfathomable four years ago."
