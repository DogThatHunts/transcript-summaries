Biohacking in Dubai: extreme heat and cultural barriers

Dubai presents unique challenges for longevity practitioners. The region experiences extreme heat (unbearable for five months per year), light pollution, noise pollution, and a culture built around late nights and evening social scenes. Sleep disruption is endemic, and outdoor activity during peak heat is impractical.

Abedrached's approach is to focus on what clients can control:

  • Optimize the bedroom. Since most people spend 7–8 hours in their bedroom, making it a controlled environment—natural sheets, optimal temperature, complete darkness, clean air, independent AC units free of mold—gives you roughly one-third of your day working in your favor.

  • Reframe the sun as beneficial, not threatening. Many in the region fear sun exposure due to cultural preferences for lighter skin. Abedrached encourages early morning or late afternoon sun exposure (when UV is lower) to capture circadian and vitamin D benefits. Sweating is not something to avoid; it's a natural response.

  • Use mineral sunscreen strategically. You can protect your face and neck from tanning while still getting torso sun exposure for vitamin D synthesis—especially important for darker skin tones, which require more sun exposure to produce adequate vitamin D.

Asprey adds practical advice: a 5–10 minute sunrise exposure (even at 7–8 a.m. in Dubai heat) combined with a pool dip provides significant circadian benefits without requiring hours outdoors.

Ramadan fasting: performance and longevity

Asprey notes that Ramadan represents a global experiment in intermittent fasting, with tens of millions practicing daily fasting for 30 days—sometimes including dry fasting (no water). He asks how to maintain performance and longevity during this period.

Abedrached emphasizes that Ramadan is primarily about family, unity, and social connection, not just fasting. The fasting component is significant but secondary. His advice:

  • Approach Ramadan intentionally. Don't stumble into it; anticipate late nights and prepare your circadian rhythm accordingly.

  • Look at the whole year, not just Ramadan. Abedrached manages stressors across the year—adding exercise and other stressors during lighter business periods, reducing them during peak work months. Ramadan is one variable in an annual stress-management strategy.

  • Adjust exercise intensity. Never maintain pre-Ramadan workout intensity during fasting. Adjust to challenge the body without creating long-term damage.

  • Prioritize hydration and electrolytes. With heat, fasting, and late nights, dehydration is a major risk. Use healthy electrolytes (not sugary sports drinks).

  • Resist the urge to break fast with sweets. Cultural tradition often involves ending the fast with something sweet, but this creates blood sugar spikes. Fighting this urge helps maintain metabolic health.

  • Maintain muscle mass through strategic movement. With intentional approach to these pillars, people can preserve muscle and general health throughout Ramadan.

Asprey agrees that adjusting intensity during periods of high business stress or travel is essential—the body cannot perform at vacation-level intensity during demanding work periods.
