The four-part formula of a curiosity machine

Rob Willis analyzes the communication style of mathematician and science communicator Hannah Fry. By transcribing 30 of her top-performing videos, Willis identifies a consistent four-part formula she uses to make complex topics impossible to ignore. The breakdown reveals how Fry leverages physical experiences, detailed mechanisms, satisfying revelations, and low-key endings to captivate audiences.

  • Start with a shared physical experience: Instead of introducing abstract concepts, Fry begins with relatable, physical sensations—like the slippery feeling of bleach on skin or the buzz of a charging metal laptop—to create an immediate curiosity gap.
  • Explain the mechanism step-by-step: Rather than jumping straight to the answer, she walks the audience through a clear chain of cause and effect, making them feel like they are discovering the solution alongside her.
  • Deliver a satisfying "click": She resolves the curiosity gap by revealing a surprising truth that reframes the initial experience (e.g., explaining that the slippery feeling is actually your skin turning into soap).
  • End small and practical: Fry avoids grand, inspirational conclusions, opting instead to close with a lighthearted joke, a practical tip, or a simple observation.
  • The core lesson of great educational content: According to Willis, effective communication is less about simplifying complex ideas and more about making people curious enough to seek out the explanation.
