The value and limits of anecdotal evidence

He asserts that while individual anecdotes do not equal scientific proof, a large collection of similar reports should serve as a catalyst for formal testing. Viewer-reported experiences with fenbendazole protocols

Campbell reads several accounts from viewers claiming remarkable recoveries from advanced, often terminal cancers after using fenbendazole, ivermectin, or the "Joe Tippens protocol":

  • Combination protocols: Commenters frequently mentioned combining fenbendazole with ivermectin, doxycycline, metformin, vitamin D3, CBD, and methylene blue.

Acknowledging negative outcomes and safety warnings

Campbell explicitly states that fenbendazole is not approved for human use in the UK or the US. He notes that these self-selected reports do not represent a controlled sample, and acknowledges that some commenters reported pets or relatives who took these substances and still passed away. He stresses the need to determine scientifically when these treatments are actually appropriate, for which cancers, and at what stages.

One commenter noted the difficulty of integrating these alternative approaches into mainstream care, sharing that they felt forced to hide their self-treatment from their oncologist out of fear that their standard chemotherapy would be discontinued.

The urgent call for clinical trials and research

Campbell criticizes health authorities in the UK, US, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand for failing to quantitatively collect and analyze this data. He notes that Florida appears to be one of the few jurisdictions beginning to test these treatments. When reflecting on why mainstream research on repurposed, low-cost drugs is not being conducted, Campbell points to viewer feedback suggesting financial disincentives within the pharmaceutical industry.
