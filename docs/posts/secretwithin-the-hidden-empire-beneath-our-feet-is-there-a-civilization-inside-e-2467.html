These disparate traditions, the narrator suggests, all point to a common archetype of hidden wisdom just beyond reach.

In the 19th and 20th centuries, Agartha legends merged with the hollow earth theory—the belief that Earth contains enormous caverns and inner realms. This sparked speculation about tunnel systems connecting locations from the Himalayas to the Amazon, with rumored entrances at Mount Kailash, Mount Shasta in California, the Great Pyramid of Giza, and Antarctica (linked to alleged Nazi expeditions in the 1930s).

Some view Agartha's inhabitants as guardians of humanity; others see the realm as a symbol of hope—an image of what humanity could achieve by transcending violence and greed.
