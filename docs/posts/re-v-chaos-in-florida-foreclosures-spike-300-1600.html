As foreclosure rates climb and home values plummet in pandemic boomtowns, Florida's housing market faces a stark correction reminiscent of the 2008 crash.

The emerging foreclosure crisis in Florida

According to data from Atom Data Solutions cited by RE-V, Florida has become the number one state for foreclosures in the US housing market, with one out of every 2,300 housing units currently in foreclosure. This rate is 10 to 20 times higher than states with the lowest foreclosure filings. RE-V attributes this spike to a statewide and regional home price downturn that has been underway for the last two years, with typical values in the hardest-hit pockets already down more than 20% from their peak.

The mechanics of the Davenport bust

Focusing on Davenport in Polk County—a major pandemic-era migration destination located between Orlando and Lakeland—RE-V highlights how a surge in home building coincided with a sudden drop-off in migration. Many buyers purchased homes at the peak of the market using low-down-payment FHA and VA loans, putting only 0% to 5% down. As home values decline, these highly leveraged owners face a high risk of delinquency, leading to a wave of short sales and foreclosures.

RE-V points to specific local listings as evidence of the correction: * A four-bedroom home purchased in 2022 for $385,000 is now listed as a lender-approved short sale for $295,000, representing a $90,000 loss. * Another home listed for $344,000 has sat on the market for six months, representing only a 5% appreciation over five years and wiping out the vast majority of its pandemic-era gains. * A townhouse that sold for $282,000 in 2021 as a new build is currently listed for $199,000.

DeSantis's proposed property tax relief

To combat the housing downturn, Florida Governor Ron DeSantis is backing a controversial November ballot measure aimed at reducing property taxes. The proposal includes increasing the homestead exemption to $200,000 for primary homeowners who have resided in the state for at least five years, explicitly excluding real estate investors. While some market participants hope this measure will boost demand and rejuvenate the market by late 2026, critics express concern over how local services like schools, police, and fire departments will fund their budgets if the tax base is reduced.

Historical comparisons: 2008 versus today

RE-V addresses counterarguments from market bulls who claim the current market is stable because the overall foreclosure rate remains historically low—around 0.5% compared to the double-digit rates seen in Miami, Tampa, and Orlando in 2009–2010. RE-V warns that the market is already seeing massive price drops of 20% to 30% on distressed listings despite these low foreclosure rates. If foreclosure rates continue to rise incrementally, or if the US enters a broader economic recession with rising unemployment, price declines could accelerate significantly.

Regional disparities and investor pullbacks

The housing downturn is highly regional. West Coast Florida counties have experienced the sharpest declines, with Charlotte County (Punta Gorda) down 22% since 2022, Lee County (Cape Coral/Fort Myers) down 17%, and Sarasota County down 16%. In contrast, Miami-Dade County values remain up 12.6% since mid-2022, though they have recently begun declining year-over-year.

Additionally, institutional investors have pulled back significantly, with purchases down 50% to 70% over the last four years. This retreat is driven by rising property taxes, soaring insurance costs, and falling rental rates. RE-V highlights a Davenport rental property owned by Wall Street investor Main Street Renewal, where the rent was cut from $2,700 to $2,460, illustrating how falling rents are dragging down underlying property values.
