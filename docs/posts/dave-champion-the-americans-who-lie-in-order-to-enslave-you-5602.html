How a 111-year-old tax law loophole reveals the government's deception about who really pays income tax in the U.S.

The myth of the Income Tax on ordinary Americans

Dave Champion argues that the U.S. income tax has never been imposed on ordinary, hardworking Americans living in the 50 states. He claims that the government's narrative—that all Americans pay income tax on their earnings—is a lie perpetuated through disinformation and legal ambiguity.

Key claims and analysis

  • Section 1 of the Internal Revenue Code does not impose a tax on all Americans. The term "taxable income" is defined in Section 63 as gross income minus deductions, and gross income is limited to income from specific sources.

  • Gross income is defined in Section 61 as income from all sources, including compensation, business gains, interest, and dividends. However, Chapter 3 of the code limits gross income to U.S. source income for non-resident aliens and foreign corporations, and foreign earned income for U.S. citizens abroad.

  • Section 1.1-1 of the regulations states that the income tax applies to "every individual who is a citizen or resident of the United States". However, Champion argues that this language is deliberately vague and does not specify location, activity, or citizenship status.

  • The U.S. Supreme Court in Gould v. Gould ruled that taxes cannot be imposed by implication. A statute or regulation must contain exacting language to establish a tax obligation. Champion asserts that Section 1 and 1.1-1 lack such language.

  • Congress and the Treasury have not clarified the tax's scope over 111 years. A 45-word version of a clear statute—imposing tax on domestic earnings of U.S. citizens and residents in the 50 states—is presented as a hypothetical solution that has never been enacted.

  • The tax is only imposed on three specific classes of persons:

  • Non-resident aliens with U.S. source income
  • Foreign corporations with U.S. source income

  • U.S. citizens residing abroad with foreign earned income

  • A fourth group of Americans becomes liable when they act as agents for non-resident aliens or foreign corporations. For example, an investment firm receiving funds from a Peruvian billionaire and holding Apple stock may be required to withhold U.S. income tax before sending dividends to Peru. In such cases, the firm becomes liable to file and pay the tax.

  • The Bruce Schaber v. Union Pacific Railroad case (1916) established that U.S. agents of foreign owners are liable for withholding U.S. income tax. This precedent is cited as evidence of how the law works in practice, not as a general tax on American citizens.

The government's deception

  • Champion argues that Congress and the Treasury deliberately use vague language to allow Americans to assume the tax applies to them.
  • After 60 years of government propaganda, he claims the public is "brainwashed" into believing the income tax applies to all workers.
  • The government, he says, "plays you"—you are the master, and the government is the servant.

Conclusion

Dave Champion concludes that the income tax has not been imposed on ordinary Americans living and working in the 50 states. Instead, it applies only to specific groups, including foreign-owned entities and citizens abroad. He calls Income Tax Shattering the Myths the definitive resource for understanding this truth.
