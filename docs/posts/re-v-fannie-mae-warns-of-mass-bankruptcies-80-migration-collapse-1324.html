Nick from ReVenture explores how falling apartment rents, soaring defaults, and shifting migration patterns are driving a housing market correction across the Sunbelt while reviving Midwestern cities.

The apartment downturn and rising default rates

The US apartment market is experiencing a severe downturn, triggering localized housing price drops across major urban centers. Data from Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac indicates that 60-day delinquency rates on multifamily mortgages have hit their highest levels since the 2008–2010 financial crisis.

To mask declining demand without officially cutting list prices, landlords are relying on record concessions. High-rise developments frequently offer up to three or four months of free rent on 12-month leases—representing effective rent discounts of 25% to 31%. These rental reductions are spilling over into the residential home-buying market by eliminating incentives for first-time buyers and forcing broader housing price deflation.

Regional rent declines and shifting migration patterns

Asking rents have fallen steeply across Sunbelt and Mountain West markets. Austin, Texas leads the decline with a 21% drop in apartment rents from mid-2022 levels, bringing rental prices back to pre-pandemic baselines. Double-digit rent drops are also affecting Fort Myers, Phoenix, Raleigh, Denver, Orlando, and Atlanta.

While real estate developers overbuilt in these regions, a sharp collapse in migration demand is the primary catalyst. Inbound migration into the South has dropped by approximately two-thirds relative to 2022 levels. Conversely, cities in the Midwest and Northeast are experiencing the country's strongest rental growth, led by Chicago, Hartford, Madison, and Buffalo. In 2025, the Midwest recorded its first net positive inbound migration year in nearly 40 years.

Structural economic shifts and landlord distress

Two major factors account for the shift in population dynamics away from the Sunbelt and toward the Rust Belt:

  • Loss of affordability: Pandemic-era price surges eliminated the cost-of-living advantage Sunbelt cities previously maintained.
  • Industrial re-shoring: Re-shoring initiatives, data center construction, and major manufacturing projects—such as Micron's chip fabrication plant near Syracuse, New York—are driving economic growth in northern states.

Concurrently, apartment building owners are entering financial distress. Developers who took out low-rate debt (2%–3%) during the pandemic are now required to refinance into 6% mortgage rates. Coupled with reduced rental revenues, property cash flows are increasingly failing to cover debt service obligations, leading to mortgage defaults.

Market divergence and advice for renters

Rental metrics show extreme regional divergence across the country. In Austin, apartment vacancy has climbed above 9%, driving home values down. Conversely, San Francisco's apartment vacancy rate has reached a 10-year low, paired with a 7% increase in home values over the past year.

For existing tenants, real estate investment trust (REIT) data shows negative rent growth on new leases alongside persistent efforts by landlords to increase rates on renewals. Renters faced with rent increases are advised to utilize local market concession data and competing listings to push back against rate hikes.
