Ketosis versus glucosis: redefining the metabolic paradigm

Champion explains his coining of the term \"glucosis\" to describe the state where cells burn glucose for energy. He argues that glucosis is an \"emergency mechanism\" rather than the normal state of human fuel. He claims that living in ketosis virtually eradicates problematic, unhealthy inflammation, giving the body the best chance to avoid chronic diseases.

The case for an ancestral carnivore diet

Champion advocates for a diet consisting of 98% animal meat and animal byproducts, arguing that human genetics are optimized for this ancestral diet. He contests the validity of blood-type diets and veganism, claiming that dietary preferences based on blood type are irrelevant once a body enters ketosis. He notes that transitioning to a carnivore diet requires a brief adjustment period (typically 5 to 10 days) for the liver and gut to adapt enzyme and bile production.

The lymphatic system and the necessity of movement

Champion explains that the lymphatic system's primary functions are supporting the immune system and clearing cellular waste. Because the lymphatic system has no pump, it relies entirely on the contraction and release of skeletal muscles. He recommends at least 40 minutes of head-to-toe skeletal muscle movement six days a week, noting that the contraction itself, rather than the heavy weight lifted, is what moves the lymph fluid.

Contested health claims: sodium, supplementation, and vaccines

Champion challenges mainstream medical advice regarding salt, stating he consumes 13 grams of sodium chloride daily to resolve nerve-signaling issues like restless leg syndrome and muscle cramps. He claims that on a carnivore diet, supplementation is largely unnecessary, except for salt and vitamin D (if working indoors), noting he has not consumed vitamin C in years and does not have scurvy. Champion shares his personal theories regarding mRNA vaccines, arguing that spike proteins are distributed systemically in healthy organs rather than remaining at the injection site, and suggests ketosis as a way to mitigate potential adverse inflammatory events.
