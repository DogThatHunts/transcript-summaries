NYC’s Mega Transformation by 2032 (4 Neighborhoods)

Four neighborhoods in NYC are undergoing real, public transformations—some with construction already underway, others just beginning to plan.

East Harlem

The second avenue subway expansion is the largest project in the city’s transit history. The first phase opened in 2017, running the Q train up to 96th Street. The second phase extends through East Harlem to 125th Street, adding three new stops: 106th, 116th, and 125th Streets.

Key developments: - The tunnel contract was signed last year for nearly two billion dollars. - The 106th Street station contract was signed in June. - The MTA aims to have trains running by around 2032 (dates are approximate). - The project reuses existing tunnel sections dug in the 1970s under 2nd Avenue. - The 125th Street station will connect to the 4, 5, and 6 trains and Metro North via a Park Avenue entrance. - The project is expected to add about 110,000 daily riders.

East Harlem has long been served by a single subway line on Lexington Avenue. This expansion marks its first direct access to the 2nd Avenue line.

Midtown south

This area underwent the biggest rezoning in 20 years. Spanning 42 blocks from 23rd to 40th Streets between 5th and 8th Avenues, it was previously zoned exclusively for offices and factories.

Key developments: - In August of the previous year, the city council voted 43 to 0 to rezone the area for residential use. - The rezoning allows for approximately 9,500 new homes. - About 3,000 of those homes are required to be permanently affordable. - Every new residential building must include affordable units. - The deal includes nearly $500 million in community investments. - Investments cover parks, subway station upgrades, and a new busway on 34th Street. - The plan allows older office buildings to convert into apartments, creating a second pipeline of housing.

This is the slowest of the four developments, as construction will take years. However, it fundamentally changes the map of the area—transforming a purely commercial zone into a residential one.

Gowanus

Gowanus was rezoned in 2021, covering 82 blocks around the canal. The plan includes roughly 8,500 new apartments.

Key developments: - Construction has already begun. - One project, Gowanus Worth, includes over 2,000 apartments with more planned. - Buyers in Gowanus are effectively purchasing next to active construction. - The Gowanus Canal is a federal Superfund site, with the EPA cleaning up over a century of industrial pollution since 2010. - The cleanup is ongoing and happens alongside construction. - Developers are required to build a public waterfront esplanade, with each project contributing a stretch in front of their development. - About 3,000 of the 8,500 apartments are set aside for affordability. - The first buildings are already open and leasing.

This neighborhood is one of the most visible examples of a public plan turning into real housing.

Brooklyn Queens line (Interboro Express)

This project is proposed, not promised. Known as the Interboro Express, it is a light rail line running roughly 14 miles from the Brooklyn Army Terminal through Brooklyn and Queens to Roosevelt Avenue.

Key developments: - It would connect to 17 subway lines. - The route runs along existing freight corridors that have been in place for a century—making it faster and cheaper to build than a new subway. - The estimated cost is about five and a half billion dollars. - The MTA’s current capital plan has set aside about half of that amount. - Approximately 900,000 people live along the proposed route. - The design is underway, with an environmental study due by the end of the year. - There is no construction date yet.
