The bloodline of Cain: serpent-like angels, Freemasonry, and snake brotherhoods

Overview

SecretWithin presents a detailed exploration of Gary Wayne's The Genesis 6 Conspiracy, examining the first five chapters of a book that claims to uncover the hidden history of humanity spanning over 6,000 years. The analysis draws on biblical texts, apocryphal sources like the Book of Enoch and Kebra Nagast, and historical records to argue that fallen angels—the "sons of God"—mated with human women to produce the Nephilim, a race of giants whose influence persists in modern secret societies like Freemasonry.

The daughters of Cain and the Nephilim

Wayne argues that understanding the Nephilim requires first examining the daughters of Cain. Drawing on the Jewish historian Josephus, who recorded that Adam and Eve had 33 sons and 23 daughters (though the Bible mentions only "other sons and daughters"), Wayne explores the question of Cain's wife. Since no other humans existed, some theologians argue she must have been one of Adam's daughters, though the Bible does not explicitly confirm this.

According to Wayne's interpretation, Cain's descendants became sources of violence and corruption in the pre-flood world. While Genesis records Cain murdering Abel, the New Testament hints that his descendants inherited a legacy of wickedness, with some interpretations suggesting Cain belonged to Satan because of his crime. Wayne connects this lineage to the Nephilim through the Kebra Nagast, an Ethiopian religious text that uses the phrase "daughters of Cain" instead of the Bible's "daughters of men," implying a more negative view of Cain's line. The text describes these daughters as prone to indulgence and sinful behavior.

Researcher Andrew Collins, cited by SecretWithin, supports this view, suggesting that the daughters of Cain could have been easily manipulated due to their already evil ways, making them susceptible to unholy unions with the sons of God.

The sons of God as fallen angels

Wayne addresses a central theological debate: were the "sons of God" in Genesis 6 angels or the descendants of Seth? He directs readers to the Book of Job, where "sons of God" is used interchangeably with "angels" to describe heavenly beings in God's presence. In Job 1:6 and 2:1, the term appears in passages describing angels presenting themselves before the Lord, and many Bible translations provide footnotes confirming that "sons of God" and "angels" are synonymous.

Wayne argues that these sons of God were fallen angels who descended to earth and took human women as wives. The Book of Enoch and other ancient texts describe how these angels violated divine law by marrying human women and fathering children "not of the spirit, but of the flesh." The second Book of Enoch similarly describes the Gregori, giant fallen angels who mated with human women.

The sexual capability of angels

A common objection to Wayne's thesis is whether angels, as spiritual beings, can engage in sexual relations with humans. Wayne addresses this by pointing to Genesis 6 itself, which clearly describes angels taking human wives and fathering offspring. He also cites 1 Corinthians 11:10, where Paul instructs women to wear a sign of authority on their heads "because of the angels," suggesting that angels posed a sexual threat. Early church father Tertullian (160–230 CE) seemingly concurred, noting that Paul instructed women to wear veils "so as to not entice angels, for dark angels love unveiled women with their beautiful hair."

While the Bible does not explicitly confirm that angels have sexual capabilities, Wayne argues it also does not deny the possibility. He notes that the lack of marriage in heaven, as mentioned in Matthew, does not preclude angels from engaging in sexual acts on earth. The Zohar, a key text in Kabbalistic tradition, describes angels as changelings capable of transforming into male or female forms in the physical realm.

The Book of Jude provides what Wayne considers additional evidence, linking the sexual immorality of fallen angels in Noah's time to the perversion of Sodom and Gomorrah. Jude 1:6–7 states: "the angels who did not keep their positions of authority but abandoned their own home. These he kept in darkness, bound with everlasting chains for judgment on the great day. In a similar way, Sodom, Gomorrah, and the surrounding towns gave themselves up to sexual immorality and perversion."

The Kebra Nagast further describes the brutal consequences: "daughters of Cain, with whom the angels sinned, conceived, but they were unable to bring forth children, and they died. Of the children who were in their wombs, some died, and some did come forth by splitting open the bellies of their mothers." Wayne elaborates that all Nephilim mothers perished because their human physiology could not withstand the unnatural births, with only a few infants surviving through what he describes as a barbaric form of Caesarean delivery.

Giants across ancient cultures

Wayne notes that giants were not exclusive to biblical accounts but were renowned across cultures including the Egyptians, Romans, Greeks, Phoenicians, and Mesopotamian societies. Primeval myths are filled with tales of extraordinary heroes of great size and unmatched strength, consistently tied to unions between gods and humans. Wayne points to famous mythological figures like Hercules, Theseus, Jason, Cadmus, and Perseus—all described as offspring of immortal gods and mortal women, and many known as dragon slayers.

Wayne draws a particularly intriguing connection to the myth of Atlantis, which was allegedly ruled by ten kings who were children of Poseidon and human women. He theorizes that secret societies like Freemasonry are working to revive the Atlantean model of governance, a system where fallen angels openly interacted with humanity. This, he argues, is a central theme of the Genesis 6 conspiracy and reflects the enduring influence of these ancient tales in modern secret societies.

The serpent-like appearance of the Nephilim

Wayne pieces together descriptions of the Nephilim's appearance from biblical accounts, historical records, and archaeological evidence. He explores the origins of the Anak, a race of giants whose name translates to "long-necked." Descriptions suggest their necks were so long they seemed to touch the sun, earning them comparisons to serpents. The Bible mentions the Zamzumites, a race of giants east of the Jordan River, whose name translates to "noisemakers," likely accounting for Josephus's description of Nephilim as fearsome to the hearing.

Josephus described the Anak as a famous race of giants of tremendous size whose countenance was so completely different from humans that they were "extraordinarily surprising, frightening and terrible to both the senses of sight and hearing." Wayne explains that the Nephilim mirrored their fathers in both nature and appearance. The Watchers, who descended from the heavens, are identified as Seraphim angels—beings with serpent-like features that serve as a bridge between heaven and earth.

Wayne further explores the idea that Satan himself assumed the form of a seraph when he deceived Eve in the Garden of Eden. The Gnostic gospel The Origin of the World describes serpent-like angels with long and narrow faces, prominent cheekbones, elongated jaws, thin lips, and slanted eyes—features that, Wayne suggests, bear a resemblance to modern depictions of aliens.

Archaeological evidence cited by Wayne includes long-headed skulls dating back to the Antediluvian era, discovered in regions like Sumer and Egypt, which he argues support the idea of Nephilim having elongated serpent-like heads. He highlights the prominence of the cobra in Egyptian culture, depicted on royal crowns as early as 4000 BCE, suggesting this iconic symbol might reference these ancient beings and their influence on early civilizations.

Post-Diluvian Nephilim are also captured in Canaanite art, which portrays beings with long serpentine necks, heads like cobras, small mouths, and coffee bean eyes. Similar images of serpentine beings have been found in ancient monuments from both Egypt and Central America, ostensibly dating back to the Antediluvian epoch, appearing as images of long-necked and sloped-headed Anakites.

The Nephilim and snake brotherhoods

According to Wayne, the Nephilim were colossal giants with incredible size, strength, and agility—fearsome hunters known for insatiable appetites, both literal and metaphorical. They were not just physically intimidating but spiritually corrupt, embodying traits that set them apart from humanity in unsettling ways.

Wayne argues that the Nephilim did not exist in isolation but actively influenced and partnered with misguided humans, forming alliances with profound consequences. They established secretive organizations referred to as "Snake Brotherhoods," deeply rooted in corruption and manipulation, which eventually overthrew the governments of the antediluvian world. Through these alliances, the Nephilim exerted control, spreading their influence far and wide and shaping the course of humanity in destructive ways.

The Book of Enoch is cited to explain the spiritual consequences: "And now giants, offspring of spirit and flesh, will be called spirits on earth, and the earth shall be their dwelling. Their bodies emitted evil spirits because they were born from human women and holy watchers." Wayne explains that these bodiless spirits—the remnants of the Nephilim—are the same malicious entities that the New Testament refers to as demons, agents of destruction sowing confusion and havoc in the world.

The seven liberal sciences and the schism of Freemasonry

Wayne informs viewers that God taught Adam seven liberal sciences in the Garden of Eden, profoundly valuable knowledge containing the secrets of the universe. This knowledge was entrusted to humanity so they could understand and govern the world, provided they used it wisely and for good.

The seven liberal sciences, as outlined in the Dowland Manuscript of the Legend of the Craft, are:

  • Grammaire: the science of proper speech and writing, emphasizing clarity and precision in communication
  • Rhetorica: the art of refined and persuasive speech, empowering individuals to craft compelling arguments and influence others
  • Dialectic: the ability to discern truth from falsehood
  • Arithmetic: numbers and calculations, forming the backbone of logical and scientific reasoning
  • Geometry: also known as the science of masonry, teaching the principles of measuring the earth and constructing physical structures
  • Music: the profound power of music, its ability to transcend language, evoke emotions, and shape cultures and spirituality
  • Astronomy: the intricate movements of stars and planets, inviting humanity to understand the cosmos and its mysteries

After being banished from Eden, Adam retained this divinely inspired knowledge and passed it on to his descendants, including his sons Cain and Seth. However, this led to a divergence in how the sciences were used, referred to as "the schism of Freemasonry."

Seth's descendants upheld the sacred sciences in their original pure form for seven generations, using this divine knowledge righteously and remaining aligned with God's will. Cain's descendants, by contrast, corrupted the sacred sciences by blending them with idolatry and mysticism, using the knowledge for selfish and wicked purposes.

Wayne references George Oliver, a writer on Freemasonry, who identifies Seth's lineage as practicing the "pure form of Masonry," rooted in righteousness and alignment with God. Cain's lineage, however, practiced a "spurious or false version of masonry," marked by corruption, misuse, and evil intentions. This corrupted form persisted until the great flood, outwardly imitating Seth's practices but fundamentally built on selfishness, wickedness, and the manipulation of divine knowledge for malevolent purposes.
