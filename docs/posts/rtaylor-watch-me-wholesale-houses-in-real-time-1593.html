Key insights

  • Wholesaling as a convenience service: Property owners and active investors often utilize wholesalers out of convenience to save time, avoid hiring traditional realtors, and quickly offload properties without making repairs.
  • The 10% rule of thumb for creative finance down payments: When structuring seller financing or subject-to mortgage takeovers, down payments are typically capped at 10% to align buyer and seller expectations, though high-cash-flow properties can justify up to 15%.
  • Subject-to vs. seller financing triggers: Subject-to is best suited for properties with high existing mortgages where the seller has little to no equity, whereas seller financing is ideal for free-and-clear properties or investment properties that fail to cash-flow under traditional bank financing.
  • The "ugly ducklings" of real estate: Two-bedroom, one-bathroom (2-1) homes are generally less desirable to mainstream investors due to lower rental demand from families, making them harder to wholesale unless secured at a steep discount (e.g., $35k–$40k) in a market with proven 2-1 flip comps.
  • Avoid overcomplicating the pitch: Beginners often lose credibility by over-explaining complex legal structures (like land trusts) to agents; instead, positioning oneself naturally as a buyer whose "lender has a few questions" is far more effective.
