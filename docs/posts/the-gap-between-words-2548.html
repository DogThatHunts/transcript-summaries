The gap between words…

A quick look at how hidden conversational rules shape what we really hear beyond the words we speak.

Abstract

Paul Grice showed that the literal words we utter rarely convey the full message. Communication relies on an unspoken Cooperative Principle that guides speakers to be truthful, relevant, succinct, and clear. When these tacit rules are broken, meaning surfaces in the gaps between what is said.

Key takeaways

  • The Cooperative Principle consists of four maxims: truth, relevance, quantity, and manner. (Grice)
  • Meaning that is implied but not spoken is called implicature. (Grice)
  • Listeners often infer hidden information from what is left unsaid, filling the “gap” with their own interpretations.
  • Most of the information transmitted in conversation travels in these gaps rather than the explicit words.
