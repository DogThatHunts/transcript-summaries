Your Brain on GLP-1s: New Research You Should See

How GLP-1 medications might be reshaping your brain — from mood to memory — and what that means for your mental health.

What are GLP-1 receptor agonists?

GLP-1 receptor agonists are medications that mimic the GLP-1 hormone, which regulates blood sugar and appetite. Examples include Ozempic, Wegovy, and Zepbound. Some newer versions, like Manjaro and Zepbound, combine GLP-1 with GIP.

These drugs don't just work in the gut. They also act in the brain, particularly in the hypothalamus and brainstem, where they suppress appetite and influence cravings and nausea.

The vagus nerve, a key part of the parasympathetic nervous system, is also affected. GLP-1s slow gastric emptying, meaning food stays in the stomach longer, leading to a prolonged feeling of fullness.

Beyond the brain, GLP-1s improve insulin sensitivity, reduce glucagon secretion, lower liver glucose production, and enhance fat metabolism in adipose tissue.

Effects on the Brain and cognitive health

GLP-1 medications show neuroprotective effects:

  • BDNF levels increase, a molecule critical for neurogenesis (creation of new neurons).
  • Amyloid and tau deposits — linked to Alzheimer’s and dementia — decrease with GLP-1 use.
  • Neuroinflammation is reduced.
  • Brain glucose metabolism and mitochondrial function improve.

These changes suggest a positive impact on cognitive function and may reduce the risk of dementia.

Mental health and behavioral Effects

Cognitive disorders

  • Evidence from studies shows positive effects on cognitive function and dementia risk.
  • A few studies show no effect, and one shows a negative effect.
  • The overall weight of evidence suggests GLP-1s likely improve cognition.

Substance use disorders

  • The brain circuits involved in obesity and substance use both rely on dopamine.
  • High-sugar, hyperpalatable foods trigger dopamine spikes, followed by cravings and rumination.
  • GLP-1s reduce cravings for processed and high-carbohydrate foods.
  • This may reduce emotional eating and food addiction.
  • Studies suggest GLP-1s may help with alcohol, nicotine, and other substance use disorders.

Psychotic disorders

  • Brain circuits for psychosis (e.g., schizophrenia) do not overlap significantly with those for obesity.
  • There is no strong evidence that GLP-1s affect psychotic symptoms.

Anxiety and mood disorders

  • Obesity is linked to higher rates of depression and anxiety due to inflammation and emotional eating.
  • Conversely, depression and anxiety can lead to weight gain via emotional eating.
  • Research shows mixed results: some studies show no effect, others show negative effects, and a few show positive outcomes.
  • Overall, GLP-1s are not likely to be effective treatments for depression or anxiety.

Eating disorders

  • Eating disorders like binge eating disorder and bulimia share components with obesity, such as emotional eating and disrupted circadian rhythms.
  • GLP-1s may benefit binge eating disorder and potentially bulimia.
  • Research is still emerging, but early studies suggest a positive effect.

Key takeaways

  • GLP-1s have neuroprotective effects and may reduce dementia risk.
  • They may help with substance use disorders by reducing food addiction and emotional eating.
  • They likely have minimal impact on psychotic disorders.
  • Effects on anxiety and depression are uncertain and not currently supported as primary treatment.

If you're considering GLP-1 medication for weight or diabetes, it's important to have informed consent. Discuss how these drugs affect your brain and mental health with your clinician.

You may already have a mental health condition or be at risk — knowing how GLP-1s work can help you make informed decisions.
