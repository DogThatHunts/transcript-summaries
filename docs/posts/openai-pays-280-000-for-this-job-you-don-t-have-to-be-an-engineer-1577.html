The rise of the forward deployed engineer

AI labs are facing a massive implementation bottleneck. While frontier models possess general-purpose capabilities, deploying them to solve specific business problems remains incredibly difficult. Nate highlights a stark gap in the industry: while Anthropic announced plans to train tens of thousands of engineers to deploy AI inside enterprises, they initially trained only 86.

This massive gap between raw model capability and real-world integration has created a surge in demand for forward deployed engineers (FDEs). Companies are paying premium salaries for this talent, with OpenAI offering up to $280,000 in base pay plus equity, and other firms like Handshake posting roles at $300,000. FDEs act as translators, bridging the gap between vague corporate goals, generalized AI models, and complex enterprise codebases.

The anatomy of an FDE: finding the leverage point

To illustrate how an FDE operates, Nate presents a case study of "Maya," a product manager at an insurance company tasked by her CEO with processing claims twice as fast.

Instead of trying to automate the entire complex claims process—which includes medical records, fraud reviews, and legal liabilities—Maya analyzes actual files to find a high-leverage bottleneck. She discovers that incomplete intake forms (e.g., missing signatures) sit unnoticed for days, causing thousands of cumulative delay days each month.

Maya designs an AI intervention specifically for this intake bottleneck. The AI simply flags missing documents and drafts customer communications upon arrival. By targeting this specific point, Maya achieves maximum business leverage: she removes 1,800 days of waiting time per month with a relatively simple build, without giving the model dangerous decision-making authority over payouts or fraud.

The three pillars of the FDE role

Nate breaks down the FDE role into three core competencies. Very few candidates start out equally strong in all three, meaning professionals from various backgrounds can transition into the field:

  • Business judgment and leverage: Identifying where a small technical build can unbottleneck the largest amount of work. This requires deep domain expertise. Nate cites an Anthropic study of 400,000 Claude Code sessions showing that domain experts achieved verified task success more than twice as often as novices.
  • Technical delivery: Building and inspecting the system. While some FDE roles require full-stack coding, others focus on system architecture, data flow, and guardrails. Crucially, FDEs must design "evals" (evaluation test sets) to test model outputs against real-world cases. Nate notes that with modern AI coding assistants, non-technical users can write highly functional code by focusing on system logic rather than syntax.
  • Deployment ownership: Staying with the project post-launch to observe real users, gather feedback, and iteratively refine the system. This requires an ownership mindset typical of strong product managers and software engineers.

How to build an FDE portfolio in 30 days

For those looking to transition into FDE roles, Nate outlines a practical, four-week project framework to demonstrate these skills to employers:

  • Week 1: Process mapping: Identify a recurring, real-world process. Gather 10 to 20 historical cases and map out the workflow to locate the primary bottleneck.
  • Week 2: Shadowing and math: Sit next to the person doing the work to understand undocumented exceptions. Perform back-of-the-napkin math to estimate the business impact of solving the bottleneck, and define strict safety guardrails.
  • Week 3: The build and evals: Build the simplest possible solution using AI coding tools. Construct a test set (evals) of clean and messy historical cases to test and refine your system locally.
  • Week 4: User testing and iteration: Have two or three real users test your solution while you watch. Iterate based on their behavior, and write a summary detailing the exact business impact and technical architecture of your project.

Navigating the job market and adjacent titles

Job seekers should look closely at job descriptions, as technical expectations vary widely. Roles titled Forward Deployed Engineer or Forward Deployed Software Engineer typically demand strong software engineering backgrounds.

However, candidates can use adjacent roles as stepping stones. Titles such as Applied AI Engineer, Customer Engineer, Solutions Engineer, Implementation Engineer, Technical Deployment Lead, or AI Product Manager often require the same translation and workflow-mapping skills but with varying degrees of coding requirements.
