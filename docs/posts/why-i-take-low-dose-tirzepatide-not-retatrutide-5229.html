Why I Take Low-Dose Tezepatide (& Not Retatrutide)

I am a 34-year-old general practitioner without type 2 diabetes or obesity. I do not have a condition that qualifies me for clinical trials involving diabetes or metabolic disease. Yet, I take low-dose tezepatide weekly.

The decision to use tezepatide over other GLP-1 medications, like retatrutide, stems from a deeper understanding of the biological effects of these drugs beyond weight loss.

The evolution of GLP-1 medications

  • Early trials like SELECT and FLOW tested GLP-1 medications in adults with established heart disease or obesity, but without diabetes.
  • The SELECT trial (2023) found that semaglutide reduced major cardiovascular events by 20% in non-diabetic adults, with benefits not fully explained by weight loss.
  • The FLOW trial (2024) showed a 24% reduction in kidney failure and cardiovascular death in patients with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease, with the effect independent of body weight changes.

Evidence of non-weight-loss benefits

  • A 2024 pilot study by Dr. Dai Chen and colleagues at the Shenzhen Institutes of Advanced Technology tested semaglutide in mice with osteoarthritis.
  • Mice given semaglutide showed preserved cartilage, reduced inflammation, and fewer bone spurs—despite losing the same weight as a pair-fed control group.

  • This suggests GLP-1 medications may directly protect joint tissue, independent of weight reduction.

  • A follow-up clinical study in 20 patients with knee osteoarthritis found that those receiving hyaluronic acid plus weekly semaglutide showed an average 17% increase in cartilage thickness on MRI, compared to a 1% loss in the control group.

  • Patients reported reduced pain and improved joint function.

Why Tezepatide over Retatrutide?

  • Tezepatide combines GLP-1 and GIP receptor activation, while retatrutide adds glucagon receptor agonism.
  • Glucagon agonism increases metabolic rate, which may lead to higher resting heart rate (up to 7 beats per minute in phase 2 trials).
  • For someone focused on maintaining lean muscle mass without increased energy expenditure, this is a significant concern.
  • Retatrutide’s cardiovascular risk profile is still under evaluation, and I avoid a drug that accelerates metabolism when I don’t want to lose more weight.

Personal experience and Safety

  • I began with 1.25 mg of tezepatide weekly and noticed a significant drop in food cravings, making healthy eating easier.
  • My genetically high blood pressure improved, and I reduced my blood pressure medication by half.

  • Initial side effects—nausea and fatigue—subsided over time, and I now have no side effects.

  • I maintain muscle mass through resistance exercise and adequate protein intake.

  • Safety concerns include:

  • Pancreatitis: Real-world data show no increased risk.
  • Thyroid cancer: No signal in real-world data.
  • Suicide risk: The FDA and EMA reviewed the data in 2024 and found no causal link.

  • Micronutrient depletion: GLP-1 medications reduce daily calorie intake by 16–39%, leading to potential deficiencies.

  • I take a multivitamin and mineral supplement daily, tested by Labdoor.com with a 99.7% score.

Final thoughts

  • I am not recommending tezepatide for general use, but I am personally committed to its long-term use.
  • The benefits I’ve observed—especially in heart and joint health—are partly independent of weight loss.
  • Exercise remains the most proven, off-label strategy for improving cardiovascular health, and I now believe that even minimal activity can yield significant benefits.
