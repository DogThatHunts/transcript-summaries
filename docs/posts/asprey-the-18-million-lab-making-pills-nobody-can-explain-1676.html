The company claims these particles, when infused into supplements, dramatically enhance cognitive function, longevity, and even consciousness. The conversation blends cutting-edge physics claims, personal anecdotes of enhanced abilities, and measured skepticism about "quantum washing" in the wellness industry.

The technology and the lab

The $18 million facility uses: - Vacuum chambers creating one-billionth of Earth's atmospheric pressure - High-energy lasers - Cold plasma clouds - Silicon wafers and semiconductor equipment

The process allegedly recreates solar surface conditions. Supplement powders (citric choline, alpha GPC, and others) are placed in the vacuum, exposed to cold plasma and laser energy, creating a "nuclear reaction" that produces igniton energy. This energy binds geometrically to hydrogen protons in the ingredients. Since the human body is 70% water, the hypothesis is that "excited hydrogen" either amplifies bioavailability or increases the impact of the active compounds—or both.

The products and results

Ignaton currently offers three supplement lines:

Cognition formula: 83% increase in mental performance, 51% increase in attention, 28% increase in short-term memory, 25% increase in operational memory (30-day results). The charged version was four times more effective than the identical non-charged formula and placebo.

Longevity formula: 37% decrease in IL-6 (inflammatory marker) and 28% decrease in CRP (C-reactive protein), markers associated with aging and disease.

Sleep formula: 25% increase in REM sleep in two weeks, measurable via Oura Ring, Fitbit, or Apple Watch. Users report improved dream recall and vivid dreams, though early iterations were "too powerful" and caused restless sleep, leading to formula adjustments.

All products come with a 30-day money-back guarantee.
