Analyzing the disconnect between U-Haul's positive migration reports and the actual price corrections hitting major Sunbelt and Southern housing markets.

Despite headlines celebrating continued "blue-to-red" state migration based on U-Haul's latest data, the underlying reality of the U.S. housing market has flipped. In this analysis, RE-V explores why top-ranked migration destinations in the Sunbelt are actually experiencing significant home price and rent declines. The illusion of U-Haul migration data

U-Haul's highly publicized migration index ranks Texas and Florida as the top two destinations for one-way truck rentals, with cities like Dallas, Houston, Austin, Charlotte, and Phoenix dominating the top ten. However, RE-V points out a glaring contradiction: eight of the top ten cities on U-Haul's list are currently in a housing market correction, with year-over-year home values declining.

RE-V explains that U-Haul's data only measures the relative share of one-way moves, failing to capture the massive drop in absolute volume: * Slowing overall mobility: According to Bank of America consumer data, metro-to-metro and state-to-state migration has plunged approximately 30% compared to 2022. * Plummeting Sunbelt inbound volume: Census Bureau data shows that actual domestic migration has dropped by 80% in Florida and 70% in Texas over the last three years. * The result: While more people still move to the Sunbelt than leave it, the volume of new arrivals is no longer high enough to absorb the surging housing inventory, causing prices and rental rates to fall.

Sunbelt corrections vs. california's slow decline

The disconnect between relative migration rankings and actual home prices is visible across the country: * Florida: Despite its high U-Haul ranking, home values are sliding. Punta Gorda is down nearly 20% over the last three years, Cape Coral and St. Petersburg are down 15%, and even Miami has begun to decline. * Texas: Austin home values have dropped 23% from their peak. San Antonio is down over 10%, Dallas is down 5% to 10%, and Houston is beginning its downward trend. * California: Positioned at the bottom of U-Haul's list due to high outbound demand (which makes renting an outbound truck incredibly expensive), California is still losing residents. However, its home values are only down about 2% year-over-year statewide, though areas near downtown Los Angeles have dropped over 10% in the last three years.

Case study: why New Orleans is america's most undervalued market

Broadcasting from the Ferret neighborhood (near Uptown) in New Orleans, Louisiana, RE-V highlights the city as a prime example of a market that has already undergone a healthy correction. According to data on the ReVenture app, New Orleans is currently the most undervalued housing market in America, trading at 12% undervalued.

RE-V highlights several notable on-the-ground observations: * Severe price cuts: A four-bedroom home originally listed at $599,000 was cut to $399,000 (below its 2017 pricing). A completely renovated flip saw a $150,000 price cut down to $339,000, and a 2,500-square-foot new build from 2022 is sitting on the market for $450,000 (roughly $160 to $170 per square foot). * The insurance premium drag: New Orleans' steep price drops—down 12% across the metro area, 19% in Orleans Parish, and over 25% in certain zip codes—reflect the high risk and carrying costs of the region. Annual homeowner's and flood insurance premiums average a hefty $4,500. * Improving local conditions: While New Orleans was named the homicide capital of America in 2022, local crime statistics have plummeted over the last three to four years. Combined with rapid gentrification (evidenced by national chains like Cava and Chipotle moving in), RE-V argues that specific neighborhoods offer strong, overlooked value for long-term real estate investors.
