CVS is dismantling the healthcare empire it spent $78 billion building

CVS spent $78 billion building a healthcare empire and is now dismantling it, triggering a 12% stock crash. Investors, however, are responding in a counterintuitive way.

  • In 2007, CVS bought Caremark, a drug price negotiator, and in 2018 acquired Aetna, a major health insurer, pharmacy, and price negotiator—creating one company controlling the entire healthcare chain.
  • Three pressures are driving the breakup: congressional pressure to break up healthcare giants, rising patient demand for care, and Amazon's erosion of drugstore shoppers.
  • The concept behind the split is the conglomerate discount: when one stock holds multiple unrelated businesses, Wall Street often values the whole less than the sum of its parts.
  • CVS CEO David Joyner stated that the split is about taking one great company and making it two, with tax-free shares issued in early 2028.
  • Analysts believe each business could stand independently after a two-year turnaround.
  • One in 59 CVS investors holds Amazon, and every trade in CVS over the last month was a buy—despite the crash—suggesting investors are betting on both sides.
