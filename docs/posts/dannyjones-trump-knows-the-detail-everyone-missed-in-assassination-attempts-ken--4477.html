Trump assassination attempts: security failures and unanswered questions

Overview

Ken Silva, an investigative reporter who spent two years researching the July 2024 Butler, Pennsylvania shooting, joins DannyJones to discuss his book The Trump Assassination Plots: What the Investigations Missed and Why It Matters. Silva presents documented security failures, inconsistencies in official narratives, and suspicious circumstances surrounding both the Butler shooting and a second assassination attempt two months later at a Florida golf course.

Key insights

  1. Thomas Crooks had extensive online presence despite FBI claims: Silva obtained archived data showing Crooks openly discussed assassinating politicians on YouTube years before the shooting, contradicting FBI statements that he had no online communications. He was also in contact with a neo-Nazi in Norway affiliated with a State Department-designated terrorist organization.

  2. Multiple security failures preceded the shooting: Local police spotted Crooks with a rangefinder at 5 p.m., about 11 minutes before he fired. The Secret Service was notified but took no action. Communication failures between local and federal radio channels meant Secret Service snipers never received warnings about the threat.

  3. The Secret Service sniper's account contradicts his own notes: The sniper who fired the fatal shot initially told Congress he didn't see Crooks until after the shooting stopped. However, his handwritten notes taken immediately after the event state he saw Crooks "crawling into place with an AR-15" before the shooting began. Cell phone footage shows him looking through his scope seven seconds before shots were fired.

  4. Crooks' body was left on the rooftop for 12 hours, then cremated with evidence still inside: The body remained exposed until 6 a.m. the next morning. The toxicology report lists only four of seven collected specimens, with no results for hair, eye fluid, or bile samples. The medical examiner released the body for cremation while bullet fragments remained in the shoulder—fragments that could have proven whether a local officer's shot hit Crooks.

  5. The second assassination attempt involved a career criminal with extensive federal contacts: Ryan Ruth had been reported to seven different federal agencies, was under active State Department investigation for arms trafficking, and yet managed to get within firing distance of Trump at a Florida golf course. He showed up at the course approximately 30 minutes after the Secret Service was informed of Trump's golf plans at 2 a.m.

The Butler shooting: July 13, 2024

Background on Ken Silva

Silva has worked as a reporter for nearly 15 years, primarily in local news and overseas. He spent most of his 20s in the Caribbean as a crime reporter before returning to the U.S. in late 2021 to work in conservative media. He chose the Butler shooting as the story he wanted to lead on and has spent two years investigating it.

The security perimeter and AGR building

The shooting occurred at the Butler Farm Show, a 20-acre complex. The AGR building where Crooks positioned himself was approximately 200 yards from Trump's stage but technically outside the Secret Service's designated perimeter. The building's roof was accessible without passing through security checkpoints.

The Secret Service had planned for campaign staff to park trucks and farm equipment to block the line of sight to the building, but this equipment was not in place when the Secret Service arrived that morning. Local law enforcement denies telling the Secret Service the property would be fenced off and secured.

Crooks' activities before the shooting

  • 3:50 p.m.: Crooks flew a drone overhead for approximately 15 minutes, presumably to scout the area and identify security positions. The Secret Service's counter-drone operator had equipment malfunctions all morning and only got his system operational about 10 minutes after Crooks retrieved his drone.

  • 4:26 p.m.: Local police reported seeing a suspicious person on a park bench near the parking lot who appeared to know where snipers were positioned. However, cell phone footage shows Crooks was on the opposite side of the site at Vendor's Row at that exact time—raising questions about who was actually on the bench and why they were considered suspicious.

  • 5:00 p.m.: Local snipers spotted Crooks with a rangefinder outside the AGR building. They texted the local command center, but the message wasn't read for approximately 20 minutes. When the command center finally broadcast the information over local radio, the Secret Service did not relay it to their own sniper teams, who operated on separate communications channels.

  • 6:00 p.m.: As Trump took the stage, local snipers spotted Crooks again. Believing he was running toward the gas station, they directed police to search that area. In reality, Crooks ran between two buildings and used an air conditioning unit to climb onto the roof.

The shooting and response

A local detective, Tyler Collins, drove to the parking lot and had a colleague boost him onto the roof. Crooks turned and pointed his rifle at Collins, who dropped down and radioed that the suspicious person had a gun. This occurred approximately 20 seconds before the shooting started.

Crooks fired eight shots in two bursts (three shots, pause, five shots), suggesting he realized he missed after the first volley. A local SWAT officer, Aaron Zaloponi, returned fire from ground level between Trump and the building. Crooks then stopped shooting.

Ten seconds after Crooks ceased fire, a Secret Service sniper fired the fatal shot that entered Crooks' jaw, exited the other side, re-entered his shoulder, and fragmented. The entire incident lasted approximately 15 seconds from first shot to the sniper's response.

The second assassination attempt: september 2024

Ryan Ruth's background

Ryan Ruth, 58, had an extensive criminal history spanning decades: - 2002: Threatened a worker with a shotgun, employed illegal immigrants, stored dynamite illegally - 2002-2003: Arrested with a "tommy gun" in his car, held police in a standoff for hours - Despite serious felonies, he received only probation and minimal jail time - Later 2000s: Charged with fencing stolen equipment from construction sites - Again received probation despite violating previous conditions

Ruth was reported to at least seven federal agencies: CIA, FBI, DHS, Interpol, Ukraine's federal agencies, and the State Department. The State Department had an active investigation into him for arms trafficking violations at the time of the assassination attempt.

The shooting and escape

Trump was golfing at his Palm Beach course when a Secret Service agent spotted Ruth in the bushes with a rifle pointed at him. The agent fired five shots from approximately 10 feet away and missed all of them. Ruth fled in a Nissan Xterra. A citizen who heard the shots took down Ruth's license plate, which led to his capture 45 minutes later on I-95.

Suspicious circumstances

  • Timing: Trump told the Secret Service at 2 a.m. he wanted to golf that day. Ruth's phone pinged near the golf course approximately 30 minutes later, according to state search warrant records. Federal charging papers claim Ruth arrived before Trump's golf plans were made, creating a discrepancy about which account is accurate.

  • Location knowledge: Ruth had no connections to the Palm Beach area, yet he positioned himself at a spot known to local reporters and paparazzi for photographing Trump. How he knew this location remains unexplained.

  • Classified information: Judge Eileen Cannon (who also presided over Trump's Mar-a-Lago classified documents case) sealed classified information in Ruth's trial under the Classified Information Procedures Act. The DOJ argued that releasing this information would damage national security, though it's unclear why details about a domestic assassination attempt would be classified.

  • Trial conduct: Ruth represented himself and was allowed to do so despite appearing mentally unstable. He was sentenced to life in prison.

Broader patterns and concerns

Silva presents evidence suggesting possible failed sting operations rather than simple incompetence: - Both men had extensive contact with federal informants and undercover agents - Both were known to multiple agencies - Security failures in both cases followed patterns that could indicate deliberate lapses - However, Silva notes that if this were a coordinated assassination plot, Trump would likely be dead
