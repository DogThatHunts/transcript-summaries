Skin health and collagen turnover

Glycine is the primary amino acid in collagen and makes up 30% of its structure. It is essential for collagen turnover, and the body requires significantly more glycine than proline or hydroxyproline.

  • The optimal daily glycine intake is estimated at 15 grams, with 12 grams needed for collagen turnover and 3 grams for vital functions like glutathione and creatine synthesis.
  • The body naturally produces 3 grams daily, but this is insufficient.

Glycine inhibits advanced glycation end products (AGEs), which form during high-temperature cooking or sugary food consumption. AGEs activate MMP1, an enzyme that degrades collagen.

  • AGEs are linked to diseases such as Alzheimer’s, heart disease, and cancer.
  • Glycine reduces AGE formation by lowering blood sugar and boosting glutathione, protecting skin collagen.

Sleep and neurological effects

Glycine improves sleep by lowering body temperature and enhancing GABA signaling, the brain’s primary inhibitory neurotransmitter.

  • Studies show a 3-gram dose before bed can improve sleep quality.
  • I take 3 grams in the evening with tea.

Glycine is generally very safe, with studies giving up to 90 grams daily without side effects.

  • I have personally taken up to 30 grams daily and experienced no negative symptoms.
  • However, some individuals may experience nausea, anxiety, or headaches, possibly due to genetic variations in neurotransmitter pathways.

Scientific evidence and mechanisms

  • Glycine has been shown to extend lifespan in the National Institute of Aging’s Interventions Testing Program (ITP), where a diet with 8% glycine increased male mouse lifespan by 6% and female mouse lifespan by 2%.
  • This effect may stem from methionine restriction, which extends lifespan in animals and human cells by up to 30%.
  • Glycine supplementation can achieve similar benefits without restricting methionine intake.
  • A high methionine-to-glycine ratio is linked to increased visceral fat and reduced fat oxidation.

Dr. Tyler Panzner notes that glycine acts as a co-agonist at the NMDA receptor, which regulates brain activity and memory.

  • Glutamate is an excitatory neurotransmitter; without glycine or serine, it cannot activate the receptor.
  • Excess glutamate—due to genetic mutations or high glutamine levels—can lead to symptoms like anxiety, headaches, and insomnia.
  • These symptoms may be more common in individuals with heightened glutamate sensitivity.

Final thoughts

  • Glycine is one of my favorite supplements and I take it daily.
  • However, it is not the only factor in my health—lifestyle changes such as diet, exercise, and other supplements also play major roles.
  • Glycine may contribute to a single-digit percentage of overall health improvements.
  • If you experience adverse effects, reduce your dose.
