Ancient secrets of how to master your energy field

Historical foundations of magnetic theory

Manly P. Hall traces magnetism back to ancient Egypt and Greece, where it was primarily used in healing. Pythagoras contributed significant knowledge on the subject, though his insights were largely forgotten. The Arabs preserved some understanding, and the Chinese discovered its applications through the development of the Mariner's compass. Magnetism remained largely neglected until the 17th century, when it became intertwined with Rosicrucianism, alchemy, and hermetic philosophy. Hall notes that alchemists, Rosicrucians, and hermitists all unfolded different phases of magnetic theory. Later, mesmerists developed the "magnetic tub"—a tank where multiple people would place their feet, hold hands, and create a battery of energy through the water.

The nature of air and atmospheric magnetism

Hall argues that the foundation of magnetic theory rests on a simple but profound fact: what we call empty air is not empty at all, but rather "the most profoundly used of all the elements that we know anything about." Air is not merely oxygen or helium, but a substance in itself—a tremendous field of magnetic energy. The atmosphere carries the magnetism of the sun, and seasonal variations (winter, summer, spring) are all connected to magnetic factors in the air.

Hall uses television as an analogy to explain how magnetic fields work: just as multiple programs can broadcast simultaneously from different directions and remain separately identified within their own channels, so too can infinite complexity of separate emanations exist within the magnetic field. This vibration in the magnetic field can be divided infinitely, allowing thousands of different channels to be differentiated and broadcast correctly. Thus, we live in the presence of "a mysterious agent, an agent that is part of everything that we are."

The human energy field and its protective power

According to Hall's interpretation of research by Tilda and others, each human body is surrounded by an etheric or energy field—sometimes called an aura, though Hall distinguishes this magnetic field from the metaphysical aura. This energy field forms an egg-like atmospheric sheath around the physical body, typically extending three to five feet in each direction.

Hall argues that this energy field is "the basis of virtue" because its reality, serviceability, and protecting power depend entirely on the emotions, thoughts, and attitudes of the person around whom it gathers. If an individual maintains mental, emotional, and physical balance—"keeping the rules"—the magnetic field forms tremendous protection. It remains healthy, constantly able to handle infections and difficulties, helps heal wounds, aids recovery of organ function, and compensates for lost body parts.

Morality as energetic integrity

Hall presents a radical reframing of morality: maintaining the integrity of one's magnetic field is not merely a spiritual or ethical concern, but a matter of survival. The energy that sustains this field comes from the sun through "a mysterious energy tube" in the magnetic field, entering through the crown of the head, disseminating through the entire body, and being excreted and recleansed through the lower centers.

Breaking natural laws damages this magnetic flow. Hall lists multiple ways the field can be compromised: physical intemperance, riotous living, negative moods and attitudes, fears, complexes, alcohol, drugs, and narcotics. When the field is damaged, it immediately reacts negatively on health, reducing vitality, making the individual more susceptible to contagion and infection, and shortening life expectancy.

Hall argues that virtue itself is defined by keeping the law of the energy field, which is also "the law of integrity." Losing one's disposition and temperament is wrong; using negative, destructive attitudes is wrong; compromising principles of right living is wrong; thinking badly, feeling unpleasantly, or engaging in actions contrary to the common good all damage the magnetic field and are therefore wrong. Selfishness injures the field, and breaking the Ten Commandments damages it. Hall emphasizes that this has nothing to do with national codes or ordinary legal concepts—it is based on "the life principle in each part of the human constitution."

The magnetic field of organs and cells

Hall extends this principle to the body's organs and systems. Each organ—the stomach, heart, brain, glandular structure, intestines, nervous system, endocrine system—has its own magnetic field and is a living organism cooperating with others for the common good. To abuse one organ is to damage all; to neglect one is to neglect all. Each organ's field contributes to the grand magnetic field surrounding the complete person.

Hall references Pythagoras and ancient Egyptian temple practices where therapy was achieved through contemplation of symmetrical geometric solids (cubes, octagons, dodecahedrons). Looking upon perfect geometric forms was therapeutic because accepting their energy as reality improved the observer's own field. Hall argues that every form in nature—natural or artificial—has a magnetic field, from the tiniest atom to the greatest galaxy, and the rules of each must be obeyed.

Harmony, discord, and external influence

Hall claims that visualizing perfection improves ourselves, while accepting discord as inevitable injures us. All natural things are benevolent; it is only when abused by humanity that these benevolences are lost. He argues that we live in a universe where everything is in harmony if we are.

On the question of whether others' negative magnetic fields can harm us, Hall takes a nuanced position: the magnetic field protects us against negative fields that do not arise within ourselves. We are not contaminated by others unless we consciously surrender our integrity through weakness—intellectual, emotional, or otherwise. We are responsible for the effects of our own mistakes on our own magnetic field.

Hall connects dispositional faults to physical illness. An individual becomes ill because of a bad disposition—not merely as a symbol, but because they have damaged the magnetic field of an essential part of their nature. If one damages the magnetic field of the digestive system, dyspepsia results; if prolonged, chronic ailments can develop. However, Hall notes that no individual is entirely infected from themselves, but never completely without themselves—all unities must be in harmony.

Magnetism, alchemy, and nutrition

Hall connects these ideas to Rosicrucian philosophy and alchemy, which he describes as "the transmutation of various phases of life" and "a transformation and a transmutation of energies"—essentially the same energies present in magnetism. Everything we do and see has its own values.

Hall argues that crystals are formed by magnetism—by a rate of vibration peculiar to a certain element. This vibration can evolve through mineral, plant, vegetable, and animal kingdoms, always present within a magnetic core. Each kingdom unfolds within this magnetic field, with individual members variously conditioned by degrees of growth.

Extending this to nutrition, Hall claims that every cell within food products has its own magnetic field. Therefore, nutrition is largely the study of the magnetic fields of various foods. When food products are adulterated or poisoned, their magnetic fields are compromised—a problem that cannot be solved merely by passing laws against physical misuse, since "the physical factor is only a fragment of it."

The universal principle of integrity

Hall argues that behind all problems—whether governmental, religious, or philosophical—lies a fundamental lack of integrity, which is simply "keeping the laws and rules of energy fields." Each field has its own integrities, all of which are compatible with one another. All departures from integrity in any field are in constant conflict. The only way individuals can escape conflict is by never abusing the energy factors of their own life—never abusing body, emotions, or mind, and never developing attitudes incompatible with the integrities nature has bestowed.

Hall emphasizes that magnetic fields are "absolutely honest" and cannot be made dishonest. When we break their rules, we lose the benefit of that particular energy. What we call evil is not an evil thing coming to us, but rather "the failure of a good thing to be developed and purified and intensified."

Evolution and the path to harmony

Hall argues that magnetic fields are in constant evolution within each person's life. An individual may be born at a certain level of magnetic integrity, but by becoming a better person, they strengthen these values. Perfection is a matter of gradually strengthening an energy resource—a process that may take many lifetimes but results in continuous growth and harmonious adjustment to the principle of life.

Hall concludes by observing that contemporary society is "in a sad state in which practically everyone has broken every conceivable rule," living day by day trying to profit from mistakes. He argues this has nothing to do primarily with theology, government laws, or legal codes, but rather with "the relationship of energy to its proper ends and purposes."

Everything that exists has a magnetic field, visible or not. The entire atmosphere and world is "one mass of magnetic interplays." As long as these are kept honest, they are all compatible. Hall's final vision is of a universe in which there was no dishonesty—a universe that would be "free from every infirmity."
