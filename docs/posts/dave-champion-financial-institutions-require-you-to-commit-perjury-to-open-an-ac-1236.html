The legal classification of taxpayers and withholding agents

Dave Champion explains his theory that the U.S. income tax system is fundamentally designed around only two taxable classes of persons: non-resident aliens with U.S. source income and foreign corporations with U.S. source income. He groups these together as "foreign persons."

According to Champion, there is a third class of persons—U.S. citizens, residents, or domestic corporations—who are not directly subject to the tax but are made liable as "withholding agents." These domestic agents are legally responsible for withholding tax from U.S. source income before it is paid out to a foreign owner. Champion argues that the thousands of pages of Internal Revenue Service (IRS) regulations exist solely to administer this cross-border withholding system, rather than to tax the domestic earnings of ordinary Americans.

The W-9 form and the alleged financial perjury trap

The jurisdictional limits of federal employment taxes
