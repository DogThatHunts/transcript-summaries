Constitutional framework of the 16th Amendment

Dave Champion explains the distinction established by the U.S. Supreme Court between direct taxes and indirect taxes. Direct taxes historically applied to property and land, requiring statutory apportionment among states so Congress could not burden the accumulation of wealth. Indirect taxes encompass excises, tariffs, and imposts, with federal courts defining an excise tax specifically as a levy on a government-granted privilege.

Addressing common assumptions about the 16th Amendment, Dave Champion states that the amendment did not authorize a new tax on labor. Instead, Dave Champion cites Supreme Court rulings holding that the 16th Amendment granted Congress no new powers of taxation, but simply resolved an accounting error from the 1895 Pollock case. Because earning a living and contracting labor are unalienable rights existing prior to the creation of the Constitution, Dave Champion argues that labor cannot be subjected to an excise tax without violating fundamental constitutional principles.
