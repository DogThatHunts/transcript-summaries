The family tree of Adam and Eve

According to Jubilees, Adam and Eve had far more children than the Bible's Genesis account mentions—not just Cain and Abel, but dozens of sons and daughters. The text provides specific names for these offspring and claims that siblings married one another to populate the earth. Cain's wife was his sister Awan, and Abel married his sister Azura. What is the Book of Jubilees?

Jubilees is a non-canonical text, meaning it was considered for inclusion in the Bible but ultimately rejected. Scholars debate its origins, with estimates placing its composition between 135 and 105 BCE—making it older than some biblical books. The text claims that an angel of God revealed all its information directly to Moses on Mount Sinai, though scholars believe it was likely written by a single author (possibly a priest or scribe) or compiled from multiple sources. The true authorship remains unknown.

A radically different creation story

Jubilees rewrites the Genesis creation account in striking ways. Most notably, it claims that angels were created on the very first day—before light, before the earth—contradicting Genesis, which never mentions when angels were created. The text also reinterprets the "days" of creation as significant epochs rather than literal 24-hour periods. Additionally, Jubilees specifies that Adam and Eve spent exactly seven years in the Garden of Eden before their expulsion, a detail absent from Genesis.

An alternative biblical timeline

The traditional biblical timeline from Adam to Noah spans approximately 1,656 years. Jubilees, however, introduces a completely different chronological system using "Jubilee years" and "weeks of years," where a Jubilee equals 49 years. This reshapes the timeline of early human history dramatically. SecretWithin argues that Jubilees places major events—including the Great Flood—at points significantly different from the traditional biblical chronology. The text provides specific dates for events Genesis doesn't even mention, such as exactly when Cain killed Abel and when Adam died.

Hidden divine laws

Jubilees claims that God gave Moses far more commandments than the Ten Commandments recorded in Exodus. These additional laws cover every aspect of life, from dietary rules to marriage restrictions. The text goes into detailed specificity about sexual behavior and forbidden relationships, reflecting what SecretWithin describes as "a deep concern for maintaining purity within the community." Remarkably, Jubilees asserts that the patriarchs—Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob—already knew and followed these laws centuries before Moses, raising questions about how such knowledge was lost.

The controversial solar calendar

One of Jubilees' most explosive claims concerns sacred time. While the traditional Jewish calendar is lunar (354 days), Jubilees insists on a solar calendar of 364 days, divided into four seasons of 91 days each. The text argues that using the correct calendar is essential for righteousness and claims that celebrating religious festivals on the wrong days constitutes sin. SecretWithin suggests this calendar dispute played a significant role in Jubilees' exclusion from the canon, as it directly challenged religious authorities who controlled the lunar calendar and thus had power over sacred time.

Why religious authorities rejected Jubilees

The exclusion of Jubilees from the biblical canon resulted from centuries of debate among religious leaders. Some Hebrew scholars, particularly the Essenes, embraced the text for its detailed guidance on righteous living. However, mainstream Jewish and Christian authorities rejected it for several reasons:

  • Contradictions with established scripture: Its alternative timeline and theological ideas conflicted with accepted biblical narratives.
  • Threat to authority: The strict laws and alternative calendar system challenged existing religious practices and threatened institutional control over religious life.
  • Consistency concerns: Religious authorities decided Jubilees' contradictions with established texts were too significant to ignore.
  • Possible Gnostic associations: Some scholars theorize that Jubilees' emphasis on secret knowledge and detailed revelations about angels and demons seemed too close to Gnosticism, which authorities viewed as dangerous.

Lasting influence and modern rediscovery

Despite its exclusion from the canon, Jubilees continued to influence religious thought for centuries. Today, as more people discover this hidden text, it is sparking renewed debates about the nature of biblical history and divine revelation.
