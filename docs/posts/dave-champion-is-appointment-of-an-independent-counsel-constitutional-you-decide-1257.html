Historical context of independent counsel appointments

The Independent Counsel Act and its expiration

Following the chaos of Watergate, Congress passed the Special Prosecutor Act in 1978 (later renamed the Independent Counsel Act) to formalize the appointment process and prevent presidential interference. Champion emphasizes that this act was not created because prior appointments were deemed unconstitutional, but to minimize future chaos. The act allowed the Attorney General to appoint an independent counsel, whose decision was not subject to judicial review, and the counsel could only be removed under specific circumstances. The act expired in 1999, after several extensions.
