Rising HOA fees, massive special assessments, and surging apartment construction are driving a major downturn in the U.S. condo market, serving as a warning for broader housing.

The emerging condo market downturn

Nick from RE-V argues that a significant condo market downturn is underway across the United States. According to data cited from Zillow and the Wall Street Journal, condo values are declining nationally, down 1.9% year-over-year. In several highly exposed markets, the drops from peak pricing are far more severe:

  • Cape Coral, Florida: Down 30% since 2022
  • Oakland, California: Down 29%
  • St. Petersburg, Florida: Down 27%
  • Jacksonville, Florida: Down 18%
  • Tampa, Florida: Down 18%
  • San Francisco, California: Down 15%
  • Denver, Colorado: Down 15%

Nick notes that sellers are taking substantial losses, with some discounting properties by over $150,000 compared to their purchase prices from just two or three years prior.

Why condos serve as a leading housing indicator

While a poll of Nick's viewers indicates that over 70% are looking to buy single-family homes and only 5% are interested in condos, he argues that all buyers should monitor the condo sector. Condos represent a highly volatile and sensitive asset class. Because they are frequently purchased as second or third homes and carry mandatory monthly fees, they are typically the first segment of the housing market to decline during a downturn and the first to recover during an upturn. Historical data from the 2005–2006 crash shows condo values peaked and dipped before single-family homes followed, and subsequently led the recovery in 2015.

The financial trap of soaring HOA fees and special assessments

Rising Homeowners Association (HOA) fees are identified as the primary catalyst for the current condo distress. In cities like New York and various municipalities in Florida, monthly HOA fees frequently exceed $1,000.

This inflation is driven by two main factors: 1. Skyrocketing building insurance: This is particularly acute in coastal Florida markets. 2. High administrative payrolls: Nick highlights a Nashville condo building where HOA fees reached $500 a month partly to fund a $190,000 property manager salary and a $100,000 assistant manager salary.

Unplanned capital expenditures also trigger massive "special assessments." Nick highlights an extreme example in St. Petersburg, Florida, where a condo's asking price was slashed 75%—from $1 million down to $250,000—because the unit was burdened with a $1,500 monthly HOA fee and an active $300,000 special assessment for building repairs.

Increased competition from the apartment rental boom

Condo owners who bought units as investment properties are facing steep competition from a surge in new luxury apartment construction. To attract tenants, apartment developers are offering concessions such as three months of free rent. This supply boom has severely depressed rental values, making it harder for condo owners to cover their mortgages and HOA fees through renting. In Austin, Texas, apartment rents have plummeted by 22%, directly undermining the underlying investment value of area condos.

Geographic trends and downtown market forecasts

Nick observes that almost every major U.S. urban core is facing downward price pressure due to rising inventory, longer days on market, and post-pandemic shifts in foot traffic and crime rates.

  • Birmingham, Alabama: Walking through downtown Birmingham, Nick notes a highly bifurcated market with a downward 12-month home price forecast of -7.5%. Inventory is high, and sales have plummeted, though wealthy suburban pockets like Shelby County (e.g., Vestavia) remain highly affluent with median incomes around $160,000.
  • West and South Coasts: Downtown areas in Los Angeles, Dallas, Atlanta, and Austin are all experiencing downward price forecasts.
  • Potential bottoms: Conversely, Nick suggests that San Francisco may be starting to bottom out and rebound, driven by the local artificial intelligence boom and rising apartment rents. Similarly, New York City experienced a 20% decline between 2022 and 2025 and may be poised for a stabilization.

Due diligence checklist for potential condo buyers

For buyers looking to capitalize on deep discounts, Nick warns that a low purchase price can easily be offset by hidden liabilities. He advises performing rigorous due diligence before purchasing any condo:

  • Review the special assessment history: Determine how frequently the building levies extra charges on owners.
  • Examine the property condition report and reserve schedule: Ensure the building has planned for major structural maintenance.
  • Check the association's bank balances: Verify that the HOA has sufficient cash reserves to cover future repairs without needing to issue emergency assessments.
  • Analyze the HOA operating budget: Look for bloated line items, such as excessive management payroll or rising insurance premiums, that could signal future fee hikes.
