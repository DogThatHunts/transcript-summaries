The legal definition of "privilege" in tax codes

Champion contrasts this with unalienable rights, citing U.S. Supreme Court precedent establishing that citizens have an inherent right to acquire, possess, utilize, and dispose of property, as well as to contract for labor. The origin of sales tax and the "foreign corporation" theory

To explain who is actually subject to sales tax, Champion introduces his theory regarding "foreign corporations." He notes that while individual citizens have a constitutional right to conduct business across state lines without state-imposed travel taxes, corporations do not share this inherent right.

According to Champion, a corporation chartered in one state (e.g., Alabama) is considered a "foreign corporation" in any other state (e.g., Ohio). To operate in a host state, the foreign corporation must apply for permission. Champion argues that this state-granted permission to a foreign corporation is the exact "government-granted privilege" upon which sales taxes are legally levied. He describes sales tax as a protectionist measure originally designed to level the playing field between local businesses and incoming out-of-state corporations.

He emphasizes that applying for a resale permit is a voluntary act of self-enrollment, wherein a business owner legally attests that they are engaged in a taxable privilege, thereby placing themselves under the state's tax jurisdiction.
