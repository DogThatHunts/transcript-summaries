The John Chang phenomenon

In the early 1980s, documentary filmmakers traveled to Indonesia to capture footage of John Chang, a master of the little-known Mo-Pai tradition, which is part of internal alchemy (Nei Dan in Chinese). Chang demonstrated abilities that left observers speechless: acupuncture via electrical discharge, igniting paper with bare hands, piercing wooden tables with plastic toothpicks, and moving objects without physical contact. Years later, a team of scientists returned with measuring equipment to test these phenomena, witnessing the demonstrations firsthand without finding clear evidence of fraud, though they also lacked definitive explanation.

According to his own testimony, Chang was visited throughout an entire night by his deceased master, who severely reprimanded him for violating the strict taboos of his lineage by allowing recordings. Deeply impacted, Chang withdrew completely from public life, forbade any new recordings, and swore never to demonstrate his abilities again. For years, nothing more was known about him until Mel Gibson shared a striking account on The Joe Rogan Experience of his own qigong master, who could point at him from a distance and project a force "as palpable as someone pushing you around"—without any physical contact. Gibson also described an incident where the master's energy work on his back propelled him up a wall with eight inches of air beneath his heels.

Chi as universal energy

SecretWithin argues that to understand these phenomena, we must stop thinking of chi as mystical and start seeing it as what many traditions describe: a universal force. The ancient Taoists, through centuries of external observation and internal introspection, concluded that all forms of life are founded on a single principle—an energy that animates existence.

In Taoism, chi is not merely something that circulates within the body but the energy that fills and sustains everything. This principle is explained through the "three powers" (san sai): heaven, earth, and human. The chi of heaven refers to solar light, lunar influence, natural cycles, and stellar radiation—everything that conditions life on earth. The chi of earth relates to the planet's magnetic field, energetic currents running through the ground, and invisible patterns structuring the environment. The chi of human is your personal energy, a dynamic system in constant interaction with those two forces. From this perspective, the human being is not isolated but a point of connection between heaven and earth—a living bridge.

What science today describes as bioelectricity could simply be a modern way of understanding this same phenomenon. A flow of energy generated through breathing and nutrition, but which, according to these teachings, can be directed by something even more powerful: the mind.

The body as an electrical and energetic system

The human body is not just matter but the visible manifestation of something invisible—energy in motion. It is a living, dynamic system organized around a central axis: the spine. Along this axis, Taoists identified three main energy centers known as Dantian, or "Fields of the Elixir": upper, middle, and lower.

The upper Dantian, located in the head, is associated with consciousness, perception, and subtle energy. The lower Dantian, located in the abdomen, is the center where energy accumulates and is stored. One defines the quality of energy; the other defines quantity. Both are connected through the central axis, functioning as a single integrated system. When these centers are in balance, energy flows smoothly and the body responds while the mind stabilizes. When balance is broken, energy begins to scatter.

The body can also be understood as a living electrical system. Each cell functions like a tiny battery, generating energy from oxygen, food, and breathing. Millions of cells die and are born every day, and the quality of this process depends largely on the energy available and how it circulates within us.

Internal alchemy and the transformation of energy

The flow of energy between the Dantian constitutes the heart of Taoist internal alchemy, Neidan. When sufficient heat is generated in the lower Dantian as a result of the interaction between the fire of the heart and the water of the kidneys, vital essence (Jing) transforms into chi energy. This energy rises to the middle Dantian where it merges with Shen (spirit) and finally reaches the upper Dantian, becoming light or spiritual energy. The goal of Qigong and internal alchemy is to develop this process: to enhance energy within the body, increase the circulation of chi, and achieve harmony between body, mind, and spirit.

Once the energy becomes more palpable, allow this sensation to move more broadly, up and down along the meridians of the arms and legs, toward the extremities, as if all the body's energy pathways were being irrigated by a continuous current.
