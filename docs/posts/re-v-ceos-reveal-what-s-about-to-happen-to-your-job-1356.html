Block, the payments company run by Jack Dorsey, laid off 40% of staff and blamed AI. Meta is rumored to be planning a 20% reduction. RE-V emphasizes this is unlike anything seen before: six to 12 months ago there was chatter about AI-driven cuts, but not at this scale or speed.

Why it's happening now

RE-V rejects the narrative that these are simply corrections for pandemic overhiring. When you examine the data, companies are cutting back to 2020 headcount levels while revenue per worker is "going through the roof." This is not a return to normal; it's an adaptation to what AI tools now allow them to do. Oracle, for example, has committed approximately $150 billion in capital expenditure to AI data center buildout and is taking on debt to fund it—hence the need to cut costs through layoffs.

Which jobs are vulnerable?

RE-V predicts mass layoffs in tech, HR, marketing, and finance—sectors already showing cuts. However, in-person jobs, manufacturing, construction, and blue-collar work are unlikely to face AI automation pressure in the foreseeable future. The regional impact will be highly localized to tech hubs: San Francisco, Denver, LA, Austin, and Boulder will see the most disruption.

The housing market fracture

Pre-foreclosure filings are surging across the US, with Florida leading at a 72% year-over-year increase. This signals downward pressure on home prices in hard-hit areas as laid-off workers struggle to maintain mortgages. However, prices are not dropping everywhere. In wealthy suburbs like Sandy Springs, Georgia, multimillion-dollar new builds are selling at $3.2 million and above, with mortgage payments exceeding $25,000 per month. This bifurcation reflects a broader economic split: some people are losing jobs and facing foreclosure; others have substantial wealth and are buying luxury real estate.

The oracle-to-nashville cautionary tale

RE-V draws a parallel to Oracle's 2020 relocation to Austin, Texas, which sparked excitement and real estate speculation. Everyone said "prices in Austin will never go down." Yet Austin has since experienced a 25% price decline—the biggest housing crash in America. Oracle is now relocating its worldwide headquarters to Nashville, Tennessee, with completion expected by 2031. RE-V warns against chasing corporate relocations as an investment thesis: the headlines about big companies moving to a city have minimal long-term impact on home prices. Instead, focus on data—home price forecasts, long-term growth scores, and negative equity rates—to make sound investment decisions.
