EpicRE | Bank Just Locked 3 Million People Out of their INSURED Savings Accounts

A bank in Paraguay, Ueno, locked out about 3 million people from their insured savings accounts after a government takeover on November 3rd. The incident mirrors potential risks in U.S. banking systems.

  • Retirees were unable to access their pensions after the bank’s app went dark and services stopped.
  • The government took over the bank, and the central bank chief resigned, with the president quitting days later in a video.
  • The first payment to affected customers came with three conditions: up to $1,565, no outstanding debt, and an existing account at a different bank.
  • The FDIC typically pays insured depositors within one business day, covering up to $250,000 per person, per bank, per ownership category — including joint accounts and IRAs.
  • A key risk is that one large transaction, like a house closing, can exceed the $250,000 limit and take years to recover.
  • The FDIC has a free tool, EDI, to calculate your deposit limits.
  • A 2024 savings app collapse — Synapse — left over 100,000 Americans locked out, with $96 million lost, because the app wasn’t a real bank.
  • If your savings app isn’t a bank, verify which bank holds your funds and ensure it’s in your name.
  • Experts recommend keeping a second bank with no ties to your primary one, holding enough for a month’s expenses.
