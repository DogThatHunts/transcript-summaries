The Nun Study

A short, compelling look at how cognitive ability and early-life writing predict dementia later in life.

  • The Nun Study tracks 678 nuns over decades, assessing their cognitive performance annually and examining their brains after death.
  • Despite showing signs of dementia in their brains at death, some nuns—like Sister Mary—remained sharp throughout life, doing activities like crosswords until age 101.
  • Researchers found that the language used in essays written by these nuns when they were 19 or 20 years old can predict who will later develop dementia.
  • Complex, dense language in early writings correlates with lower risk of dementia, while simpler sentences are linked to higher risk.
