Dave Champion | Why RFK Jr Is Failing At Making America Healthy Again?

Author and commentator Dave Champion critiques RFK Jr.’s early tenure at HHS, arguing that systemic regulatory capture, political compromises, and a reliance on government solutions hinder true health reform.

Dave champion's critique of the MAHA agenda

In this presentation, author and commentator Dave Champion evaluates the progress of the "Make America Healthy Again" (MAHA) agenda under Robert F. Kennedy Jr.’s leadership at the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS). Roughly seven months into the administration, Champion argues that RFK Jr. is failing to achieve substantive reform due to regulatory capture, political compromises, and a failure to aggressively challenge established health narratives.

The limits of RFK Jr.’s early wins at HHS

Champion analyzes several policy actions from early 2025, characterizing them as superficial public relations victories rather than systemic reforms:

  • Voluntary food dye agreements: Champion highlights that Big Food's agreement to remove certain food dyes was entirely voluntary rather than regulatory. He suggests this indicates the immense difficulty of forcing change through the regulatory process and claims a quid pro quo was likely involved, allowing companies to revert their policies once the administration leaves office.
  • CDC thimerosal recommendations: In May 2025, the CDC's Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices recommended that healthy individuals from six months of age receive thimerosal-free flu vaccines. Champion argues this recommendation has near-zero real-world impact because only one thimerosal-free flu vaccine is available in the U.S., and the general public remains unaware of the guidance.

The FDA, Pfizer, and the politics of mrna vaccines

Champion criticizes recent FDA guidance regarding COVID-19 vaccines, alleging political interference and corporate favoritism:

  • Selective vaccine guidance: The FDA revised its guidance in May 2025 to recommend that low-risk populations avoid Novavax and Moderna COVID-19 vaccines. However, Pfizer’s mRNA vaccine was notably excluded from this recommendation.
  • The Bourla-Trump connection: Champion claims that President Donald Trump restricted RFK Jr. from releasing HHS's massive data trove on SARS-CoV-2 and mRNA injections. He attributes this restriction to private dinners between Trump and Pfizer CEO Albert Bourla prior to the inauguration. Champion argues that releasing this data could expose inaccuracies in Pfizer's 2020 clinical trial data, potentially stripping the company of its legal immunity.
  • Moderna pediatric approval: Champion points to the FDA's July 2025 full approval of Moderna's mRNA vaccine for children aged six months to 11 years as a direct contradiction to the agency's own safety guidance for low-risk populations.

The bully pulpit model: champion's blueprint for health reform

Champion outlines how he believes the HHS Secretary should operate, advocating for an aggressive, confrontational approach to public health communication:

  • Exposing the food pyramid: Champion argues that RFK Jr. should have used his first days in office to publicly declare the federal food pyramid a fraud designed to support agricultural profits rather than human health.
  • The carnivore diet and diabetes: Champion asserts that type 2 diabetes could be entirely eliminated within 120 days if patients adopted a carnivore diet.
  • A hypothetical clinical trial: To prove this claim, Champion proposes a highly controlled, 500-participant study conducted on a sequestered military base. Participants would be paid $4,000 per month to eat a strict carnivore diet for four months. Champion suggests that upon the study's successful conclusion, HHS should hold a press conference to publicly name and blacklist any journalists, medical professionals, or media outlets that previously disputed the claim.

FDA regulatory updates and the legacy of Ancel Keys

While largely critical of federal agencies, Champion notes some potential areas of progress under FDA Commissioner Dr. Marty McCary:

  • Demands for patience: McCary recently urged the public to have patience regarding the review of mRNA products. Champion rejects this plea, arguing that a competent scientific team could prove within weeks that the 2020 trial data submitted by Pfizer and Moderna was inaccurate and insufficient, justifying an immediate revocation of their approvals.
  • Saturated fat guidelines: Champion expresses cautious optimism regarding McCary's announcement that the FDA will review federal dietary guidelines on saturated fat. Champion attributes the historical bias against saturated fat to Ancel Keys, whom he accuses of taking bribes from the sugar industry in the late 1950s to falsify research and shift blame away from carbohydrates.

Personal responsibility and the rejection of federal authority

Ultimately, Champion argues that relying on the federal government to solve public health issues is fundamentally flawed. He asserts that the decline of American health accelerated in 1980 when the government began issuing dietary guidelines. Instead of waiting for government initiatives, Champion advocates for personal liberty and individual responsibility, urging citizens to educate themselves on human physiology through his book, Body Science.

In addition to his health claims, Champion promotes his book Income Tax: Shattering the Mist. He presents the highly contested legal claim that federal law does not actually impose an income tax on ordinary working Americans. Citing his own 32-year history of not filing tax returns or paying income tax, Champion argues that the tax system is a "scam" perpetuated by government propaganda, and that citizens who study the actual statutes can safely exit the system.
