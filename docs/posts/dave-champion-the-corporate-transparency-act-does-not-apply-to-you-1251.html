The Corporate Transparency Act and federal jurisdiction

Dave Champion argues that the Corporate Transparency Act (CTA) and its Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) reporting requirements to FinCEN do not apply to corporations created within the 50 states of the Union. He outlines a legal theory dividing congressional power into two categories: subject-matter jurisdiction (nationwide enumerated powers granted by the states) and territorial jurisdiction (authority limited strictly to Washington, D.C., and U.S. territories or possessions). Champion asserts that because states never ceded corporate regulatory authority to the federal government, the CTA operates solely under federal territorial jurisdiction.

Statutory definitions and legal interpretations

Champion analyzes statutory phrasing within the CTA, contending that the definition of "state" in the act refers exclusively to federal territories rather than the 50 Union states. He points to alternative statutory definitions in Title 26 that explicitly specify "the 50 states" as evidence that Congress differentiates between federal territories and Union states. Additionally, he highlights an exemption within the CTA for entities not owned directly or indirectly by foreign persons, arguing this excludes domestic companies owned by American citizens. He references the March 2024 ruling in National Small Business United v. Yellen, where a federal district court found the CTA unconstitutional for exceeding enumerated powers, though he notes the court did not address territorial jurisdiction specifically.

State involvement and treasury dispute letters

According to Champion, state governments assist federal enforcement of the CTA primarily to secure federal grant funding. He claims state Secretary of State offices distribute reporting notices alongside annual business franchise renewals to encourage voluntary compliance through fear of statutory penalties. To counter this, Champion promotes a paid custom letter template designed for business owners to submit to the U.S. Treasury Department to dispute BOI reporting demands.

Promotional materials, tax theories, and health claims

Throughout the presentation, Champion promotes several self-published books available through his website:

  • Tax and legal publications: In Income Tax: Shattering the Myths, Champion contends that Congress never legally enacted an income tax on ordinary workers, asserting he has not filed or paid income taxes for 31 years. His companion guides, The Business Guide to Payroll Withholding and The Business Handbook for W-9 and 1099, claim that private companies lack statutory authority to withhold employee pay or mandate W-9 filings.
