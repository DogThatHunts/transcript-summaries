The transition from wet to dry fasting

Lauren explains that her journey began with a failed five-day water fast. By day three, she experienced severe dizziness and lethargy, leading her to believe water fasting was not right for her. Later, she accidentally went nearly 24 hours without food or water during a busy workday and felt highly energized, with complete mental clarity.

Because she was already adapted to a ketogenic, carnivore lifestyle, her body was primed to run on fat stores and ketones. She notes that unlike water fasting, which often triggers constant hunger pangs when water enters the digestive tract, dry fasting completely suppresses both hunger and thirst for extended periods.

The science of deep autophagy and localized healing

According to Lauren, dry fasting forces the body into an extreme state of survival, accelerating autophagy—the process where the body breaks down old, damaged, or diseased cells and replaces them with new ones. She claims that this process is highly targeted:

  • Old injuries: On days four and five of her fast, Lauren experienced acute pain in her lower right abdomen (the site of an appendix removal three years prior) and her shoulder (where she had arthritic pain). She argues this pain represents the body actively repairing deep scar tissue and inflammation.
  • Pathogen starvation: While water fasting allows bacteria, viruses, and parasites to survive on consumed water, dry fasting deprives these pathogens of the moisture they need to exist, causing them to die off.
  • Detoxification pathways: Lauren describes releasing orange-colored debris (which she calls "slag") in her urine during her first seven-day fast. In her subsequent eight-day fast a year later, she noticed significantly less debris, which she attributes to having a lower baseline toxic load.

The host notes that the concept of breakfast was heavily promoted by cereal companies to boost sales, shifting society away from intuitive eating.

To understand natural human biology, Lauren suggests looking at children and animals, who instinctively refuse both food and water when sick. She believes dry fasting taps into this same primitive, self-healing mechanism.

Preparation, execution, and refeeding protocols

For those interested in dry fasting, Lauren and the host outline a structured protocol to ensure safety:

  • Preparation: In the days leading up to a fast, individuals should consume nutrient-dense animal proteins and fats, taper off caffeine to avoid withdrawal headaches, and heavily saturate the body with high-quality electrolytes (recommending sugar-free options like Keto Pro).
  • Mindset: Lauren advises setting a modest goal, such as 24 hours, and extending it day-by-day based on how the body feels, rather than committing to a long multi-day fast upfront, which can lead to psychological pressure and failure.
  • Activity level: While light walking in nature is encouraged, strenuous workouts and gym sessions should be avoided. The body needs to conserve energy to focus entirely on internal cellular repair.
  • Refeeding: Breaking the fast must be done carefully. Lauren recommends starting with water and electrolytes, followed by bone broth or lean, easily digestible proteins (like stewed beef). High fats should be avoided initially to prevent digestive distress, and carbohydrates should be avoided to prevent insulin spikes.
  • Recovery matching: Lauren emphasizes that the recovery phase must match the duration of the fast. If someone dry fasts for seven days, they must dedicate the following seven days to rest and rebuilding, as this is when stem cell regeneration and mitochondrial rebuilding actually occur.
