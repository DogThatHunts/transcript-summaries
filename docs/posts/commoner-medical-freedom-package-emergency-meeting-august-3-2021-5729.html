Overview of the common law approach to vaccine mandates

The Commoner Law Group held an emergency meeting on August 3, 2021, to present what it describes as a common law strategy for individuals facing employer COVID-19 vaccine mandates. The speaker argues that public and private mandates rely on intimidation and tacit consent, asserting that individuals can lawfully counter these demands without resorting to protests, unions, or formal exemption requests.

The foundational principles of common law

The presenter asserts that common law stems from natural rights rather than statutory regulations or government institutions. Under this framework, individuals are considered sovereign and personally responsible for asserting their rights.

  • Natural sovereignty: The speaker posits that rights are inherent rather than granted by governments, claiming that statutory codes often convert rights into privileges through contractual agreements.
  • Individual responsibility: The presentation emphasizes that individuals must assert their own rights rather than relying on lawyers, labor unions, or political representatives.
  • Simplicity of principles: The speaker characterizes common law as a simple set of ancient principles focused on avoiding harm to others, respecting property, honoring contracts, and avoiding community harm.

The San Francisco case study and four-step notice process

The speaker details a campaign involving employees of the City and County of San Francisco, who were directed by Human Resources Director Carol Eisen to report vaccination status and obtain COVID-19 injections within ten weeks of full approval by the Food and Drug Administration.

  • Avoiding tacit consent: Rather than submitting vaccination status forms, the group advised employees to submit conditional acceptances to avoid implicitly granting authority to employer mandates.
  • Targeting individuals in authority: The process directs formal legal notices to specific officials, such as Carol Eisen, in their personal capacities to hold them accountable under color of law claims.
  • Establishing a paper trail: The strategy relies on a four-step sequence of formal written communications delivered via registered mail or professional document servers.

Deconstructing the conditional acceptance document

The core strategy begins with a conditional acceptance letter. Instead of outright refusing a mandate, the employee formally accepts the employer's offer contingent upon the official proving several specific claims.

  • Requirement of proof: The letter demands proof that the mandate is not issued under coercion, that safety profiles are established without invalidated placebo groups, and that officials accept personal financial and physical liability.
  • Affidavit requirement: The official is given ten days to respond in affidavit form under full commercial liability, point by point.
  • Challenging regulatory authority: The document asks officials to prove how agencies like the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention or officials like Anthony Fauci obtained lawful jurisdiction over individual bodily sovereignty.

Step-by-step document escalation: Courtesy notice, default, and estoppel

If the targeted official fails to provide a point-by-point sworn affidavit, the process moves through three subsequent notices designed to establish legal default.

  • Courtesy notice: If the official provides a standard policy statement or remains silent, a three-day courtesy notice is issued clarifying that responses must be in affidavit form under oath.
  • Notice of default: If silence persists, a notice of default is served, asserting that the official's failure to respond constitutes tacit agreement with the claims and establishes prima facie evidence of silence.
  • Notice of estoppel by acquiescence: The final document informs the official that they are legally stopped from enforcing the policy or taking action against the employee, alleging that further enforcement strips them of official immunity and creates personal civil liability.

Why exemptions fail and individual responsibility matters

The speaker cautions against applying for medical or religious exemptions, arguing that asking for an exemption implicitly acknowledges the employer's authority to grant or deny bodily autonomy.

  • Pitfalls of exemption requests: The presenter claims that seeking exemptions places individuals in a subordinate position, whereas conditional acceptance asserts equal or superior standing.
  • Revoking prior agreements: For individuals who previously signed contracts or requested exemptions, the group recommends filing an affidavit of truth to revoke past consent based on a lack of informed understanding.
