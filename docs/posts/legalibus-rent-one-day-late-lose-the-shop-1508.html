How the legal principle of equity prevents landlords from using strict contract rules unconscionably, protecting tenants from devastating minor technicalities.

Strict legal contracts can sometimes lead to devastatingly unfair outcomes, such as losing a long-term business over a single day's delay in rent. While the letter of the law remains valid, the legal principle of equity steps in to address the conscience of the parties involved. Rather than rewriting the contract, equity restrains individuals from enforcing their strict rights when doing so would be unconscionable.

Key takeaways

  • Equity acts in personam: LegaliBus explains that equity acts against the person rather than deleting the legal rule itself, restraining them from acting unconscionably.
  • Discretionary, not automatic: Equitable relief is entirely at the court's discretion, meaning the behavior of the party seeking help matters significantly.
  • A high bar for intervention: Equity does not rescue individuals from bad luck or poor business decisions, but it steps in when enforcing strict rights offends conscience.
