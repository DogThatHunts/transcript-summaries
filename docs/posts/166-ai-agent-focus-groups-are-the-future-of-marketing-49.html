# 166 AI agent focus groups are the future of marketing

How advertising veteran Justin Brooke uses a 13-persona synthetic audience to critique and rewrite copy before spending a single dollar on ads.

In this episode, 20-year advertising veteran Justin Brooke joins the host to discuss the future of direct response marketing: AI-powered virtual focus groups. By running ad copy and sales pages through a "synthetic audience" of 13 highly detailed buyer personas, marketers can receive instant, brutally honest feedback and optimized rewrites for pennies per run. Brooke explains how this system—which mirrors methodologies proven by Harvard, Stanford, and the New York Times to be over 90% accurate—has generated over $260,000 for his business and consistently delivers a 4x to 6x return on ad spend (ROAS) for his clients.

Key insights

  1. Synthetic audiences rival human focus groups: Academic research from Harvard and Stanford, alongside practical applications by the New York Times, demonstrates that AI-driven virtual focus groups can achieve up to 92% accuracy compared to real human testing.
  2. Dossier depth dictates accuracy: Simply prompting an LLM to "pretend to be a customer" yields generic, low-quality feedback. To achieve high accuracy, each persona must be backed by a detailed dossier (around 1,400 words) containing demographics, pain points, and empathy maps.
  3. The power of "embody" over "pretend": Brooke notes that LLMs perform better when instructed to "embody" a role rather than "pretend" to be it, as "pretend" signals the model to generate superficial or fake personas.
  4. Parallel processing is essential for speed: Running multiple persona critiques sequentially causes severe latency. Grouping persona agents to run in parallel within workflow builders like MindStudio dramatically reduces execution times.
  5. Instant, low-cost iteration: Running a full ad critique costs roughly 13 cents, while a comprehensive sales page analysis costs about 20 cents, allowing marketers to endlessly refine copy before launching campaigns.

The mechanics of a virtual focus group

Brooke's system, built on MindStudio (though adaptable to N8N or Make), operates as a multi-step automated workflow:

  • User input: The marketer pastes their raw ad copy or uploads a PDF of a draft sales page.
  • Parallel critique: The copy is passed simultaneously to 13 distinct buyer personas representing different segments of the ideal client profile (ICP).
  • Copywriter synthesis: A dedicated copywriter agent compiles the feedback, highlights blind spots, and drafts three new variations based on proven direct response formulas.
  • Algorithmic scoring: A custom prediction engine scores each variation to identify the one with the highest statistical likelihood of converting.

This workflow shifts marketing from a reactive process—where budgets are wasted testing unproven copy on live audiences—to a predictive process, where copy is "washed" and optimized before a single ad dollar is spent.

Building high-fidelity buyer personas

To achieve the 92% accuracy benchmark, Brooke emphasizes that you cannot rely on simple, single-sentence prompts. Each of his 13 virtual focus group members is backed by a 1,400-word dossier containing:

  • Demographics and professional background: Specific careers, income levels, and business sizes (e.g., Sarah Chen, a struggling female e-commerce store owner, or Marcus, a struggling male SaaS founder).
  • Empathy mapping: Detailed breakdowns of what the persona is feeling, saying, hearing, and seeing daily, alongside their core pains and gains.
  • Varying success levels: Brooke deliberately includes both successful and struggling personas. This helps filter out bad-fit customers for advanced offers and ensures beginner offers resonate with the right crowd.

Prompt engineering for direct response copy

Brooke shares several key prompt engineering techniques that keep the AI output highly realistic and actionable:

  • Avoid "pretend to be": Instructing the model to "embody" a world-class copywriter or a specific prospect yields significantly deeper, more authentic responses.
  • Guardrails against AI tropes: LLMs tend to overdo voice instructions, sometimes making the copy sound like "a Gen Z or a hipster." Brooke uses explicit negative prompts to keep the tone professional and authoritative.
  • Actionable formatting: Instead of generating a dry executive summary, Brooke prompts the copywriter agent to format its response like an internal team email. This makes the output feel like a collaborative message from a real team member, complete with quoted feedback and direct copy-paste variations.

The prediction engine and scoring matrix

Once the copywriter agent generates three variations, Brooke uses a custom prediction engine to score them. Rather than relying on the LLM's default opinion of what "good" copy is, Brooke recommends designing a custom scoring matrix.

This matrix weighs different variables—such as alignment with the persona's pain points, adherence to copywriting best practices, and direct feedback metrics—to output a percentage score (e.g., "86% likelihood to convert"). Brooke suggests that investing a small amount of capital to have a math professor build a custom algorithm is the best way to dial in this predictive scoring for long-term accuracy.
