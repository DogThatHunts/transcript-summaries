Zillow slashes its housing market forecast

The massive disconnect between buyers and sellers

A central theme of the analysis is the profound financial disconnect between current homeowners and prospective buyers. RE-V illustrates this using a property in Nolensville, Tennessee:

  • The seller's perspective: The current owner purchased the home in 2019 for $503,000 with a 3.7% interest rate, resulting in a monthly mortgage payment of approximately $2,600. Because of this low payment, the seller feels little pressure to slash prices and could theoretically rent the home for cash flow.
  • The buyer's perspective: Listed at $799,000 (after a $75,000 price cut), a new buyer facing a 6.7% mortgage rate would have a monthly payment of $5,700. This requires an estimated household income of nearly $300,000, pricing out the vast majority of local buyers.

RE-V argues that this gap is keeping the housing market in a state of stagnation, as sellers cling to peak-market valuations while buyers simply cannot afford the payments.

Rising mortgage rates and the coming seller reality check

Mortgage rates have climbed back to 6.7% from a recent dip to 5.9%, driven by rising long-term bond yields and inflation concerns tied to energy prices and geopolitical tensions.

While RE-V does not expect these higher rates to depress demand further—given that demand is already near zero—he believes they will force sellers to confront reality. Currently, over 20% of active mortgage holders have interest rates above 6%, surpassing the share of holders with rates below 3%. As more neighbors with higher mortgage rates are forced to sell and cut prices, the remaining holdouts with low interest rates will eventually lose their pricing power.
