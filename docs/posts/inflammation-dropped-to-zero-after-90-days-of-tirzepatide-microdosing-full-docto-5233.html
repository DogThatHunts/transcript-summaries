Despite years of dietary and supplement efforts, key markers—particularly inflammation and cholesterol patterns—remained stagnant.

After a decade of treatment, Linda’s doctor began recommending a microdose of tirzepatide, a low-dose GLP-1 medication, to help restore her body’s natural balance. Initially hesitant due to concerns about dependency, she agreed to try it after three months of being on a strict, healthy diet with no significant weight loss.

After just 90 days of weekly microdosing, her lab results showed dramatic improvements.

Key Lab changes

  • HSCRP (High-Sensitivity C-Reactive Protein): A marker of silent, chronic inflammation. Linda’s level dropped from 0.7 to below 0.2—essentially undetectable—indicating near-complete resolution of vascular inflammation. This change is not visible in standard blood work and is typically overlooked by most doctors.

  • Apolipoprotein B (ApoB): A more accurate indicator of cardiovascular risk than total cholesterol. Linda’s ApoB decreased from 92 to 75, which is considered optimal and within the 'perfect' range of 75–80.

  • LDL Particle Count (LDL-P): This test measures the number and size of cholesterol particles. Linda’s LDL-P dropped from 1465 to 1145—a 320-point reduction—equivalent to 300 fewer harmful particles in her bloodstream.

  • LDL Buoyancy Pattern: A critical distinction between 'type A' and 'type B' LDL. Linda had long been in the 'type B' category, which is associated with a higher risk of plaque formation and cardiovascular disease. After the microdose, her pattern shifted to 'type A', indicating a significant reduction in cardiovascular risk.

  • Fasting Insulin: Prior to treatment, Linda’s fasting insulin was around 4, considered ideal. After the microdose, it dropped to 2.5. While low insulin is often seen as positive, a level below 2.5 is a red flag indicating possible malnutrition or under-eating. This finding revealed that Linda was consuming only about 800 calories per day—far below what is needed for metabolic health.

The drop in fasting insulin, while initially celebrated, revealed a deeper issue: Linda was under-eating. This condition, if left unaddressed, can lead to malnutrition, energy decline, hair loss, and metabolic slowdown over time.
