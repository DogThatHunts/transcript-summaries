Sethra | 5 Books That Reveal Why People Become Addicted to Approval

An exploration of five essential books that examine the psychological cost of chronic people-pleasing and how to reclaim your personal boundaries.

In this video, Sethra explores the psychological trap of chronic people-pleasing through the lens of five insightful books. They discuss how the compulsive need for approval shifts from a pleasant desire to an unhealthy necessity that erodes personal identity and boundaries. Ultimately, Sethra argues that overcoming this addiction is not about becoming selfish, but about learning to stand firm in yourself even when facing disapproval.

Key takeaways

  • People-pleasing vs. generosity: Harriet Braiker's The Disease to Please highlights that true generosity is a choice, whereas people-pleasing is a compulsive defense mechanism used to avoid rejection and anger.
  • The source of self-worth: Joyce Meyer's Approval Addiction challenges readers to consider who they allow to define their worth, urging a shift away from external validation.
  • Kindness is not compliance: In Not Nice, Aziz Gazipura distinguishes genuine kindness—which can say no and disappoint others—from submissive compliance.
  • The danger of self-policing: The Quiet Weights of Human Becoming by von Peiseth warns that repeatedly compromising your preferences leads to policing yourself before anyone else even has to.
  • The true opposite of people-pleasing: Referencing Sue Patton Thoele's The Courage to Be Yourself, Sethra notes that the alternative to people-pleasing is not selfishness, but the capacity to hear disapproval without abandoning your own identity.
