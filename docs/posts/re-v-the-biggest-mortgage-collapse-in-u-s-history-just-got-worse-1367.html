The affordability crisis and demand collapse

RE-V argues that the housing market's fundamental problem is not supply but price. Mortgage payments have become economically unsustainable for the median buyer. With payments at 37% of gross income—matching 2006 levels—and renting now 30–40% cheaper than buying in many markets, the rational choice for buyers is to wait or rent indefinitely.

The speaker emphasizes that seven Federal Reserve rate cuts (from 5.5% to 3.7% since August 2024) have failed to stimulate demand, nor has the AI boom or record stock market levels. The issue is that prices remain too high in inflation-adjusted terms. Until prices fall substantially, buyers will remain on what RE-V calls "the biggest strike from the housing market in U.S. history."

Seller resistance and the coming forced sales

Many sellers still believe they have leverage, listing homes at inflated prices and delisting when they don't sell quickly. However, RE-V argues this strategy is doomed. Rising property taxes, insurance costs, and HOA fees will force millions of current mortgage holders—many with rates above 6% and payments consuming 35–40% of income—to sell over the next 1–2 years. These owners will lack the luxury of holding out for higher prices; they will be forced to sell at market-clearing prices.

The speaker cites examples of major price cuts already occurring: an Ocala, Florida home that sold as a new build for $364,000 in July 2022 sold for $224,000 in August 2025—a 40% loss. While such declines are not yet universal, they signal the beginning of a broader correction.

The migration factor

The Sunbelt's correction is partly explained by the collapse in inbound migration: Florida's net migration fell 90% from 310,000 (2022) to 23,000 (2025); Arizona's migration is near a 35-year low at 0.4% of population growth. Meanwhile, the Northeast continues losing residents, which will eventually pressure prices despite current appreciation.
