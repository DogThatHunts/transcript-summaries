The law of cause and effect

The first principle centers on understanding that nothing is random or luck-driven. SecretWithin quotes Hall from Chapter 3 ("The Human Body and Symbolism"):

"The immutability of the law was stressed, that every thought and act must react according to the nature of the energy liberated thereby. This reaction was symbolized by the circle, the symbol of infinite action."

SecretWithin interprets this to mean that life operates in cycles—civilizations rise and fall, currencies collapse, and even ancient civilizations may have been more advanced than previously believed. He references erosion marks on the pyramids (allegedly dating them to 14,000–15,000 years ago rather than the conventional 4,500 years) and links this to the Younger Dryas flood event, arguing that cyclical patterns govern all existence.

The concept of "infinite action" is central: SecretWithin argues that mental, spiritual, and physical action are not separate categories but all forms of energy transfer. Most people mistakenly believe meditation and mindset differ from physical effort, but successful people recognize that all three must work in concert. "There is no luck," he asserts; what appears random is simply the result of hidden causes we fail to trace.

The key distinction SecretWithin draws is between reasoning by analogy (comparing oneself to others, following trends, seeking permission) and reasoning by first principles. Successful people, he claims, break arguments down to their fundamental truths, asking "why" repeatedly until reaching the root cause. This shift from analogy to first principles is what transforms someone from a passive observer into a "creator of your reality." Authenticity and uniqueness matter: copying another's path violates the law of cause and effect, because you are not addressing the underlying causes specific to your situation.

The law of spirit

The second principle concerns the hierarchical nature of existence. SecretWithin cites Hall from Chapter 12 ("Pythagorean Mathematics"):

"The universe is made up of successive gradations of good. These gradations ascend from matter, which is the least degree of good, to spirit, which is the greatest degree of good. In man, the three spheres of his threefold nature merge into a single unit, making him a single living soul."

SecretWithin connects this to Hermetic philosophy, specifically Hermes Trismegistus (meaning "thrice greatest"), who allegedly mastered three planes of existence: physical, mental, and spiritual. He also draws parallels to ancient yogic philosophy, which posits that the universe evolved from nothingness → energy → awareness → consciousness → mind → body.

The practical implication is that the physical world is merely the grossest manifestation of subtler dimensions. SecretWithin cites placebo effects and the sophistication of the human body (which quantum computers cannot yet replicate) as evidence that mind governs matter. To create change in physical reality, one must not focus on manipulating the gross material world but instead on the subtle source from which effects originate. "The subtle inevitably leads to the gross," he argues, meaning that all lasting physical change begins in the mind.

The law of imagination

The third and final principle elevates imagination as the supreme creative force. SecretWithin quotes Hall from Chapter 25 ("The Pythagorean Theory of Music and Color"):

SecretWithin interprets "star in man" and "celestial body" as references to the subtlest dimension of human existence. He cites Einstein's assertion that "imagination is the only thing that truly governs this world" and references Neville Goddard, a 19th-century philosopher who emphasized imagination and awareness as the most powerful forces available to humans.

However, SecretWithin makes a critical distinction: passive visualization (merely picturing something) differs fundamentally from creative visualization. The latter involves feeling—what Goddard calls "thinking feelingly." When mind and heart align in a single congruent line, the probability of manifesting a desired outcome increases dramatically. SecretWithin illustrates this with examples: wanting a job while genuinely feeling the associated emotions of having it, or imagining business growth while embodying the feelings of success.
