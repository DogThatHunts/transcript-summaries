The doctrine of missing parts

Dave Champion introduces what he calls the "doctrine of missing parts," a phenomenon where organizations enforce rules or laws that they cannot physically produce when requested. He argues that this practice is highly prevalent in American payroll withholding, where employers routinely deduct taxes from paychecks based on a misunderstanding of federal tax law.

Redefining employee and employer in tax law

Champion examines the statutory language of the Internal Revenue Code (IRC), specifically focusing on 26 U.S.C. § 3401 and § 3402. He asserts that Congress routinely redefines common English words to have highly specific, non-standard legal meanings:

  • Wages: Defined in § 3401(a) as remuneration for services performed by an "employee" for his "employer."
  • Employee: Defined in § 3401(c) as including officers, employees, or elected officials of the United States, a federal territory (such as Puerto Rico or Washington, D.C.), or any agency or instrumentality thereof.
  • Employer: Defined in § 3401(d) as the person or entity for whom an individual performs services as an "employee."

Based on these definitions, Champion argues that the statutory definition of "employee" excludes private-sector workers in the states of the Union, meaning private-sector firms have no legal authority under these sections to withhold taxes from their workers' paychecks.

The statutory mechanism of wage withholding

According to Champion, 26 U.S.C. § 3402 does not actually impose a tax; rather, it acts as a command to collect a tax imposed elsewhere in the code. He claims that a historical review of Treasury Decisions (such as TD 8734 and TD 8881) and IRS Cumulative Bulletins reveals that wage withholding under § 3402 is legally designed to apply exclusively to non-resident aliens receiving U.S.-source income. He asserts that in the history of the modern federal income tax, there are no authoritative Treasury or IRS documents instructing businesses to withhold wages from domestic American citizens earning domestic income.
