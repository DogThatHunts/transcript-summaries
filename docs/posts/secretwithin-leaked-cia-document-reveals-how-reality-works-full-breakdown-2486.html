The Gateway Process: a declassified CIA document on consciousness and reality

SecretWithin presents a breakdown of the 1983 "Analysis and Assessment of Gateway Process," a classified U.S. Army Intelligence report by Lieutenant Colonel Wayne McDonnell. The report documents a training system designed to enable out-of-body experiences and, according to McDonnell's analysis, access to alternate dimensions and states of consciousness beyond physical reality.

The origins of the Gateway Process

The Gateway Process was developed in the 1970s by Robert Monroe, a New York broadcasting executive who discovered that specific sound frequencies could alter human consciousness. While experimenting with sleep-learning techniques in the 1950s, Monroe experienced an unexpected out-of-body experience (OBE) triggered by a particular sound frequency. He subsequently replicated this experience nine times over six weeks and went on to found the Monroe Institute in Virginia. Monroe popularized the term "OBE" and became a prominent researcher in human consciousness studies.

The scientific framework

McDonnell grounds his analysis in established physics and neuroscience. He consults biomedical engineer Itzhak Bentov and applies quantum mechanics, classical physics, and theoretical physics to explain the phenomenon. The report argues that:

  • Reality as energy: The universe is not composed of solid matter but rather oscillating energy grids vibrating at extraordinarily high frequencies. All matter, including the human brain and consciousness, is fundamentally energy.

  • Consciousness as a tuning mechanism: Human consciousness functions like a radio receiver, tuned to perceive only a narrow band of the energy frequencies that exist in the universe. The brain filters out most available energy, allowing us to experience a coherent reality.

  • The holographic universe: Drawing on theories by neuroscientist Karl Pribram and physicist David Bohm, McDonnell argues that both the universe and the human mind are holograms. The mind attunes itself to the universal hologram to achieve consciousness. Holograms, he notes, can encode such detail that a holographic projection of microscopic organisms would reveal actual organisms under magnification.

How the Gateway Process works

The technique employs two primary methods:

Frequency Following Response (FFR): The brain attempts to mimic frequencies introduced through headphones by adjusting its own brainwave patterns.

Binaural beats: Different frequencies are played in each ear; the brain detects and processes the difference between them. For example, 100 Hz in one ear and 104 Hz in the other produces a perceived 4 Hz frequency.

Hemi-sync synchronization: The process synchronizes the left and right brain hemispheres. The left hemisphere (logic, reason, focus) typically operates in the beta frequency range (13–30 Hz), while the right hemisphere (emotion, creativity, intuition) operates in the alpha range (8–13 Hz). By bringing both hemispheres into coherence, the process is described as converting the mind from a chaotic "lamp" into a focused "laser."

The mechanics of "clicking out"

McDonnell proposes that as brainwave frequencies accelerate through the Gateway Process, consciousness can temporarily exit normal space-time. He references the Planck distance (1.6 × 10⁻³⁵ meters), the smallest meaningful unit of length in physics. When brainwave oscillations drop below the Planck distance, McDonnell argues, consciousness "clicks out of time-space and joins infinity"—a state he calls the "Absolute."

According to the report, consciousness passes through intervening dimensions between the material universe and the Absolute. The Absolute itself is described as conscious, infinite energy without boundaries, occupying every dimension simultaneously but remaining imperceptible to ordinary awareness.

The Absolute and spiritual implications

McDonnell identifies the Absolute with concepts found across religious and mystical traditions:

  • Hebrew mysticism: The Absolute at rest in infinity parallels Kabbalistic philosophy.
  • Christian theology: The description echoes the concept of the Trinity.
  • Eastern religions: McDonnell cites Tibetan teachings and ancient Hindu texts as intuitive descriptions of the same reality.

He argues that the Absolute is the conscious source of all creation and that individual human consciousness is a projection of this universal consciousness. Upon death, individual consciousness returns to the Absolute while retaining individual identity within a collective consciousness—a concept McDonnell frames as a form of immortality.

Military applications and intelligence gathering

The U.S. military's interest in the Gateway Process centered on its potential for remote viewing and intelligence gathering. If soldiers could project their consciousness anywhere undetected, they could gather intelligence without physical presence. McDonnell recommends a phased military approach: first, training personnel to synchronize their brains using hemi-sync; second, learning to induce the state at will; third, achieving out-of-body experiences for remote viewing; and finally, developing verification methods for the accuracy of information gathered.

However, McDonnell also warned that because the Gateway Process is accessible to anyone, the military should prepare for encounters with "intelligent and non-friendly entities" existing outside the boundaries of time and space—a scenario he termed "psychic war."

The missing page controversy

The original Gateway Report was 30 pages long. When the document was declassified and circulated, page 25 was missing. For years, the CIA claimed it did not possess the missing page, despite numerous FOIA requests and petitions. In 2021, after Vice published an article highlighting the controversy, the Monroe Institute released the complete report, including page 25. According to SecretWithin, page 25 contains McDonnell's conclusion that the Gateway Process could be a universal tool for self-understanding and spiritual development, applicable across all religions and cultures.

Risks and reported outcomes

SecretWithin notes that while many Gateway Process practitioners report enhanced self-awareness, creativity, and mental clarity, some have experienced intense anxiety, depression, and in rare cases, psychosis. The process can create altered states of consciousness that may cause practitioners to become detached or "untethered" from ordinary reality, potentially unable to return.

The broader implications

McDonnell's report attempts to reconcile science and spirituality, suggesting that the Gateway Process could provide empirical evidence for concepts traditionally associated with faith—God, the afterlife, and the nature of consciousness. SecretWithin concludes by posing the central question: if science and spirituality are describing the same underlying reality, what does that mean for our understanding of existence?
