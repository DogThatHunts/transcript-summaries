Claude Agrees With Everything. This Skill Fixes It.

How to overcome AI confirmation bias by using a multi-perspective prompting framework that simulates a council of diverse thinkers.

AI models like Claude often act as "yes men," agreeing with whatever premise you feed them. To bypass this confirmation bias, researchers use an "LLM Council" to evaluate ideas from multiple angles. This simplified single-model version prompts five distinct personalities before a neutral judge synthesizes the final, objective decision.

Key takeaways

  • The "yes man" problem: Large language models will readily build a perfect case for why an idea is either brilliant or terrible depending entirely on how you frame the question.
  • The LLM Council: Originally conceived by AI researcher Andrej Karpathy, this approach queries multiple different models (such as Google, ChatGPT, Claude, and Grok) to get a balanced perspective.
  • A simplified single-model alternative: Instead of querying different models, you can prompt a single model to act as five distinct personas, including:
  • The steel man: Builds the strongest possible version of your idea.
  • The critic: Tries to break the idea down and prove why it is wrong.
  • The first principles thinker: Ignores conventional wisdom to analyze the idea from the ground up.
  • The neutral judge: A final agent that synthesizes all five perspectives into an objective final verdict.
