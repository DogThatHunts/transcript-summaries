How you can talk with God and get an answer: Yogananda's method

Overview

Paramahansa Yogananda presents a systematic approach to establishing direct dialogue with the Divine, drawing on biblical examples, Hindu scripture, and his own spiritual experiences. He argues that communication with God is not merely possible but a definite fact—one that requires specific conditions, sustained intention, and a fundamental shift in how we conceive of and relate to the Divine.

God is both personal and impersonal

Yogananda addresses a common philosophical objection: that God is too abstract or impersonal to respond to individual prayers. He argues that this view is contradictory. If God created personal beings—humans with individuality, thought, feeling, and will—then the Creator cannot be wholly impersonal. An impersonal force would lack the reciprocity that characterizes all personal relationships.

He contends that God can and will establish a personal relationship with each person who permits it. The impersonal conception of God leaves an impression of a remote being who receives prayers without responding—"a heartless silence." But this, Yogananda argues, is a philosophical error. God is everything: personal and impersonal. Many saints have heard God's voice; there is no reason why others cannot.

Man is made in the image of God

Yogananda interprets the biblical statement "in the image of God made he man" to mean that humans share God's essential attributes. God is spirit; man's essential nature is also spirit. But the resemblance goes further. The human body and consciousness form a microcosmic representation of God.

Through consciousness, humans possess omniscience and omnipresence in thought—one can immediately imagine being at the North Star or on Mars. Consciousness is aware of itself; God's cosmic consciousness is aware of itself in every atom of creation. Humans also possess will, reason, feeling, and love—all attributes of the Creator. In these qualities, man may be said to be made in the image of God, and therefore capable of communion with Him.

God talks through vibration

Yogananda introduces a central mechanism for divine communication: vibration. God is consciousness and energy. When God speaks, He vibrates. His cosmic energy continuously manifests as sound and takes form in the material world—solids, liquids, fire, air, and ether are all particular rates of vibration of God's cosmic energy.

The Lord is constantly talking to us through these vibrations, but most people cannot hear because "our intuitive telephonic system is out of order." God does not need a throat to speak. If one prays strongly enough, the prayer vibrations bring an immediate vibratory response, manifesting in whatever language the person is accustomed to hearing. God, being cosmic vibration, knows all languages.

Yogananda claims direct experience of this: he hears God's voice as cosmic sound (Aum), which transforms into intelligible language—English or Bengali—giving him precise instructions.

The conditions for receiving God's response

Yogananda identifies several prerequisites for God to answer:

Sincere, whole-hearted devotion. The average person prays with the mind only, not with the fervor of the heart. Such prayers are too weak to bring response. One must speak to the Divine Spirit with confidence and the feeling of closeness to a father or mother. God is moved only by love.

A definite conception of God. Without a clear conception—such as the Divine Mother—one does not receive a clear response. This specificity of intention matters.

Unconditional love and demand. The relationship with God should be one of unconditional love. More than in any other relationship, one may rightfully demand a reply from Spirit in its aspect as the Divine Mother. God is constrained to answer such an appeal because the essence of a mother is love and forgiveness.

Unshakeable faith and persistence. A half-believing prayer is insufficient. One must make up one's mind: "He is going to talk with me." Regardless of how many years God has not answered, if one continues trusting, one day He will respond. Most people don't receive a response because of their disbelief.

Intense, unceasing zeal. God will not respond to casual or mechanical prayer. The Hindu scriptures teach that if a devotee talks to God with intense devotion for one night and one day without interruption, He will respond. Yogananda emphasizes that few will do this because of daily engagements and distractions.

Renunciation of attachment to God's gifts. God tests devotees to ensure they want Him, not His gifts. He will not reveal Himself if the heart is filled only with longings for His gifts. Man's love is his sole gift to God. One must be like a naughty baby who cries until the mother comes—refusing to be comforted by anything except the mother's presence.

Biblical and scriptural support

Yogananda cites numerous scriptural passages to establish that God-human dialogue is documented throughout religious tradition:

  • Solomon's dream (1 Kings 3:5–13): God appears to Solomon and invites him to ask for anything. Solomon requests understanding, and God grants it along with riches and honor he did not ask for.
  • David's inquiries (referenced from 1 Samuel): David asks God whether to go up against the Philistines, and God answers affirmatively.
  • Jeremiah 29:13: "And ye shall seek me and find me, when ye shall search for me with all your heart."
  • 2 Chronicles 15:2: "The Lord is with you while ye be with him, and if ye seek him he will be found of you."
  • Revelation 3:20: "Behold, I stand at the door and knock. If any man hear my voice and open the door, I will come into him."

Yogananda also references Jesus's statement "I and my father are one" and Swami Shankara's declaration "I am spirit and thou art that" to support the idea that all humans are made in the image of divinity.

Yogananda's personal experience

Yogananda recounts his first experience of hearing the divine voice as a child sitting on his bed. A thought came powerfully: "What is behind the darkness of closed eyes?" An immense flash of light manifested in his inner gaze, revealing divine shapes of saints sitting in meditation in mountain caves, like miniature cinema pictures on a screen of radiance within his forehead.

When he asked aloud "Who are you?" a celestial response came: "We are the Himalayan yogis." His heart thrilled. The vision vanished, but silvery beams expanded in ever-widening circles. He then heard a voice like murmuring clouds: "I am Ishwara, Lord. I am light." His mother and eldest sister, Roma, were nearby and also heard the divine voice. This experience filled him with such happiness that he determined to search for God until becoming wholly one with Him.

Yogananda teaches that as one develops spiritually and concentrates on the single eye in the forehead, inner sight opens. One beholds another world of many lights in great beauty. Visions of saints appear. With deeper concentration, one hears the voice of God again and again.

The nature of God's love and the purpose of creation

Yogananda presents a striking view of God's motivation. God yearns for human love—it is the one thing God does not possess unless humans choose to bestow it. The whole creation was designed as a test for humanity. By our conduct in this world, we disclose whether we want the Lord or His gifts.

God will not command us to desire Him above all else because He wants our love to be freely given, without prompting. "That is the whole secret in the game of this universe." As long as humans are wayward children, "pygmies crawling on this ball of earth and crying for his gifts while we ignore him, the giver," they fall into many pits of misery.

Because God is the essence of our own being, humans cannot truly express themselves until they learn to manifest His presence within them. It is because we are divine, a part of Him, that we cannot find lasting satisfaction in anything material.

The process of receiving God's response

Yogananda outlines a progression in how God responds to devoted seekers:

  1. Initial acknowledgment. God first responds by giving something the devotee has wanted, showing that the devotee is in His attention.

  2. Testing the sincerity. But the devotee must not be content with His gifts. One must let Him know that satisfaction will never come until one has Him.

  3. The vision or voice. Finally, God gives an answer in a vision. The devotee may see the face of a saintly being or hear a divine voice talking directly. This is communion with God.

  4. Ongoing relationship. Once this contact is established, the devotee will never feel separated from God again. The divine experience remains permanently.

However, Yogananda acknowledges that achieving this "once" is difficult because the heart and mind are not convinced, and doubt creeps in due to materialistic beliefs.

God's accessibility and the role of will

Yogananda emphasizes that God will answer every human being, irrespective of caste, creed, or color. He cites a Bengali saying: if you give a soul call to God as the universal mother, she cannot remain silent—she has to speak.

He stresses the power of will and determination. If one is absolutely determined to attain something, nothing can stop you. It is when one gives up that one writes the verdict against oneself. The man of success doesn't know the word "impossible."

Faith is described as "the limitless power of God within you." When attuned to God's consciousness within, humans can create worlds. In one's will lies the almighty power of God. When difficulties come and one refuses to give up, when the mind becomes set, God responds.

Practical guidance

Yogananda offers concrete advice for those seeking to communicate with God:

  • Speak to God constantly. Tell Him any thought that comes into your mind.
  • Say to Him: "Lord, reveal thyself, reveal thyself. Don't take silence for an answer."
  • Work for God while speaking to Him constantly.
  • Remove from your mind all doubt that God will answer. Most people don't get a response because of disbelief.
  • Never again doubt that God will respond if you are constant and persistent in your demands.
  • Accept no excuse from the world or the body. When your heart call is intense, He will come.
