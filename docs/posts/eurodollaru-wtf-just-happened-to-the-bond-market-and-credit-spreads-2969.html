Bond market chaos: credit spreads, September effects, and the repo connection

EurodollarU examines three interconnected market disturbances that converged in late September 2026, each with implications for equity investors and the broader economy.

Credit spreads flash yellow

Junk-rated credit spreads (CCC-rated bonds) have exploded to around 1,170 basis points—above the August 2024 carry-trade spike and well above the tariff-disorder highs of early 2025. This represents not a one-time volatile spike but a credit cycle deterioration that has been building for over a year, since the Tricolor and First Brands failures in September 2024.

EurodollarU emphasizes this is a "flashing yellow" warning, not yet red. The next critical level to watch is 1,300 basis points; historically, 1,500 basis points signals serious systemic stress. The widening is driven partly by rising interest rates (which increase refinancing costs), but more fundamentally by a deteriorating credit cycle. Real-world evidence includes 16 trucking companies filing for bankruptcy in less than a month due to elevated diesel prices—a harbinger of broader stress in weaker credits facing higher rates and an energy shock.

Notably, investment-grade spreads have remained largely stable until recently, creating an unusual divergence. However, investment-grade spreads are now beginning to participate in the latest sell-off, which EurodollarU identifies as a potential turning point. If credit stress spreads from junk to investment-grade bonds, it signals the risk aversion is broadening—a development that could force the stock market to pay attention.

The vix-credit spread disconnect

Historically, credit spreads and the VIX move together: when spreads widen, equity volatility rises. Yet for over a year, the stock market has largely ignored the credit cycle deterioration, focused instead on AI and central bank reassurances about economic resilience. EurodollarU argues the stock market has been "preoccupied" with AI's potential capex boom while the credit market reflects a collision of negative forces: a fragile macroeconomy, an energy shock, and a deteriorating credit cycle.

The tighter correlation between the VIX and investment-grade spreads (versus junk spreads) reflects passive investing flows: mechanical stock buying pushes flows into bonds, and bond managers have increasingly crowded into investment-grade credit while avoiding risky segments. If investment-grade spreads begin to widen meaningfully, this correlation could snap the stock market to attention.

The September cubed effect: bottleneck plus calendar plus repo

EurodollarU identifies two distinct September effects in bond markets:

The September bottleneck: A recurring low point in the seasonal liquidity calendar around September 15th, historically associated with major market crises (LTCM in 1998, Lehman in 2008, the 2019 repo blowup). This bottleneck appears every year but only creates major problems when other stresses align.

The September effect: A persistent pattern since at least 2017–2018 in which long-term Treasury yields tend to rise between August and October, visible globally across US Treasuries, JGBs, German bonds, and other government debt. EurodollarU has spent over a decade trying to explain this phenomenon and suspects it relates to a global dollar/euro dollar factor, possibly involving bank regulations or collateral flows, but remains uncertain.

The September cubed effect: When these two effects combine, they create an accelerating sell-off in long-term Treasuries in the second half of September. EurodollarU traces this pattern through 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, and 2026, showing consistent post-bottleneck acceleration in Treasury yields.

The mechanism likely involves the basis trade—a leveraged arbitrage between Treasury futures and cash bonds that has become a marginal source of buying in the Treasury market. Basis traders rely on repo financing. When repo liquidity deteriorates around the September bottleneck, basis traders are either forced to sell Treasuries or unable to buy them as usual, creating a supply shock that drives yields higher.

Repo fails spiked to ~$320 billion in mid-September 2026, consistent with the bottleneck pattern. This signals impaired collateral flow, which—in the euro dollar system—means impaired cash flow. EurodollarU argues this technical disruption, layered atop the September calendar effect, explains much of the sharp Treasury sell-off in late September.

Key takeaways

  1. Credit spreads are widening on a credit cycle basis, not just rate sensitivity, and are now pulling investment-grade spreads higher—a sign of broadening risk aversion.

  2. The stock market has decoupled from credit stress due to AI enthusiasm and central bank messaging, but if credit spreads continue to widen into investment-grade territory, equities will likely face pressure.

  3. September's technical disruptions—bottleneck, calendar effect, and repo stress—may explain a significant portion of the Treasury yield surge, independent of fundamental economic or inflation expectations.

  4. Now that October has arrived, the September effects should fade. If Treasury yields stabilize and credit spreads stabilize, it would suggest much of the recent move was technical. If they continue to widen, it signals genuine credit cycle deterioration.

  5. The collision of AI optimism, energy shock, macroeconomic fragility, and credit cycle stress remains unresolved. The stock market's focus on AI may be misplaced if credit stress accelerates and demand destruction from high energy costs takes hold.
