mRNA and new cancers

A conference presentation argues that mRNA vaccines have triggered a surge in aggressive cancers and calls for a royal commission into Australia's COVID response.

Overview

This is a conference presentation from Sydney, Australia, calling for a Royal Commission into COVID-19 management and vaccine safety. The speakers argue that mRNA vaccines have caused serious harms, particularly a phenomenon they term "turbo cancers"—unusually aggressive malignancies appearing at advanced stages in younger patients. The presentation combines technical claims about vaccine mechanisms with legal and regulatory critiques of Australia's pandemic response.

Key insights

  1. Claimed link between mRNA vaccines and cancer: Emeritus Professor Angus Dalgleish argues the connection is "vigorously denied" by authorities but supported by substantial evidence, including case reports of rapid cancer progression following booster doses.

  2. Immune suppression mechanism: Dalgleish contends that booster vaccines reduce T-cell response while producing antibodies against mutated virus variants, potentially compromising the body's natural cancer surveillance.

  3. Viral integration into human DNA: Dalgleish claims mRNA sequences can integrate into the human genome near oncogenes (cancer-promoting genes) or tumor suppressor genes, potentially activating or silencing them unpredictably.

  4. Regulatory and legal failures: Dr. Melissa McCann argues Australia's COVID vaccine program violated established pandemic protocols, lacked proper legislative authority, and bypassed standard pharmaceutical safety review processes (PBAC).

  5. Suppression of dissent: McCann describes doctors being warned, re-educated, and restricted from discussing vaccine side effects, framing this as an unprecedented departure from scientific freedom.

Emeritus Professor Angus Dalgleish's presentation

Dalgleish opens by stating he will present evidence for a "very strong link" between mRNA vaccines and cancer. He claims the SARS-CoV-2 virus was "basically manufactured," noting a positive charge on viral inserts that allows binding to multiple receptors beyond ACE2. He highlights that 79% of the spike protein shares homology with human proteins, particularly platelet factor 4 and myelin—similarities he argues are listed as rare vaccine side effects on the UK MHRA website.

Dalgleish reports observing melanoma patients who had been stable for years suddenly relapsing after booster vaccines, despite his advice to avoid them. He attributes this to T-cell suppression, citing research showing boosters elevate antibodies against outdated viral variants while reducing T-cell activation. He states this research was published in the BMJ but subsequently "disappeared."

He describes a phenomenon he calls "turbo cancers"—cancers presenting at stage four instead of stage one, with multiple primary sites, rapid progression, poor treatment response, and occurring in younger patients. He notes that surgeon James Royal reported seeing cancers that would have been curable a year ago now presenting as late-stage disease.

Dalgleish presents a case of a doctor with lymphoma whose PET scan showed dramatic lymph node swelling within days of a booster vaccine (September 8 to September 30). He also mentions a recent report of a heart tumor containing vaccine sequence after booster vaccination—a finding he describes as "really quite incredible" given the rarity of heart tumors.

On mechanisms, Dalgleish argues that mRNA vaccines function as "vax genes" that insert randomly into the genome. He contends they can activate oncogenes or silence tumor suppressor genes, creating what he calls "astronomical" potential for new cancers. He concludes: "The evidence is so overwhelming you would have to be completely mad, incompetent or negligent not to stop this immediately."

Dr. Melissa McCann's presentation

McCann frames the Royal Commission call around Australia's departure from established pandemic guidelines and legislative safeguards. She notes that Australia had a pre-existing pandemic plan (adopted for COVID in February 2020) that anticipated managing the pandemic without vaccines, relying instead on hygiene, PPE, and protection of at-risk groups.

McCann argues that proportionality, individual liberty, and minimization of social disruption—core principles of the pandemic plan—were abandoned in favor of prolonged lockdowns, curfews, vaccine mandates, and restrictions on healthy people. She describes unprecedented regulatory oversight of doctors, including restrictions on prescribing and warnings against expressing opinions contrary to government vaccination policy. She personally experienced this after speaking publicly about vaccine side effects, receiving a caution and being required to undergo "re-education."

On mandates, McCann argues that while most were enacted through state and territory powers, the Commonwealth government announced mandatory vaccination for aged care workers following National Cabinet agreement. She contends that describing this as "not mandating" is an "extraordinary narrow description" of what occurred. She also notes that Australians were told the virus would "hunt down the unvaccinated" and were encouraged to pressure reluctant family members.

McCann highlights a financial arrangement where Commonwealth funding to states and territories was tied to vaccine doses delivered—combining "financial incentives and public health coercion" in what she calls an "unacceptable combination."

On legislative authority, McCann argues the COVID vaccine program sat outside Australia's standard statutory framework for government-funded vaccines (section 9B of the National Health Act, which requires PBAC review). Instead, Parliament enacted section 132G in September 2021, authorizing the minister to provide COVID vaccines retrospectively. This power expired June 30, 2022, raising questions about the legal basis for Commonwealth expenditure thereafter.

McCann notes that five years later, COVID vaccines are transitioning to the National Immunization Program and were finally reviewed by PBAC in May 2026. The PBAC recommendation targets only elderly, at-risk, and immunocompromised populations—a risk-stratified approach McCann argues should have governed the program from the outset.

She presents three cases of vaccine injury: Reece, a physiotherapist in his 20s who developed end-stage heart failure and myocarditis after vaccination; Caitlin, a healthy 23-year-old who developed myocarditis and died suddenly at work within weeks; and Tony, a healthy man in his 40s who developed debilitating fatigue and cognitive dysfunction. McCann argues these individuals would not have been vaccinated under a properly risk-stratified program.

McCann draws a parallel to thalidomide, noting that Australia's modern regulatory system was created after that tragedy. She argues the TGA should have applied the same precautionary principle that led Frances Kelsey at the FDA to refuse thalidomide approval—insufficient evidence of safety and concerning pre-approval animal data, "precisely the same as could be said of the COVID vaccines."

McCann details safety signals known by regulators: deaths among frail elderly by January 2021, serious and fatal clotting syndromes by March 2021 following AstraZeneca vaccination, and clinical trial data containing confirmed deaths from myocarditis and cardiac arrests in children. Yet she argues doctors and patients were frequently reassured that events not accepted by the TGA were "simply not a vaccine side effect."

She concludes by asking whether the government acted "reasonably, proportionately, and consistently with its duty to protect the health and rights of Australians," or whether the response "demands the most comprehensive public inquiry that Australia can undertake."

Contested claims and gaps

The presentation makes several claims that are presented as established fact but remain contested in mainstream medical and regulatory literature:

  • Dalgleish's assertion that mRNA sequences integrate into human DNA is disputed by regulatory agencies, which maintain that mRNA is degraded and does not integrate into the genome.
  • The characterization of observed cancer patterns as causally linked to vaccination is presented as evidence but lacks the epidemiological controls and peer-reviewed publication that would establish causation.
  • McCann's legal arguments about legislative authority and regulatory process are specific to Australian law and would require legal expertise to evaluate.
