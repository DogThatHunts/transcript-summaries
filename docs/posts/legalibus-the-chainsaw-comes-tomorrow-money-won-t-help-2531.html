When a 400-year-old oak faces the chainsaw tomorrow, money alone won't save it—but equity law's arsenal of injunctions, specific performance, and restitution can.

Equity law offers remedies that common law cannot: when irreplaceable things are at stake, courts can stop harm before it happens, force performance of promises, or undo deals entirely.

Key takeaways:

  • Injunctions halt actions before they occur—stopping the chainsaw tonight, not compensating for the felled tree tomorrow.
  • Specific performance compels someone to keep their promise rather than merely pay damages for breaking it.
  • Rescission voids a deal as if it never happened; rectification corrects documents that misstate the bargain.
  • Account of profits shifts focus from the plaintiff's loss to the defendant's gain; tracing follows property through successive transfers.
  • Common law remedies are monetary; equity's tools are preventive, restorative, and restitutionary—designed for things money cannot replace.
