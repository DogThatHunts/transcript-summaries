Kratom, 7-OH, and addiction

GLP-1 peptides and body composition

DannyJones and Bell discuss Ozempic, Retratrutide (a triple agonist), and the emerging WVE-007. Key concerns:

  • Muscle loss is the hidden cost. GLP-1 drugs suppress appetite but don't preserve muscle. Someone losing 50 lbs might lose 6 lbs of muscle, reducing metabolic rate and glucose regulation. Upon discontinuation, rebound weight gain is often catastrophic.
  • Retratrutide (triple agonist) targets insulin resistance, appetite, and glucagon (fat loss), offering more comprehensive action than Ozempic alone.
  • WVE-007 (the "James Bond peptide") shows promise: in 85 days, patients gained 3.2% muscle mass while losing 9.4% visceral fat and 4.5% total body fat—a recomposition rather than simple weight loss. Dosing is once or twice yearly.
  • The biological tax: Both speakers acknowledge that all drugs work only while you take them, and long-term effects of these peptides remain unknown.

Bell emphasizes that obese individuals are often protein-deficient and micronutrient-deficient, not just calorie-surplus. Fixing food choices (lean meats, whole foods) is step one; drugs are step two.

Testosterone, steroids, and long-term use

  • Arnold Schwarzenegger (age 78) and Sylvester Stallone (age 80) abused steroids heavily in their youth and remain alive, though Arnold had heart issues. This suggests extreme steroid use doesn't necessarily shorten lifespan dramatically, though the sample size is small.

Bell argues for opening access to performance-enhancing drugs under medical supervision, reasoning that if peptide companies can prescribe unapproved compounds, why not allow doctors to prescribe Trenbolone with informed consent?
