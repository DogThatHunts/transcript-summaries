An analysis of real estate data reveals the metropolitan areas facing the steepest housing market corrections in 2026 due to surging inventory and plummeting demand.

The shifting real estate landscape

According to data from ReVenture, the U.S. housing market is experiencing highly localized corrections. While the Midwest and Northeast continue to see upward price trends, several major metropolitan areas are facing significant downward pressure. ReVenture forecasts that these declines will accelerate into 2026, driven by a combination of elevated inventory, rising days on market (DOM), and increasing price cuts. For opportunistic buyers, this shift represents a return of negotiating leverage.

The top ten markets forecasted for declines in 2026

10. orlando, Florida

  • Forecasted 2026 decline: Continuing downward trend
  • Current state: Prices dropped 4.2% in 2025, marking the largest decline since 2011.
  • Market drivers: Active inventory has reached a 10-year high of nearly 12,500 homes, while buyer demand has plummeted over 35% from its pandemic peak.

9. seattle, washington

  • Forecasted 2026 decline: -4.5%
  • Current state: Prices are down 1.2% over the last 12 months.
  • Market drivers: Inventory has surged to 5,800 homes (a 10-year high and nearly four times the pandemic low). Homes are sitting on the market for an average of 77 days, well above the long-term average.

8. dallas-fort worth, texas

  • Forecasted 2026 decline: -4.5% (bringing the total decline to nearly 10% from its peak)
  • Current state: Already down 3.8% year-over-year and 5.7% from its peak.
  • Market drivers: A massive wave of home completions from the 2020–2022 building boom is hitting the market just as migration to the area slows. Active inventory is at record highs, while December sales fell below long-term averages.

7. tampa, Florida

  • Forecasted 2026 decline: -4.9% (following a 5.6% drop in 2025)
  • Current state: Significant county-level declines are already underway, with Pinellas County down 8% and Manatee County down 7.8%.
  • Market drivers: Florida is experiencing a severe drop in migration—now at a 15-year low—driven by skyrocketing property taxes, rising insurance costs, and local affordability constraints.
