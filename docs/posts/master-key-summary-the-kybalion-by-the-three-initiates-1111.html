Introduction to the Kybalion and Hermetic philosophy

First published in 1908 and attributed to the pseudonymous "The Three Initiates," The Kybalion outlines the foundational principles of Hermetic philosophy. Though its true authorship remains unconfirmed, the book is widely attributed to William Walker Atkinson (1862–1932), a prominent figure in the New Thought movement. The teachings themselves are credited to Hermes Trismegistus, a legendary figure who bridged Egyptian and Greek wisdom, forming the basis of alchemy, astrology, and metaphysical science. Rather than offering rigid dogma, the text presents seven core principles to help individuals understand and align with the immutable laws governing reality.

The seven Hermetic principles

The core of Hermetic philosophy rests on seven universal laws that operate across physical, mental, and spiritual planes:

  • Mentalism: "The All is Mind." The universe is fundamentally a mental creation, meaning that thoughts shape human experience and reality.
  • Correspondence: Often summarized as "as above, so below." This principle posits that patterns repeat across different levels of existence, meaning the microcosm reflects the macrocosm.
  • Vibration: Nothing rests; everything is in constant motion. The frequency of vibration determines the nature of matter, thoughts, and emotions.
  • Polarity: Everything has an opposite, which is merely a different degree of the same spectrum (e.g., hot and cold, love and hate).
  • Rhythm: Life moves in cycles, characterized by a natural ebb and flow. Recognizing these cycles allows individuals to navigate transitions with ease.
  • Cause and effect: Nothing happens by chance. Every cause has its effect, and every effect has its cause, allowing individuals to become conscious creators.
  • Gender: Masculine (assertion and direction) and feminine (intuition and creativity) energies exist in all things and must be balanced for true mastery.

Practical applications for daily life and business

To integrate these teachings, the narrator suggests three daily practices:

  1. Mental transmutation: Reframing challenges by consciously shifting one's inner state and perspective.
  2. Aligning with cycles: Tracking personal energy levels to take bold action during high periods and rest during low periods.
  3. Balancing thought and action: Harmonizing strategy (feminine) with execution (masculine).
