Statutory definitions and tax authority

Champion and the host examine key definitions in Title 26 (the Internal Revenue Code) and Title 8 of the United States Code. Champion further explains that under 26 U.S.C. § 7701(a)(9), the phrase "United States when used in a geographical sense" designates territorial jurisdiction—specifically federal territories and the District of Columbia—as opposed to nationwide subject-matter jurisdiction. He maintains that income tax functions primarily as an indirect cross-border excise on non-resident aliens and foreign entities, which is why enforcement authority historically resided under the Assistant Commissioner (International).

Form 1040 and the Treasury decisions

Discussing the mechanics of tax filing, Champion points out that neither the statutes nor the federal regulations explicitly define the specific class of people required to file a standard Form 1040. Instead, he notes that 11 separate historical Treasury decisions govern the form's designated use, specifying that it applies to non-resident aliens earning U.S.-sourced income and their domestic agents.

Champion recounts historical legal defenses, including the Wesley Snipes case, arguing that defendants often rely on flawed arguments (such as the Section 861 position) rather than challenging the statutory scope of filing requirements. In his view, presenting direct statutory gaps and Treasury decisions creates an evidentiary burden the government avoids litigating in open appellate courts.

Navigating workplace payroll and HR departments

When attempting to halt payroll withholding, Champion emphasizes that corporate size dictates the outcome. In large corporations, HR and payroll staff rely entirely on institutional software and established procedures, viewing the underlying tax statutes as irrelevant to their day-to-day operations.

For independent contractors facing Form W-9 disputes, Champion suggests providing signed reliance affidavits under penalty of perjury or using pre-arranged small claims actions to give businesses judicial cover against failure-to-withhold penalties.
