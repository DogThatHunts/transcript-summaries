Master Key | Your Invisible Power (1921) by Genevieve Behrend

A foundational guide to mental science and the law of attraction, detailing how to use visualization and focused willpower to manifest personal abundance and inner peace.

Foreword and the principles of mental science

Genevieve Behrend introduces the foundational premise that fear must be banished from consciousness to attain one's desires. She argues that true joy comes from within, rather than from external possessions, and presents visualization as a systematic method to align the individual mind with the universal creative spirit.

Chapter 1: order of visualization

Visualization is described as a tool for organizing the mind and eliminating destructive mental hurry. Behrend asserts that visualization does not violate natural laws but fulfills them, comparing it to how inventors mastered flight by understanding and applying existing physical principles.

Chapter 2: how to attract to yourself the things you desire

Using the analogy of a magic lantern, Behrend explains how the mind projects mental "slides" onto the physical world. She emphasizes that a strong, steady will is required to hold the desired image in place and prevent conflicting thoughts from distorting the projection.

Chapter 3: relation between mental and physical form

Behrend addresses objections that visualizing material things is too superficial. She posits that physical matter is a necessary channel for the self-differentiation of the spirit, representing the natural progression of an idea into a tangible vehicle.

Chapter 4: operation of your mental picture

The relationship between the individual and universal mind is compared to a telephone system with branch centrals. Behrend explains that because the individual mind is a projection of the originating spirit, it has the inherent power to solidify thoughts into physical realities.

Chapter 5: expressions from beginners

This section shares several anecdotes of individuals using visualization to achieve their goals. Examples include a woman who manifested a specific sum of money, a doctor's wife who realized her childhood dreams, and industrial leaders who mentally planned their projects before execution.

Chapter 6: suggestions for making your mental picture

Behrend provides a practical exercise for visualization. She recommends practicing before breakfast and before bed, performing deep breathing exercises to centralize energy at the solar plexus, and mentally projecting oneself into the desired scenario with sensory detail.

Chapter 7: things to remember

Key guidelines for successful visualization are outlined, including: * Knowing exactly what conditions you wish to produce. * Avoiding strenuous effort, which suggests anxiety or opposition. * Keeping your desires secret to prevent dissipating your mental energy. * Allowing the creative power to determine the specific circumstances of manifestation.

Chapter 8: why i took up the study of mental science

Behrend shares her personal background, describing the deep loneliness she felt as a young widow. After seeking answers in various spiritual movements and meeting the seer Abdul-Baha, she discovered the writings of Judge Thomas Troward, which offered her a logical foundation for inner peace.

Chapter 9: how i attracted to myself $20,000

To test the principles of mental science, Behrend set a goal to manifest $20,000 to travel to England and study with Troward. By consistently visualizing the money and maintaining a state of mental ease, she successfully secured the funds within six weeks.

Chapter 10: how i became the only personal pupil of t. Troward

Despite Troward's initial refusal to take pupils, Behrend traveled to England. She secured an interview and eventually won his mentorship by correctly solving a spiritual riddle about a biblical passage from the Book of Revelation. Troward's subsequent letter outlines his simple, non-occult approach to study, centered on faith and Christian principles.

Chapter 11: how to bring the power in your word into action

Words are described as the physical embodiment of creative thoughts. Behrend explains that repeating words like "joy" sets up corresponding mental vibrations that eventually manifest as physical states of being.

Chapter 12: how to increase your faith

Behrend suggests that everyone possesses faith, though it is often misdirected as fear (faith in negative outcomes). To build positive faith, she advises individuals to mentally relive past moments of success and confidence.

Chapter 13: the reward of increased faith

Aligning one's thoughts with the constructive, forward movement of universal law allows the individual to act as a master of their circumstances rather than a victim. Behrend notes that failures occur when there is a mismatch between what one thinks and what one tries to produce.

Chapter 14: how to make nature respond to you

Because the universal mind is highly sensitive and responsive to suggestion, Behrend claims that an individual who recognizes their divine connection will find helpful ideas and opportunities flowing to them from their environment.

Chapter 15: faith with works—what it has accomplished

Using the story of Tyson, an Australian millionaire who dreamed of irrigating the desert, Behrend illustrates how a single focused idea can attract kindred ideas and financial support from the external world to achieve massive physical results.

Chapter 16: suggestions as to how to pray or ask believing you have already received

Behrend defines "scientific prayer" as the recognition that the spiritual prototype of a desire already exists the moment it is thought. She outlines a structured prayer format that involves cleansing the mind of negative emotions, affirming unity with divine love, and accepting the desire as already fulfilled.

Chapter 17: things to remember

The final chapter emphasizes self-mastery and the unity of soul and body. Behrend presents Jesus as a master mental scientist who demonstrated the latent power available to all humanity, urging readers to conquer negative habits and recognize themselves as instruments of divine operation.
