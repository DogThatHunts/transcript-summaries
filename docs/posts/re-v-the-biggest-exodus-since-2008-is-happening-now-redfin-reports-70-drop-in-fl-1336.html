Real estate investor purchases have plummeted nationwide as high mortgage rates eclipse rental cap rates, creating severe cash flow deficits for financed residential buyers.

Major pullback in real estate investor purchases

Investor home purchases across the United States have fallen by more than 50% from their pandemic peak, reaching levels not seen since 2014–2015. In several Sunbelt and boomtown markets, the drop is even more severe:

  • Jacksonville, FL: Down 77%
  • Atlanta, GA: Down 73%
  • Charlotte, NC: Down 70%
  • Orlando, FL: Down 68% (falling from 4,300 purchases per quarter in 2022 to 1,400 per quarter)
  • Nashville, TN: Down 67%
  • Phoenix, AZ: Down from 9,400 purchases per quarter to 3,000 per quarter (a 65% drop)

This sharp contraction contradicts mainstream media narratives that Wall Street institutions are endlessly dominating residential housing. While investors previously purchased 30% to 50% of homes in specific neighborhoods, rising ownership costs and cooling rents have forced many to pull back. Additionally, proposed federal legislation passed by the U.S. House of Representatives aims to ban institutional buyers holding 350 or more single-family homes from purchasing additional properties.

The debt-versus-cap-rate math

From 2010 to 2022, 30-year mortgage rates were artificially suppressed below average cap rates, enabling real estate investors to easily generate positive yield through leveraged debt. Currently, that dynamic has inverted:

  • National average cap rate: ~4.8%
  • 30-year mortgage rate: ~6.5% to 7.0%

Because the cost of debt exceeds the operating profit yield of residential property, purchasing rentals with financing results in immediate negative cash flow in most markets.

Regional market trends and rental metrics

Market stability and investor interest vary widely based on local rent trajectories and structural market conditions:

  • Resilient coastal markets: Investor demand has held up best in coastal areas like San Francisco, San Jose, Anaheim, Seattle, and New York, where buyers are driven more by long-term wealth preservation than immediate cash flow yields.
  • Top rent growth markets: San Francisco (+11% YoY), San Jose (+6.2%), Virginia Beach (+4.9%), Milwaukee, and Chicago continue to experience strong apartment rent increases.
  • Deepest rent declines: San Antonio (-6% YoY), Denver (-4%+), Austin (-4%+), Tampa (-4%+), and Phoenix (-4%+) face significant rental market deflation.
