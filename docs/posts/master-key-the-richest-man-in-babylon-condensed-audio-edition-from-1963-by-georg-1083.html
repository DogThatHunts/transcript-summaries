George S. Clason, through the character of Archad, shares a fable from ancient Babylon that illustrates how personal financial success is built on three foundational principles.

  • Archad, a man from a humble merchant family, begins with a simple realization: wealth is a power that enables greater possibilities in life.
  • He learns from Algamish, the moneylender, that a part of all one earns should be kept for oneself—starting with one-tenth of earnings.
  • The first law is not just saving, but making savings work: every saved copper piece becomes a seed that grows into a tree of wealth, with its children earning for you.
  • After a failed business venture with the brickmaker, Archad learns that wealth must be managed with competence and experience, not just with savings.
  • He then learns to use his savings wisely—investing in real assets like land and goods, and allowing them to generate returns.
  • The final lesson is that opportunity only comes to those who are prepared and consistent in their actions, not those who wait for luck.

The three precepts of financial success

  • First: Live upon less than you earn Archad begins by saving one-tenth of every income, rejecting the idea that spending is a sign of failure.

  • Second: Seek advice from those with experience He learns to consult competent individuals—like the shieldmaker or brickmaker—before making investments, ensuring decisions are grounded in real-world knowledge.

  • Third: Make your savings work for you His savings grow not just in value, but in productivity—each saved piece earns its own returns, and those returns earn more, creating compounding wealth.

Key takeaways

  • A part of all you earn is yours to keep—start with one-tenth, even if it seems small.
