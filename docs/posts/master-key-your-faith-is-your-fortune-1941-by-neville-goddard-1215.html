Master Key | your Faith is your Fortune (1941) by Neville Goddard

The core premise of consciousness

  • The nature of "I AM": Neville Goddard argues that the biblical phrase "I AM" represents the unconditioned awareness of being, which is the ultimate creative force. Creation begins when this formless awareness conditions itself by imagining and believing itself to be something specific.
  • The Bible as psychological drama: Goddard asserts that the Bible is not a historical record but a psychological drama of the human soul. The characters and stories represent personified qualities of consciousness, hidden from the uninitiated by ancient mystics.
  • The law of expression: To change one's external circumstances, one must first change their internal state of consciousness. The external world acts as a mirror, reflecting one's internal convictions and self-valuation.

Psychological interpretations of biblical events

  • The virgin birth and Christmas: Goddard interprets the virgin birth as a psychological process where the unconditioned consciousness (the virgin womb) conceives a desire (the child) without external aid. Christmas represents the moment man discovers his consciousness is the savior of his world.
  • Crucifixion and resurrection: The crucifixion is the fixation of a new conscious claim, where the "I AM" is nailed to a specific desired state through feeling. The resurrection is the subsequent manifestation of that state in the physical world.
  • The Trinity: The Father represents the aspect that makes the impression, the Mother/Holy Spirit represents the receptive womb of the mind, and the Son is the resulting physical expression.
  • True prayer: Prayer is described as a spiritual marriage ceremony. Instead of begging an external deity, the individual must go within, shut out the senses, and completely assume the feeling of the desire already fulfilled.

Disciplining the mind and achieving victory

  • The twelve disciples: The disciples represent twelve distinct qualities of the human mind that must be disciplined. For example, Simon Peter represents disciplined hearing (filtering out negative suggestions), Andrew represents courage, and Judas represents detachment (the ability to let go of the old self).
  • The allegory of Monte Cristo: Goddard analyzes Alexandre Dumas's The Count of Monte Cristo as a mystical biography. Edmond Dantès's journey from imprisonment to discovering treasure on Monte Cristo symbolizes the human soul discovering its true identity as God.
  • Joshua's formula for victory: The story of the fall of Jericho is presented as a blueprint for overcoming obstacles. Joshua represents the seeker, Jericho the objective, and the walls the barriers. By sending Rahab (the ability to travel psychologically) to dwell in the desired state, the seeker achieves victory through mental stillness and conviction.
