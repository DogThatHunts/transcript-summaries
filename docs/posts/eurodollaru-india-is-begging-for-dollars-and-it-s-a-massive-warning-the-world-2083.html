The energy shock feedback loop

A primary driver of India's immediate dollar demand is the price of oil. Because India imports the vast majority of its crude oil—which is invoiced and financed in US dollars—any increase in oil prices acts as a direct dollar shock.

EurodollarU explains that even a moderate price increase from $60 to $95 per barrel increases an importer's dollar requirements by over 58% for the same physical volume. This creates a destructive feedback loop: higher oil prices increase dollar demand, which weakens the rupee, making future oil imports even more expensive in local currency terms and fueling domestic inflation.

In an effort to prioritize scarce foreign exchange for essential imports like energy, the Indian government has launched an aggressive campaign to restrict gold and silver imports.
