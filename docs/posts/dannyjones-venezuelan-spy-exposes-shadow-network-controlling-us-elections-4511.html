Stolen elections: the takedown of democracies worldwide

Overview

DannyJones interviews author and former intelligence official about claims that Venezuelan-controlled election software has infiltrated U.S. voting systems. The conversation centers on allegations that Smartmatic and Dominion voting machines, developed with Venezuelan government backing and Chinese components, contain backdoors allowing vote manipulation. The author argues this represents a coordinated effort by Venezuela, China, Iran, and Russia to undermine American democracy, with complicity from U.S. intelligence agencies and elected officials.

Key insights

  1. The origin of the system: According to the author, Hugo Chavez's regime commissioned three Venezuelan engineers to develop vote-manipulation software in the early 2000s after polls showed Chavez would lose a 2004 referendum. The software was tested on lottery machines and later deployed internationally.

  2. Global expansion and U.S. infiltration: Smartmatic, allegedly Venezuelan-owned but disguised through corporate structures, began running elections in the U.S. around 2006–2007. The author claims the software contains 14 "backdoors" allowing various vote-switching methods, and that voting machines are connected to servers in Belgrade, Serbia, running Chinese Huawei hardware.

  3. Intelligence community knowledge and inaction: The author alleges that U.S. intelligence agencies discovered this system but chose to protect it rather than expose it. An FBI officer reportedly warned whistleblowers to "hide this stuff" or face arrest. The author suggests factions within the CIA have used the system for their own purposes.

  4. The Fox News settlement puzzle: When Fox News was sued by Dominion for $757 million in 2023, the author claims Fox's lawyers were offered evidence proving the voting machines were compromised—evidence that could have won the case—but declined it. The author speculates this settlement was orchestrated to silence media coverage of election fraud.

  5. Broader criminal network: The author argues the Cartel de los Soles (Venezuelan military and government officials) uses drug trafficking profits to fund U.S. political operations, NGOs, and media influence. He alleges members of Congress from both parties have benefited from bribes to adopt the voting software in their districts.

The whistleblowers and their investigation

The author met two independent whistleblowers—Martine Rodile (Venezuelan, formerly DEA) and Gary Bernson (ex-CIA officer who led operations in Afghanistan post-9/11)—who spent five years and $5 million of their own money investigating election fraud. After the 2020 election, they approached the Trump administration with evidence but were told not to release it prematurely. When Sidney Powell's "Kraken" claims collapsed, the whistleblowers moved underground, recruiting 20 engineers from within Smartmatic and Dominion who provided source code and operational details.

The author claims the FBI warned them to leave the country or face arrest. They relocated the engineers and their families to another country, where Patrick Byrne (Overstock.com founder) funded their continued work.

The 2020 election and alleged manipulation

The author argues that Venezuelan actors decided to support Biden over Trump in 2020 because they initially preferred Trump in 2016 (viewing him as less threatening than Clinton). The software was deployed in approximately 33 key swing districts. The author cites statistical anomalies, dead voters, and mail-in voting irregularities as evidence, though he acknowledges the system is "almost impenetrable from the outside" without insider information.

International dimensions

The author claims:

  • China invested capital and provided high-level technicians to perfect the software as part of its "unrestricted warfare" strategy against the U.S.
  • Iran collaborated with Venezuela on drone and missile programs, with bases allegedly aimed at Florida.
  • Russia participated in hacking Caribbean communications cables carrying sensitive U.S. government communications.
  • Cuba mentored the operation, with Fidel Castro viewing it as superior to decades of failed guerrilla campaigns.

The author alleges that U.S. intelligence agencies discovered these hacks but kept them secret. He claims Trump learned about Russian surveillance of White House communications via a cable near Iran and confronted Putin about it during an Alaska meeting, which explained the meeting's brevity and apparent failure.

U.S. government complicity

The author argues that U.S. officials have known about the system for years but protected it for geopolitical reasons. He cites:

  • A 2006 State Department cable warning about Smartmatic that was ignored.
  • Congresswoman Carolyn Maloney's letter to the Treasury Department raising concerns, which led to an investigation but no action.
  • CEPPS (Consortium for Elections and Political Process Strengthening), a USAID program allegedly promoting the software globally through the National Democratic Institute and International Republican Institute, covering both political parties.
  • The Trump administration inadvertently hiring Berkeley Research Group—run by Frank Holder, allegedly a Cuban and Venezuelan asset—to investigate 2020 fraud claims. The group's report found no irregularities, which was then cited by mainstream media to discredit fraud allegations.

The book's suppression

The author's manuscript was accepted by Skyhorse Publishing but rejected at the last minute. The author attributes this to threats from "high-level people," possibly connected to the Dominion lawsuit against Fox News. He self-published the book after a year-long legal battle to exit his contract.

Contested claims and gaps

  • The author presents allegations about specific officials (Georgia governor and secretary of state) accepting bribes but acknowledges lacking resources to fully document all cases.
  • Claims about Biden's "most extensive voter fraud organization" quote are presented as a Freudian slip; fact-checkers argue the quote was taken out of context from a podcast where Biden was discussing fraud prevention.
  • The author's assertion that Venezuelan actors control Mexican drug cartels is disputed by available sources, which describe collaboration rather than control.
  • The extent of CIA involvement remains unclear; the author distinguishes between the "official CIA" and a "shadow CIA" or "deep state," but provides limited specifics.

As of the conversation, the Trump administration is reportedly in a "conundrum" about whether to go public. The author mentions a lawyer in Trump's inner circle who has been entrusted with investigating election fraud.
