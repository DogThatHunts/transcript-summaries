Order flow and algorithms

  • Linda Raschke states that the largest market participants "are very, very good at disguising their movement" and execute through multiple accounts and firms, making tape reading futile for retail traders.
  • She cites a test: on a one-minute chart, 80% of intraday volume spikes occur at prior intraday swing highs or lows, revealing algorithmic triggers rather than fundamental buying or selling.
  • Algorithms can initiate and flatten positions in milliseconds — "faster than you can blink an eye" — creating noise that humans cannot process cognitively in real time.
  • Raschke advises gratitude for algorithmic liquidity but warns against trying to reverse-engineer their footprints; instead, focus on price-action structure and time-of-day tendencies.

Time horizons and edge

  • Raschke challenges the notion that professionals only trade long horizons, citing Renaissance’s Medallion Fund, Monroe Trout, and Toby Crabill as examples of elite funds with sub-24-hour holding periods.
  • She argues that certainty decays with longer time horizons because open systems introduce unpredictable variables (geopolitics, natural disasters, breakthroughs).
  • Her edge comes from capturing "waves" created by large-player impact that last hours to days, not from forecasting multi-month trends.
  • The "main idea" — determining whether the session will be low-to-high or high-to-low — is the primary decision; everything else is execution detail.

Risk management and position sizing

  • Raschke uses fixed fractional sizing: one unit per $100,000 of equity, recalculated each quarter based on average true range (ATR) dollar volatility per market.
  • She keeps bet sizes equal across all setups because "I don’t know where the winners are going to be" and varying size invites Murphy’s Law.
  • For discretionary traders, a prolonged drawdown signals a process error (fatigue, regime change, sloppiness) — cut size until the cause is identified.
  • Systematic traders should not reduce size in drawdowns; that is often when the system is about to produce a large winning trade.
  • Trade management: only ~15% of trades warrant a trailing stop; most should target a small profit (0.5 ATR) or a larger target (3 ATR), with scaling out per Perry Kaufman’s method (first three pieces close together, last two wider).

Trading psychology and flow state

  • Raschke delegates order execution to an assistant to eliminate emotional interference — "I’m the worst at executing… I buy when I should sell."

  • Flow state equals single-minded absorption — like practicing piano or skiing — where pattern recognition becomes automatic and verbal processing disappears.
  • Anxiety blocks flow; eliminating distractions (phone in another room, closed office door) and maintaining physical health (exercise, sleep) are prerequisites.
  • She recommends paper-trading a simple volatility-breakout system across five markets to desensitize to wins/losses and cultivate process-over-outcome mindset.

Ai and technology
