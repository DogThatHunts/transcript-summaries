Swadia | Once You Get Money, Upgrade These 8 Things Immediately

Former Wall Street CEO Swadia shares a practical four-part framework to strategically upgrade your life, health, and relationships as your income grows.

Swadia's guide to upgrading your life after earning money

In this address, former Wall Street professional and CEO Swadia outlines a strategic approach to personal finance, arguing that wealth is easily squandered on superficial lifestyle upgrades. Instead, Swadia introduces the "4S framework"—stamina, skills, security, and soul—to guide high earners toward investments that yield genuine well-being, resilience, and personal growth.

The diminishing returns of wealth

Swadia references the evolving psychological research on wealth and happiness. While Nobel laureate Daniel Kahneman's famous 2010 study suggested emotional well-being plateaus at $75,000, his updated 2023 collaborative research revealed that happiness continues to rise with income. However, this relationship is logarithmic rather than linear: achieving the same incremental boost in happiness requires doubling your income each time (e.g., from $80,000 to $160,000, and then to $320,000). Because of this diminishing marginal utility, strategic spending is required to extract real value from additional earnings.

Stamina: upgrading the machine

Before upgrading material possessions, Swadia advises upgrading the physical and mental "machine" responsible for earning that income.

  • Optimize sleep: Citing sleep studies comparing sleep deprivation to alcohol impairment, Swadia emphasizes investing heavily in sleep health, including high-quality mattresses and pillows.
  • Automate positive habits: Use financial resources to lower the friction of healthy behaviors, such as hiring a personal trainer or paying for a premium gym membership.
  • Eliminate daily bottlenecks: Invest in tools that directly enhance focus and comfort, such as standing desks or high-end noise-canceling headphones to block out environmental distractions.

Skills: compounding your capabilities

Swadia's mentor once advised: "If you want to make more money, don't upgrade your lifestyle, upgrade your life skills." In an era increasingly disrupted by artificial intelligence, continuous adaptation is essential.

  • Master foundational skills: Invest in evergreen capabilities that pay recurring dividends, such as corporate finance, communication, negotiation, and time management.
  • Establish a learning budget: Set aside dedicated time and money every six months to acquire new skills and close professional gaps.
  • Seek high-level mentorship: Proactively invest in relationships with experienced mentors who can offer decades of strategic insight in a single conversation.

Security: hedging against downside risks

Making money and keeping money require entirely different skill sets. While wealth is often built by taking calculated risks, it is preserved through smart hedges.

  • Mitigate concentration risk: Avoid over-allocating into single assets. Swadia shares a personal lesson of failing to hedge a major stock position that rose to $140 before crashing rapidly.
  • Build a BATNA fund: Maintain a robust emergency fund representing six to nine months of living expenses. Named after the negotiation concept "Best Alternative to Negotiated Agreement," this fund provides the leverage to walk away from unfavorable professional situations.
  • Manage financial complexity: As wealth grows, hire qualified specialists—such as tax accountants, financial planners, and estate attorneys—to protect assets and secure your family's future.

Soul: investing in meaning

Though investing in the "soul" offers the worst financial return, Swadia argues it provides the highest life return.

  • Fund personal passions: Allocate money toward creative and intellectual pursuits that bring genuine joy, such as Swadia's decision to study Western harmony at the Berklee College of Music.
  • Nurture relationships: Citing the 90-year Harvard Study of Adult Development, Swadia notes that the quality of close relationships at age 50 is the single most accurate predictor of physical health and happiness at age 80.
  • Practice active generosity: Use financial resources and time to support charitable causes, help strangers, and lift up others.
