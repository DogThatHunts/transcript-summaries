The illusion of central bank control

Many investors closely watch the Federal Reserve, inflation reports, and payroll figures, but EurodollarU argues that these mainstream metrics fail to capture the true state of the global economy. The modern monetary system has evolved far beyond traditional definitions of money into a complex, offshore, bank-centered network of wholesale funding, repurchase agreements, derivatives, and collateral chains—collectively known as the Eurodollar system.

Because this system operates outside domestic regulatory boundaries, central banks cannot directly measure or control it. By the early 1980s, the Federal Reserve abandoned tracking monetary aggregates like M1, M2, and M3, shifting instead to targeting the federal funds rate. This policy shift deliberately suppressed the natural information contained in free-market short-term interest rates. Former Fed Chairman Alan Greenspan acknowledged this in 1997, calling the task of finding and interpreting this missing monetary information a "severe challenge" for analysts. Consequently, modern economists rely on lagging indicators like CPI, GDP, and heavily revised payroll data, which only describe economic outcomes that have already occurred.

The monetary signal framework

To navigate this unmeasurable system, EurodollarU utilizes the "monetary signal framework." While the global monetary system cannot be observed directly, it leaves distinct "fingerprints" across financial markets. This framework functions like observing a black hole: scientists cannot see the black hole itself, but they can verify its existence and behavior by studying how surrounding objects react.

Instead of relying on a single indicator, the framework synthesizes multiple independent market signals to establish economic probabilities. Key market instruments to monitor include:

  • The treasury yield curve and TIPS curve: Indicating market expectations for growth, future interest rates, and inflation protection.
  • Interest rate swaps and swap spreads: Revealing balance sheet capacity and systemic risk perceptions among major financial institutions.
  • Treasury bill yields and collateral markets: Signaling whether high-quality collateral is abundant or scarce.
  • Exchange rates and funding spreads: Providing real-time data on global dollar liquidity and systemic restrictions.

When multiple independent curves align to communicate the same message, they reveal the real-time probabilities being priced in by institutions putting actual capital at risk.

Distinguishing temporary price shocks from true inflation

Misinterpreting market signals can lead to severe portfolio misallocations. For example, when tariffs rise or energy prices spike, the financial media often warns of a return to 1970s-style persistent inflation. However, EurodollarU emphasizes that a temporary spike in consumer prices is not the same as a sustained monetary inflation regime.

True, persistent inflation requires supportive monetary and financial conditions. By analyzing Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS) and calculating break-even inflation rates, investors can see how the market prices inflation across different horizons. During recent price scares, the TIPS curve indicated that while short-term price increases were possible, long-term inflation compensation remained subdued.

If an investor mistakes a temporary supply-side price shock for permanent inflation, they might avoid duration, over-allocate to inflation-protection assets, and completely miss the onset of weaker consumer demand and disinflation. When rising costs force households to cut back on discretionary spending, the initial price shock ultimately acts as a deflationary drag rather than an inflationary catalyst.

  2. Real-world application: Comparing newly released economic data directly against market curves to see if forward-looking indicators confirm or reject the mainstream narrative.
  3. Continuous refinement: Regularly testing portfolio assumptions against evolving market signals to determine if a chosen investment thesis still aligns with systemic realities.
