Will Barron | why your Sales Process Is Quietly Killing your Business (and how to fix it)

In this presentation, sales mentor Will Barron explains how bloated, semi-improvised sales processes quietly drain revenue from service businesses. He argues that business owners often waste time and money on complex tools, automated spam, and rigid scripts instead of focusing on human-to-human connection and structured, high-leverage sales habits. Barron outlines exactly what tactics to discard, what core principles to keep, and introduces a simple A-to-G framework to build a predictable sales engine.

The trap of the bloated sales process

Barron asserts that many service businesses carry a broken sales process built on disconnected tactics stacked on top of each other. Business owners often cycle through cold email tools, complex CRMs, and premature hires without understanding where their pipeline is actually failing. He emphasizes that marketing is a one-to-many activity, whereas sales must remain a highly personalized, one-to-one outreach.

What to eliminate from your sales strategy

To clean up a broken sales system, Barron recommends immediately discarding several common but ineffective tactics:

  • Generic automated outreach: Mass cold emails and automated LinkedIn campaigns quickly turn into spam, drowning prospects in robotic messages.
  • Overcomplicated methodologies and scripts: Forced acronyms, rigid scripts, and manipulative closing lines alienate modern, educated buyers.
  • Bloated CRMs: Complex pipelines with dozens of stages and fields cause decision paralysis. Barron advises using a simple dashboard with only a handful of stages.
  • Niche AI writing tools: Buyers are starving for genuine human attention. Barron suggests replacing automated copy with personalized, researched messages.
  • Premature sales hires: Hiring a closer to fix a broken sales process is an expensive mistake. Barron warns that you cannot hand over a system you have not built yourself.

The core sales elements to keep

Instead of chasing shortcuts, Barron highlights the high-leverage activities that consistently drive revenue:

  • The diagnosis call: This is the highest-leverage part of the sales process. It involves sitting with a prospect to identify where they are, where they want to go, and positioning your service as the bridge between those two points.
  • Genuine urgency: Rather than using fake deadlines or pressure tactics, find the actual trigger event causing the prospect to seek help.
  • Early financial alignment: Discussing budgets and maths early prevents being blindsided by pricing objections at the end of the process.
  • Dedicated sales time: Barron advises blocking out non-negotiable sales time in your calendar every week to prevent the "feast and famine" cycle that occurs when business owners stop selling during project delivery.

The A-to-G framework for sales execution

Barron organizes his recommended daily and weekly sales habits into a simple, alphabetical framework:

  • A is for Audit: Clean out the pipeline and remove dead leads that clutter your focus.
  • B is for Building the bar: Create a simple qualification checklist to ensure only legitimate opportunities enter your CRM.
  • C is for Commit: Protect your daily sales time to move deals one step forward without skipping days.
  • D is for Drift: Understand that failing to commit leads to revenue drift, empty calendars, and losing clients to competitors who maintain consistent sales activity.
  • E is for Engine: Prioritize the diagnosis call above all else, aiming to book more of these calls each week.
  • F is for Figures: Calculate the cost of the prospect's problem against the cost of your solution to demonstrate a clear return on investment.
  • G is for Closing the gap: Earn the right to ask for the business by asking a simple closing question, such as: "Does it make sense to get started?"
