Abstract

The video explains the purpose of a Social Security card and the responsibilities of the number holder.
It outlines when government agencies, employers, or private organizations may request the number and what disclosures are required.
It emphasizes that the SSN belongs solely to the individual and that misuse should be avoided.

Key takeaways

  • The name on a Social Security card is the holder’s legal name, used for signing official documents.
  • Your SSN is yours alone; keep the card safe and record the number in a secure place.
  • If your citizenship or immigration status changes, you must apply for a replacement card with proof of identity.
  • Employers must record your name and number exactly as shown on the card to ensure correct earnings reporting.
  • Private organizations may use SSNs for record‑keeping, but such use is neither required nor prohibited by federal law; the relationship is a private matter.
  • Any government agency requesting your SSN must tell you whether providing it is mandatory or voluntary, its authority, and how the number will be used.
  • For non‑citizens, the card may be marked “not valid for employment” unless proper DHS authorization is present.
