Introduction

Dave Champion, author of Income Tax Shattering the Mist, begins by stating that IRS forms do not reveal their true legal purpose, which is why most people would not sign them if they knew the truth. He emphasizes that 32 million businesses in the United States use the W-9 form, often under the misconception that it is required, but this is not the case.

Purpose of W-9

  • Title: Request for Taxpayer Identification Number and Certification
  • Purpose: To obtain a taxpayer identification number, but the form does not specify who is required to fill it out or under what circumstances.

Legal requirements

  • 26 U.S.C. 6041: Requires information returns on payments, but only applies to those making payments in the course of performing the function of a public office, not private sector businesses.
  • 26 CFR 1.6041: Regulations for 6041 also state that the requirement does not apply to private sector companies.
  • 26 CFR 1.1471-1B148: Classifies the W-9 as a withholding certificate, which is only to be provided to a withholding agent.

Withholding agent

  • Definition: A withholding agent is a person making payment of U.S. source income belonging to a foreign person.
  • Payor: The term payor includes a withholding agent and does not apply to payments between ordinary domestic businesses.

Falsehoods of W-9

  • Falsehoods: When signing a W-9, individuals attest to falsehoods about who the requester is, who owns the income, and their role.
  • Penalties: Individuals are implicitly stating that the income belongs to a foreign person, which is often not the case.

Solutions for Businesses

  • Documentation: Businesses should ask vendors and contractors to sign a document that certifies their status rather than demanding a W-9.
  • Certification: This document should specify whether the business is owned or controlled by a foreign person, acting as a U.S. agent for a foreign person, or receiving U.S. source income belonging to a foreign person.
