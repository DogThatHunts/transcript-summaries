Master Key | Three Magic Words (1954) by U.S. Andersen

An exploration of the creative power of the universal subconscious mind, revealing how individuals can overcome mental barriers to manifest health, success, and spiritual unity.

Introduction and forward

U.S. Andersen introduces the core philosophy of his work, asserting that true security does not lie in material advancements or technology, but in discovering the soul. He argues that thought is the only mover in creation, and the universal subconscious mind—or God—is the creator that manifests exactly what an individual thinks.

Chapter 1: the lock

Andersen explains how early life experiences, pain, and sensory perceptions forge a "lock" in the subconscious mind, creating negative "prompters" that dictate a person's life. While the subconscious mind is an all-powerful creative instrument, it acts as a servant to the conscious mind, which serves as the master switch. To unlock one's potential, individuals must eradicate negative self-conceptions and focus entirely on the timeless spirit within.

Chapter 2: illusion

This section reframes evil, disease, and suffering as human-made illusions arising from a limited ego viewpoint rather than creations of a loving God. The spiritual law of cause and effect dictates that the subconscious mind acts like a garden, growing whatever seeds of thought are planted. True progress comes from accepting spiritual abundance with faith rather than trying to force outcomes through sheer willpower.

Chapter 3: mind

Andersen explores the mind as pure thought and energy, distinct from the physical brain. Bypassing the conscious mind through tools like hypnosis reveals that the subconscious mind reasons deductively, possesses perfect memory, and responds entirely to suggestion. True self-mastery is achieved by consciously directing our thoughts to program the subconscious with positive suggestions.

Chapter 4: form

This chapter details how intelligent energy and vibration coalesce to create physical matter, meaning that consciousness exists in all things. Evolution is framed as the universal mind's journey to expand its own self-consciousness by manifesting as finite forms. As human consciousness expands, it moves closer to a spiritual oneness with God, eventually transcending the limitations of physical form.

Chapter 5: intuition

Andersen defines intuition as the ability to align with the all-knowing universal mind, encompassing phenomena like telepathy, clairvoyance, and precognition. He references parapsychological research to argue that mind is independent of space and time. True genius is described as an intuitive grasp of universal laws, which is accessible to anyone who silences the doubts of the conscious mind.

Chapter 6: Faith

Faith is defined as the mental conviction that bridges thought and physical manifestation. To break ingrained habits of negative thinking, the author prescribes a strict "30-day mental diet" during which individuals must reject all negative thoughts and consciously select only constructive, positive ideas.

Chapter 7: attraction

This section explains the law of attraction, where the subconscious mind manifests whatever an individual is deeply convinced of. True success requires relinquishing the vanity of personal isolation and trusting the universal mind to execute plans, rather than trying to dictate the exact physical steps or timeline.

Chapter 8: love

Andersen positions love as the supreme law of life and the ultimate urge toward spiritual unity. Fear and hate are defined as reactions to frustrated love, while physical intimacy and sexuality are celebrated as natural, pleasurable expressions of divine connection. True love begins with recognizing the spiritual unity of all life.

Chapter 9: Success

Success is redefined as creative service to humanity rather than the mere accumulation of money, which is simply a medium of exchange. Real prosperity flows naturally when individuals focus on productivity, utilize their imagination to challenge perceived limitations, and turn their work over to the divine.

Chapter 10: health

Asserting that physical ailments are bodily projections of mental conflicts—such as guilt, resentment, and fear—Andersen frames health as the natural state of spirit. True healing is achieved not by treating the physical body, but by surrendering the ego and affirming one's inherent spiritual perfection.

Chapter 11: immortality

This chapter distinguishes the mortal, transitory ego from the deathless, universal self. Death is reframed as a peaceful transition and an expansion of consciousness back into the infinite, unified mind, free from the limitations of personality, space, and time.

Chapter 12: the key

Andersen reveals the book's central metaphysical secret: that individual consciousness is identical to the universal mind, meaning that "you are God." By letting go of the ego and realizing this ultimate truth through meditation, individuals can transcend physical limitations and achieve absolute peace and power.
