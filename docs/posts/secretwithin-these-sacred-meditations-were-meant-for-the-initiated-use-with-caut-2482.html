SecretWithin | These Sacred Meditations were Meant for the Initiated. (Use with caution)

Five ancient meditations that promise to unlock hidden spiritual forces and guide the soul toward awakening.

Introduction

  • Steiner presents five sacred meditations drawn from his manuscripts, claiming they awaken the higher self and open perception beyond the physical.

Meditation 1: morning meditation

  • Begin with silence before dawn, creating a gateway of stillness.
  • Perform a powerful inner gesture lifting the soul toward the higher self.
  • Speak silently the mantra: "More radiant than the sun… I am this self."
  • Repeated practice builds inner strength and a sense of being lifted.

Meditation 2: inner identification

  • Focus on a sentence from Light on the Path: "Before the eyes can see they must be incapable of tears."
  • Let the phrase penetrate emotionally for five minutes, avoiding analysis.
  • Cultivate a deep stillness, becoming blind and deaf to external impressions.

Meditation 3: devotional concentrations

  • Direct attention to specific body points while repeating inward phrases: I am, It thinks, She feels, He wills.
  • Visualize energy streams flowing inward to the heart, then outward again.
  • Over time, this practice is said to awaken a living force and bridge micro‑ and macro‑cosm.

Meditation 4: evening retrospection

  • Observe the day as if watching a film, without judgment.
  • Move backward through events, cultivating a clear, objective awareness.
  • Keep a dream journal to capture symbolic impressions, which Steiner claims can reveal spiritual currents.

Meditation 5: Light on the Path

  • Meditate on four profound sentences for 14 days each, e.g., "Before the eyes can see they must be incapable of tears."
  • Steiner warns these are living spiritual powers that, if dwelt upon sincerely, awaken intuition, clarity, and strength.

Auxiliary exercises

  1. Concentration of Thought – Focus on a simple object for five minutes to train mental clarity.
  2. Initiative in Action – Act daily from a resolved will, not habit or command.
  3. Composure in Joy and Sorrow – Cultivate equanimity, keeping inner balance amid external emotions.
  4. Positivity (Christ Impulse) – See the good in all, fostering hope and light.
  5. Freedom from Bias – Develop objective thinking, free from cultural filters.
  6. Regular Repetition – Practice all exercises consistently, mirroring natural rhythms.

Closing thoughts

  • Steiner emphasizes that the heart will become the true organ of spiritual perception.
  • He urges sustained, purposeful meditation to transform oneself and, by extension, the world.
