Will Barron | The Real Reason Your Service Business Feels Stuck

Sales mentor Will Barron breaks down the three foundational sales mistakes keeping service businesses stuck under five million dollars in revenue and how to fix them.

Will Barron, founder of salesman.com, argues that service businesses struggling to scale past the $5 million revenue mark are rarely held back by a simple lack of leads. Instead, they are trapped by foundational flaws in their sales execution. Barron outlines three root causes of stagnant revenue and provides actionable strategies to overcome them.

The trap of chasing unqualified leads

Barron observes that business owners often spend significant time and energy nurturing prospects who will never buy. These prospects may have a general interest but lack the budget, authority, or immediate urgency to make a decision.

To illustrate this, Barron shares a case study of Jamal, a mentorship client running a commercial cleaning company. Although 14 of Jamal's 15 active accounts were prestigious luxury car dealerships, his sales pipeline was cluttered with generic office and supermarket leads. Barron coached Jamal to purge all non-dealership prospects from his CRM and focus exclusively on nearby prestige showrooms. By narrowing his focus, Jamal secured six new high-value dealership accounts within three months.

The fix: Document a strict qualification checklist. This list must define the exact stage of growth, specific pain points, trigger events creating immediate urgency, and decision-making authority required for a prospect to enter the pipeline. Anyone who fails these criteria should be politely declined or referred elsewhere.

Escaping the feast-and-famine sales roller coaster

Many service business owners find themselves on a "sales roller coaster" because they only prospect when client work dries up. When business is good, they focus entirely on delivery and stop all sales activity. Once current projects end and revenue drops, panic sets in, prompting desperate outreach to any prospect willing to listen.

Barron emphasizes that a consistent sales system removes this desperation. When a business maintains a healthy pipeline, the owner can show up to sales calls with genuine options, allowing them to qualify prospects honestly, maintain price integrity, and avoid behaving like a "slimy used car salesperson."

The fix: Block out dedicated, non-negotiable time in the calendar every day or week solely for active outreach and follow-ups. This time must be strictly protected from administrative procrastination, such as updating CRM fields or rewriting scripts.

Articulating hard ROI and asking for the close

Many sales calls feel successful to the business owner but ultimately end in silence. Barron explains that this usually happens because the seller failed to provide a clear, quantifiable return on investment (ROI) that the prospect could use to justify the spend to themselves or their stakeholders.

While modern B2B sales do not require aggressive, outdated closing techniques, sellers must still connect their services to hard financial outcomes and explicitly guide the prospect to the next step.

The fix: Clearly calculate and explain the financial ROI of your service during the call. Once the value is established, ask a straightforward closing question, such as, "Does it make sense to move forward with this?" to initiate the onboarding process.
