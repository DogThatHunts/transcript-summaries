Zillow's warning and rising treasury yields

This rise is attributed to factors like government deficit, oil shocks, and AI debt, and it sets a floor for how low mortgage rates can fall. Higher treasury yields directly translate to higher mortgage rates, which in turn lead to lower homebuyer demand, increased supply, and ultimately, lower prices. Despite these declines, RE-V states that national home prices remain in a bubble, resulting in historically low home sales and a significant drop in mortgage applications. The housing market is described as being in a "massive housing market recession," with buyers backing out of deals at record rates. The real problem: home prices, not mortgage rates

RE-V contends that while rising interest rates seem alarming, current mortgage rates (around 6.5-7%) are actually quite normal when viewed historically. Rates were in the 8-9% range in the 1990s and 5-6.5% in the 2000s. The perception of high rates is skewed by the artificially low rates during the pandemic. The real issue, according to RE-V, is the home value to income ratio, which currently stands at 4.3 (a typical home value of $371,000 divided by a median income of $86,000). This is significantly higher than the long-term average of 3.5, indicating that the housing market is 22% overvalued compared to its 75-year norm. Historically, such high ratios (seen in 2006 before the crash and after World War II) have always corrected, either through price drops or periods of housing disinflation where incomes rise while prices stagnate.

Buyer-seller standoff explained

A major impediment in the current market is the disconnect between buyers and sellers. Many sellers hold mortgages with very low rates (e.g., 3.5%) secured during the pandemic. They perceive the value of their low-interest mortgage as substantial, making them reluctant to cut prices. RE-V illustrates this with a home in Nolensville, Tennessee, listed at $725,000. The owner's payment is $2,900/month at a 3.56% rate. However, a buyer today would face a $4,300/month payment at current rates for the same price. To achieve the owner's payment, the house would need to be priced at $475,000, a 34% decline, bringing it closer to its pre-pandemic sale price of $460,000. This creates a situation where buyers cannot afford current prices at current rates, while sellers are unwilling to compromise due to the perceived value of their cheap mortgages.

Market shift: Buyers gaining leverage

RE-V predicts that buyers will eventually gain the upper hand because sellers cannot hold out indefinitely. The market is subtly shifting: the percentage of mortgages under 3% has dropped from 25% in 2022 to 19%, while mortgages over 6% now constitute 22% of the market. As more owners with higher rates enter the market, pricing will increasingly reflect the current reality of what it takes to sell a home, rather than being influenced by the discounted value of cheap mortgage rates held by existing owners. This trend is expected to continue, leading to more realistic pricing.
