In this video, host RE-V analyzes recent Redfin data revealing an unprecedented spike in home contract cancellations across the United States. Record housing contract cancellations and the buy vs. rent gap

Redfin reported that nearly 14% of homebuyers on pending sales canceled their contracts in February 2026, marking the highest cancellation rate in the platform's decade-long dataset. In several major Sunbelt markets—including Tampa, San Antonio, Fort Worth, Atlanta, and Jacksonville—cancellation rates approached 20%.

RE-V attributes this surge to severe affordability constraints and economic jitters:

  • High monthly payments: New townhomes priced between $390,000 and $430,000 require estimated mortgage payments of $2,800 to $2,900 per month.
  • Severe rent differential: In suburban areas north of Nashville, renting a comparable home costs approximately $1,900 per month. Nationwide, buying is currently over 40% more expensive than renting.
  • Buyer anxiety: Nervous buyers are increasingly stepping back after evaluating updated lender numbers or simply withholding earnest money deposits.

Builder competition driving down existing home prices

Homebuilders are actively lowering prices and offering rate buy-downs to clear inventory, placing heavy pressure on existing homeowners trying to sell.

  • Price undercutting: RE-V highlights an existing flipped home listed at $500,000 ($329 per square foot), while nearby builders are selling larger, brand-new floor plans for $392,000 ($200 per square foot).
  • Local price declines: According to ReVenture app forecasts, certain suburban Nashville zip codes are projected to experience a 7% decline in home values over the coming year.
  • Misaligned seller expectations: Many existing homeowners have not adjusted to market changes, keeping list prices well above fair value despite sitting on the market for extended periods.

Labor market fears and AI job disruption

In addition to interest rates and inflation, labor market uncertainty is acting as a major headwind for the housing market. RE-V cites a Redfin survey indicating that 59% of Americans believe artificial intelligence advancements will eliminate jobs and reduce their ability to afford homes in the future.

White-collar buyers in particular are expressing concern over prospective raises and job security amid high-profile corporate workforce reductions, such as Block laying off 40% of its staff and rumored cuts at other tech firms. A channel poll conducted by RE-V revealed that only 8% of viewers intend to buy a home at current prices, while 40% stated they would only purchase if prices drop by 20% to 40%.

Tactical playbook for leverage in a buyer's market

Because the gap between active sellers and available buyers has widened significantly, RE-V emphasizes that buyers retain the balance of power in most U.S. markets and recommends specific tactics for securing discounts:

  • Track true days on market (DOM): Sellers frequently delist and relist properties to reset DOM counters. Identifying a property's cumulative history reveals actual seller distress.
  • Treat list prices as preliminary: Buyers should evaluate properties based on what they are willing to pay rather than being deterred by high ask prices.
  • Demonstrate financial readiness: Submitting a pre-approval letter or proof of cash liquidity alongside low offers establishes credibility.
  • Embrace initial rejections: Sellers often reject aggressive first offers but frequently return to negotiate weeks later if no other buyers materialize.
  • Leverage the due diligence period: Using a standard 10-day inspection period allows buyers to conduct thorough property evaluations while retaining the right to cancel and recover earnest deposits.

Regional market split: high-cancellation vs. competitive areas

While Sunbelt metros face elevated cancellations and price corrections, several regional markets remain tight and competitive. Redfin data indicates that contract cancellation rates remain under 4% in cities such as San Francisco, San Jose, Oakland, Milwaukee, and Nassau County, New York. RE-V notes that in markets with positive price growth forecasts above 2% to 3%, buyers will face continued competition and limited negotiating room.
