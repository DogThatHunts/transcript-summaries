Master Key | Your Word Is Your Wand (1941) by Florence Scovel Shinn

Florence Scovel Shinn outlines how spoken affirmations, intuitive action, and mental non-resistance can reframe negative conditions to manifest health, financial abundance, and divine purpose.

Overview and the power of the spoken word

In this sequel to The Game of Life and How to Play It, Florence Scovel Shinn posits that human speech acts as a creative force capable of reshaping external realities. Citing biblical principles and metaphysical concepts, Shinn asserts that individuals possess the authority to transform unhappiness, sickness, and lack into joy, health, and abundance through targeted spoken affirmations.

Shinn emphasizes the importance of the imaging faculty, warning against forcing outcomes through intense mental visualization. Instead, she advocates speaking words that invite spontaneous mental clarity and align with a divine design. She contends that negative emotional states—such as fear, anger, and resentment—disrupt the nervous system and trigger disease, whereas emotional poise and unwavering faith enable mastery over personal circumstances and physical conditions.

Success, prosperity, and financial supply

Shinn argues that every individual enters the world fully financed by God, with universal supply accessible through spoken faith. She illustrates this concept through several anecdotal examples:

  • The magic purse: A woman possessing only two dollars was instructed to bless her money and affirm an endless, crammed, jammed supply; shortly after, she received a gift of six thousand dollars.
  • The open door of destiny: A man seeking employment was given the phrase, "Behold, I have set before thee the open door of destiny, and no man shall shut it, for it is nailed back," leading to a sudden executive position in another city.
  • The tea room business: A student followed an intuitive hunch to launch a business without initial capital, applying affirmations of divine supply to establish a bustling, highly profitable shop.
  • Circulating money: Shinn asserts that giving precedes receiving. In one case, a woman seeking five hundred dollars was advised to open her channels by giving generously and tipping service staff, which unexpectedly yielded a five-hundred-dollar gift from her hostess on her deadline date.

Relationships, love, and marriage

Addressing romantic and interpersonal dynamics, Shinn attributes disharmony to mental division and subconscious fears:

  • Love and "the other woman": Shinn claims that fear of rival interference stems from a belief in duality, advocating affirmations of divine oneness to dissolve insecurity.
  • Alignment in marriage: For marriages to endure, partners must exist in the same thought world. Shinn recounts a couple who drifted toward divorce because the husband inhabited a prosperous mindset while the wife maintained a limited, bargain-seeking consciousness.
  • Forgiveness: Shinn claims that holding ill will or resentment brings personal failure, whereas practicing forgiveness releases karmic consequences and opens pathways for good.

Faith, guidance, and active demonstration

Shinn distinguishes passive hope from active faith, stressing that spiritual principles require physical or practical alignment, which she terms digging ditches:

  • Faith chests versus hope chests: Shinn advises keeping personal intentions private until materialized, noting that publicizing unfulfilled goals invites external doubt.
  • Acting on intuition: Demonstrating faith often requires following seemingly minor hunches. For example, a woman seeking to rent a room only succeeded after buying specific lamps to signal her internal readiness to the subconscious mind.
  • Everyday signs: Shinn maintains that guidance arrives constantly through unexpected channels, such as a casual remark from a stranger providing the exact resolution to a persistent worry.

Health, body correspondences, and physical ailments

Shinn posits that physical sickness originates from spiritual violations or negative thought patterns, asserting that true healing requires correcting subconscious beliefs:

  • Gallstones and hard growths: Shinn claims resentment and hatred form hard deposits in the physical body, which can only be dissolved through forgiveness and goodwill.
  • Eye conditions: Visual impairment is linked to fear, suspicion, or a refusal to see divine opportunities in the present.
  • Hearing issues: Deafness is described as a reflection of strong personal stubbornness or a subconscious refusal to hear unpalatable truths.
  • Rheumatism and false growths: Attributed to persistent fault-finding, acid thoughts, and underlying fear.

Non-resistance and overcoming obstacles

Shinn teaches that resisting or hating a condition impresses it deeper into the subconscious, causing it to recur:

  • Neutralizing repeated patterns: A woman plagued by a partner's constant mention of female relatives resolved the pattern by practicing non-resistance and blessing all relatives mentally.
  • Neutralizing debt and loss: Shinn argues that debt and loss are subconscious illusions. By affirming that no loss exists in divine mind, lost items are either restored or replaced by superior equivalents.
  • Mastery over circumstances: Shinn asserts that individuals possess inherent dominion over natural elements, citing instances where group intent or spoken affirmations cleared thick fog or brought rain during droughts.
