In this short video, Jen Gottlieb shares communication insights derived from researcher Frank Luntz's 30 years of focus group testing.

Luntz's work reveals that effective communication is not about what you say, but rather what your audience actually hears.

By shifting the focus from sounding smart to being understood, speakers can use seven specific phrases to capture attention and build trust.

Key takeaways

  • Simplify the message: Using the phrase "Let me put that in plain English" builds trust because people naturally trust what they can easily understand.
  • Verify comprehension: Asking "Tell me what you took from that" helps confirm that your core message actually landed with the listener.
  • Paint a picture: Saying "Imagine this" leverages visual storytelling, which is far more memorable than dry facts.
  • Address their needs first: Leading with "Here's why this matters to you" ensures you are focusing on the listener's problems rather than your own.
  • Drive engagement and focus: Asking "So what would you do?" sparks active engagement, while transition phrases like "The bottom line is" and "Here's what I want you to remember" signal key takeaways that keep the audience leaning in.
