The flaws of traditional retirement accounts

Lombardi critiques the financial products marketed to the average American, such as 401(k)s, IRAs, SEPs, and 529 college savings plans. He argues that these accounts are designed to benefit the government and Wall Street rather than the saver:

  • Hidden fees: Lombardi claims that Wall Street fees can steal over 50% of an individual's retirement savings over a 30-year period.
  • Tax traps: Deferring taxes to retirement is a gamble, Lombardi argues, pointing out that the highest marginal US federal tax rate was 70% in the 1980s and 92% in the 1960s. With national debt rising, future tax rates are likely to increase.
  • Lack of liquidity: Traditional retirement accounts penalize users for accessing their own money before age 59½ and force distributions at age 72.

The mechanics of Section 7702 plans

To bypass these limitations, Lombardi advises copying the strategies of the ultra-wealthy by utilizing IRS Section 7702. This section of the tax code governs permanent life insurance policies, such as Indexed Universal Life (IUL) plans. When structured properly as an overfunded policy, the container offers unique benefits:

  • Zero downside market risk: The cash value is tied to market indexes using derivatives (call options). If the market goes up, the account captures the gains; if the market crashes, the account has a 0% floor, meaning not a penny of principal is lost.
  • Tax-free leverage: Instead of withdrawing money and triggering taxes, policyholders take collateralized loans against their cash value. Because the cash value continues to compound while the loan is outstanding, the growth often exceeds the loan interest, creating a profitable "negative interest rate loan."
  • Asset protection: In most states, these plans are legally protected from lawsuits, creditors, and divorce. They also bypass probate and are incontestable in court.
