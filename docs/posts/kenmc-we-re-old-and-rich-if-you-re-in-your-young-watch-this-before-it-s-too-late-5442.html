What is Success sabotage?

Success sabotage refers to the hidden behaviors that undermine personal growth, even as one achieves success in other areas. It's a concept explored by KenMc and Seale Stanford at The Ranch in St. Joe, Texas, as part of an ascension process where strengths grow alongside weaknesses.

The ascension process and its dangers

As people grow stronger, both their strengths and weaknesses become more apparent. This process of ascension means that the 'dark side'—such as self-sabotage—also intensifies. For example:

  • The better one becomes at a skill, the more their ego grows.
  • This can lead to behaviors that appear successful on the surface but damage long-term well-being.

The shift in definitions of Success

KenMc reflects on how his definition of success has evolved:

  • In his youth, success meant financial achievement.
  • After becoming a father, he prioritized family.
  • Now, as he ages, health becomes the top priority.

Personal strengths and sabotages

KenMc identifies three personal strengths:

  • Making money
  • Studying intensely
  • Committing to personal development

He lists three personal sabotages:

  • Not exercising (especially as he ages)
  • Shopping excessively
  • Wasting time on attractive people, particularly those with money

He notes that these behaviors are not just about lifestyle—they are self-sabotaging habits that erode health and discipline.

The role of habits and distractions

KenMc identifies several self-sabotaging habits:

  • Spending hours scrolling on devices
  • Skipping meals, workouts, and meditation
  • Prioritizing distractions over health and personal goals

He emphasizes that these small daily choices compound, leading to long-term deterioration.

A process for self-reflection

KenMc and Seale Stanford use a structured exercise:

  • List three personal success strategies.
  • List three personal sabotage behaviors.
  • Identify how to address and correct those sabotages.

This process helps individuals become more self-aware and intentional in their growth.
