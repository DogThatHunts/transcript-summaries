The AI Debt Crisis Has Already Begun (your 401K Is Next)

ITM Trading presents a warning: the AI boom is not just a tech trend—it's a debt-driven phenomenon that is already infiltrating your financial life.

Key insights

  • $500 billion in AI-related debt has been borrowed this year alone, nearly four times the amount from the previous year.
  • This debt is not confined to tech companies; it is tied to retirement accounts, banks, and even paychecks.
  • The cost of insuring against corporate debt defaults has hit record highs for major U.S. tech firms like Amazon, Intel, Microsoft, and NVIDIA.
  • A broader risk index meant to track such debt has become calmer, suggesting a hidden crisis.
  • Private credit lenders—largely unregulated—have seen default rates climb to 6.3%, with 17% of borrowers adding interest to principal instead of paying it.

Where the Debt Is hiding

  • Your 401k: Top U.S. tech and AI companies make up a large portion of the S&P 500. If you hold index or target-date funds, you're likely exposed.
  • Your 'safe' savings: Private credit loans are being sold to insurance companies, annuities, and pension funds—making conservative retirement accounts riskier than they appear.
  • Your paycheck: If tech companies face financing issues, the lenders could fail, impacting the businesses you work for or serve.
  • Your bank: U.S. banks are financing private credit firms. If those investments lose value, banks themselves could be exposed.

The hidden risk: circular financing

  • Tech companies are increasingly relying on each other for financing—like a lemonade stand buying lemons from its owner.
  • On paper, both parties appear to profit. But if one stops funding the other, both suffer.
  • In the AI ecosystem, this interdependence means a failure in one company could trigger a cascade failure across the system.

A warning from history

  • In 2008, millions of Americans who didn’t bet on housing saw their retirement savings collapse.
  • That wasn’t due to personal risk—but because of layers of debt and financial structures built on risky bets.
  • The AI boom may be repeating this pattern: a transformative technology, but one operating in a fragile, interconnected debt system.

What this means for you

  • You don’t need to own AI stocks to be at risk.
  • The people profiting from AI are not the ones who will bear the losses when the system fails.
  • The ordinary American—whether invested or not—is vulnerable.

What you can do

  • Understand what you're actually invested in across all retirement accounts.
  • Recognize that the financial system is not immune to failure.
  • Protect your wealth outside the system—such as with physical gold and silver.
  • Gold and silver are not tied to debt, derivatives, or unproven profits. They are tangible, ownership-based assets.
  • Consider speaking with a wealth protection specialist to build a personalized plan.
