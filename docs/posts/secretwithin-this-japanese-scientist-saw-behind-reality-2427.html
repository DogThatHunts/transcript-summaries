Overview

SecretWithin presents a lecture by Michio Kushi, a Japanese philosopher and founder of macrobiotics, who proposes a comprehensive framework for understanding reality across multiple levels—from the infinite universe down to individual human consciousness. The core argument is that most people live in self-created illusions, mistaking their mental images for objective reality, and that awakening to this fact is the path to genuine freedom and happiness.

The structure of reality: from infinity to matter

Kushi describes reality as operating on two fundamental levels:

The absolute world is infinite, beginningless, and endless—what some traditions call God. It has no time, space, beginning, or end. It is non-phenomenal, existing beyond form and manifestation.

The relative world is everything we perceive—matter, energy, life, and thought. This world is governed by two opposite tendencies or polarities, traditionally called yin and yang, or plus and minus.

According to Kushi, the entire universe moves because of the interaction between these two opposite forces. He illustrates this with examples: high and low, right and left, hot and cold, expansion and contraction. These antagonistic complementary forces appear everywhere once you start looking.

The cascade from infinity to human form

Kushi traces a hierarchical descent from the absolute to the physical:

  1. One infinity splits into two opposite tendencies
  2. Energy and vibration emerge from the interaction of these tendencies
  3. Pre-atomic particles (electrons, protons) form from vibrations
  4. Physical matter (elements, atoms) combines from particles
  5. Plants emerge on Earth from this matter
  6. Animals evolve from plants
  7. Humans arrive as the final step in this chain

He emphasizes that humans did not begin at conception. Rather, conception marks only the beginning of human form. Before that, we existed as our parents' reproductive cells, their blood, the food they ate, and ultimately the plant kingdoms that transformed into their bodies. "We came from vegetable kingdoms," he argues, tracing an unbroken lineage back through billions of years to the infinite source.

Matter is vibration, not solid

Kushi's description of matter aligns with modern physics findings. He argues that matter is "something like a ghost, appearing and disappearing in space"—"nothing but mist or vibration, massive vibration." The video notes that modern physics confirms this: atoms are 99.9% empty space, particles are described as vibrations in invisible fields, and at the deepest level, science finds no solid building block, only energy and vibration.

This extends to the human body. Your body contains trillions of cells, each with trillions of atoms, each atom almost entirely empty space filled with vibrating energy. "So the body you see in the mirror looks solid, but at its deepest level, it's vibration."

Thought as vibration and creation

Kushi argues that thought is nothing but vibration. Every thought you create is a vibration that doesn't stay inside your head—you are constantly sending it out, moment by moment. This is demonstrated through a live exercise where he guides a student to imagine different scenarios (his girlfriend, war, a peaceful forest) while standing with arm extended. The student's physical stability shifts with each mental image, suggesting that thoughts have measurable effects on the body.

He uses a metaphor: your mind is a blue sky, and every thought you create is a cloud. Some are small white clouds, some are long streams, and some are dark clouds covering the whole sky. When you have no creation of image and enter deep meditation, you are connected with the blue sky—the non-relative, absolute world. When you start thinking, clouds appear and you lose that connection.

The implications are profound. When you spend a whole day in worry, that worry goes out as vibration. When you replay an old argument, that anger is sent out. When you think about revenge, that is transmitted. And when you think with gratitude, that goes out the same way.

The illusion of ownership and competition

Kushi challenges the modern obsession with ownership. He argues that nothing truly belongs to us. When you die, you must leave everything behind. A house, money, property—these are not owned but only used temporarily. "Just you illusioned that as if I am owning. Just I'm using it, not owning."

He extends this to wealth: "If he has a million dollars, then he's a millionaire. But actually he doesn't own that." Energy gathers and then disperses. The whole competitive race built around accumulation is therefore built around things nobody truly owns. The house will pass to someone else, the money will move on to other hands.

The enemy is not external

Kushi argues that many people believe sickness comes from outside—from accidents, curses, or enemies attacking. Once you think this way, you see enemies everywhere: viruses, bacteria, even your own inherited DNA. The natural response is to build weapons: medications, injections, chemical sprays.

But he claims there is only one real enemy: yourself—your self-delusion, your abuse of eating, your thinking, your lifestyle. All others are not enemies but friends who came from the universe through the order of nature. If we are weak, we become their food and they invade. If we are strong, they never attack us.

He illustrates this with mosquitoes. If you eat well and maintain strong blood and tissue, mosquitoes avoid you because they know your blood is thick and your tissue is tight. But if you eat poorly, you become "a wonderful, attractive paradise for mosquito." The same logic applies to viruses and bacteria: the condition of the body decides what happens.

Nations and systems as collective illusions

Kushi extends this analysis to nations themselves. He asks: Where does America actually exist? On a map, certainly. But who made the map? Who wrote the national anthem, the Constitution, the states? Every single thing that proves the nation exists came out of human minds. "We think it exists, and that's why we act as if it exists."

A nation is one of the most powerful forces in our lives, yet it holds together only because millions of minds carry the same image and act on it simultaneously. The same mechanism creates enemies, devils, and battlefields. An image is created, shared, believed, and then everyone lives inside it as if it were real.

He lists examples of collective delusions: "Jewish people think we are the chosen race. Chinese people think we are the central race of the world. French people think we are the most refined. The American people think we are the protector of freedom and liberty. Japanese people think we are the descendants of the sun. Illusion, delusion."

The delusional system of modern life

Kushi applies the same critique to the structure of modern life itself. You go through grammar school, high school, college, then ask what job you can get and what salary. You work in a company for decades, and at 65, you retire. "Who made this orientation of life? Because nobody designed it for themselves. We were born into it the same way we were born into a nation."

He finds this deeply problematic. After a journey from the infinite universe, through billions of years of transformation, through the plant kingdom and thousands of forms of life, arriving on Earth as a human being—is the purpose to become a company employee? "In this system, you're selling your life. One hour has a price. One week has a price. One year has a price. For decades, someone buys your time."

He argues that 90 to 99% of modern people are governed by this delusion, working as slaves without knowing it, converting their life into money. And when suffering comes, they blame the devil or some enemy without questioning the system of images they live inside.

Egocentric thinking and the creation of hell

Kushi identifies what he calls egocentric thinking as the root of suffering. This is the belief that "I am the good one, universe number one good guy, no fault." It is arrogance and delusion. "This tiny one is perfect one." When you think this way, you create enemies and involve yourself in a battlefield every day. "This kind of world we call hell. Practically everyone, almost everyone, modern people living in hell. Not living in the paradise."

But paradise, he argues, is not elsewhere. "Paradise or the kingdom of heaven is nothing but this world, this solar system, this galaxy, this entire universe itself. There is nowhere else. But we, by our illusion, make our world imagine like hell."

The path to awakening: Gratitude and love

After explaining all the illusions, Kushi arrives at his conclusion: We have to wake up. He compares the modern world to a nightmare—a world of battlefields, enemies, and endless worry. Like any nightmare, the first step is waking up from it.

When you wake up with clear eyes, you see something different. You see that your parents made your life possible, that your ancestors carried the chain of life to you, that you came through the plant world, through air, water, and land. Everything around you was part of your journey.

Everyone you meet came through the same journey. Every person is a spirit, a manifestation of the universe or God. Everyone is seeking the same things—health, freedom, and happiness—because everyone came from the same source.

Happiness begins with gratitude. "Thank you for the parents. Thank you for the ancestors. Thank you for the plants, the sky, and the stars. Thank you for the food. Thank you for the invisible God." This appreciation felt from the heart is the beginning of happiness and awakening as a real, free human being.

When gratitude becomes deep enough, something interesting happens: you automatically want to share it. When you see a suffering person, you want to help. Nobody has to teach you this. It appears by itself. And that, in Kushi's words, is love. "Brotherhood and sisterhood come naturally out of appreciation."

The role of food and macrobiotics

Kushi believed that food plays a direct role in consciousness and awakening. This was his life's work, called macrobiotics. In his view, what you eat shapes what you think.

He claimed that: - Eating meat feeds images of battles and wars - Sugar and dairy feed sentimentality, laziness, and sadness - Cereal grains like brown rice, wheat, barley, and corn eaten every day clear the mind and open it toward heaven

He pointed out that grains grow upward toward the sky, suggesting a spiritual dimension to dietary choice.

The cage and the key

Kushi's final message is encapsulated in a simple statement: "The delusions are the cage. Gratitude is the door, and the one holding the key is you."
