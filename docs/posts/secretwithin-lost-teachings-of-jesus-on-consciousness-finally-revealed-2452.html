The lost teachings of Jesus on consciousness

Overview

SecretWithin explores the Gospel of Thomas, a collection of Jesus's sayings discovered in 1945 in Nag Hammadi, Egypt, after being hidden for roughly 1,800 years. The creator argues that these teachings were deliberately excluded from the canonical Bible—possibly during Emperor Constantine's religious reforms in 325 AD—because they contained knowledge that could empower people to gain freedom and reshape their reality. Unlike traditional biblical teachings that emphasize looking outward to an external God, the Gospel of Thomas directs practitioners inward, toward consciousness itself. SecretWithin claims this gospel contains three core principles of reality creation that align with ancient hermetic teachings and modern manifestation philosophy.

The three principles of reality creation

Clarity: focus on what you know

SecretWithin interprets Gospel of Thomas saying 2—"Recognize what is in your sight, and that which is hidden from you will become plain to you"—as an instruction to focus on what is known and visible rather than becoming paralyzed by the unknown. The creator argues that humans naturally fear the unknown, and that this fear strengthens when we fixate on it. Instead, he advocates starting with what is already clear and actionable, allowing understanding to unfold as you move forward.

The principle is illustrated through practical examples: a business founder who acts on a single idea and learns as they go; a YouTuber who uploads consistently without knowing how to monetize, only to discover partnership opportunities later. SecretWithin contends that action precedes clarity, not the reverse. When you focus on what you can control now and release attachment to unknowns, the universe (or God, or higher powers) reveals the rest in due time.

Gratitude and the mirror principle

Gospel of Thomas saying 41 states: "Whoever has something in his hand will receive more, and whoever has nothing will be deprived of even the little that he has." SecretWithin interprets this as the law of gratitude and reflection. Reality, he argues, functions like a mirror: your internal state—your thoughts, emotions, and attitude—is reflected back to you in external circumstances.

When you focus on what you have and feel grateful, you attract more abundance. When you focus on lack and scarcity, reality mirrors that back. Optimists, he notes, consistently experience better outcomes not by luck but because their positive attitude shapes how they perceive and interact with reality.

SecretWithin also introduces the concept of attachment and humbling. As people gain success, they often become attached to outcomes—money, clients, views, recognition. This attachment creates an imbalance in the "matrix" (the quantum field of reality). Life then responds with tests or setbacks to humble the person and restore balance. Once attachment loosens and gratitude returns, opportunities realign and problems resolve.

He illustrates this with a personal anecdote: a friend who wanted marriage attended a self-development course where he was asked to imagine his life if he never married. By accepting that possibility and letting go of desperate attachment, he shifted his internal state. Three months later, he reconnected with someone from high school and eventually married. The shift came not from wanting, but from acceptance.

SecretWithin references Greg Braden's The Divine Matrix, arguing that human thoughts and feelings create disturbances in the quantum field, similar to how mass bends gravitational fields in physics. When humanity operates from fear—fear of recession, loss, uncertainty—it creates an undesirable disturbance that attracts negative circumstances. The ego, he explains, is not inherently bad; it is the set of identities we adopt in different contexts. The question is whether we consciously choose our thoughts and emotions or let them choose us.

Unity of heart and mind

SecretWithin presents the third and most powerful principle: the alignment of heart and mind. He argues that miracles occur when intention (mind) and feeling (heart) move together as one. This principle appears repeatedly in the Gospel of Thomas through sayings like "When you make the two one, when you make the inside like the outside and the outside like the inside, then you will enter the kingdom" and "If two make peace with each other in one house, they will say to the mountain, Move, and it will move."

Modern society, SecretWithin contends, lives almost entirely in the mind—logic, reasoning, productivity, achievement—while creativity, intuition, and heart are pushed aside. This imbalance prevents the "two" from becoming one.

Fear is presented as a potent example of unified heart and mind: when you fear something, you feel it emotionally and think about it constantly. The feeling and thought align, creating a powerful imprint in the divine matrix that shifts you into a corresponding version of reality. Conversely, when you align your heart and mind around what you desire—not what you fear—you create a positive imprint.

SecretWithin uses the example of a YouTuber's self-image. If a creator believes they are bad at making videos, that belief shapes their thoughts (I struggle), their emotions (doubt, hesitation), and their actions, resulting in videos that reflect that identity. But if the same creator chooses to embody the identity of a capable, growing creator, their thoughts, emotions, and decisions shift, and over time their results match that new identity.

This connects to the concept of "fake it till you make it"—stepping into the shoes of an ideal self and embodying that version as best as possible now, even if not yet comfortable. SecretWithin references psycho-cybernetics, the idea that self-image shapes behavior, and notes that even physical transformations like plastic surgery often follow an internal shift in self-perception.

The removed prayer and operating from the end state

SecretWithin references a prayer removed from the Bible, originally in ancient Aramaic (John 16:23), which he argues Greg Braden has discussed. The prayer states: "All things that you ask straightly and directly in my name you shall be given. Ask without hidden motive and be enveloped by your answer that your gladness be full."

He breaks this into three parts:

  1. Ask straightly and directly: Focus on what you want to move toward, not what you want to avoid. Most people spend energy thinking about problems and escape routes rather than envisioning a clear desired outcome.

  2. Ask without hidden motive: Do not judge desires as possible or impossible. Judgment fixes energy and closes you off. Openness and receptivity create space to receive.

  3. Be enveloped by what you desire: Operate from the end state, as if you already have what you want. Feel it fully and immerse yourself in it. SecretWithin credits Neville Goddard with this concept and frames it as imprinting your intention into the matrix and shifting into a parallel reality.

The key question is: Who am I being when I already have this? At every stage, consciously embody the person you must be to experience the reality you want.

The mirror principle and maintaining alignment

SecretWithin reiterates that reality is a reflection of your thoughts and feelings, though there is often a delay between intention and manifestation. During that delay, your task is to maintain alignment—your attitude, focus, and the unity of heart and mind. How you coordinate through those periods determines how effectively you shape your reality.

He frames reality as responsive, not fixed. The subtleties of the mirror principle—the small shifts in attention and feeling—are where the magic happens.

Conclusion

SecretWithin concludes that all teachings from the Gospel of Thomas reduce to one truth: your reality begins with you—your thoughts, feelings, and the identity you choose to embody. By aligning heart and mind, asking clearly without judgment, and immersing yourself in the feeling of what you desire, you can shift the matrix and step into the life you want.
