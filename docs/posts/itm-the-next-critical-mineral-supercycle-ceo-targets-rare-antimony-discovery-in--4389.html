Antimony, a critical mineral overlooked for years, faces a surge in demand as China tightens exports and North America eyes a new supply breakthrough.

The Antimony supply crisis

China dominates global antimony supply and has recently suspended exports to the U.S., with the temporary relief set to expire in November. This has triggered a supply shortfall, with an estimated 30,000 tons per year deficit in antimony production.

Market demand and usage

Antimony is primarily used in military equipment and flame retardants. Its demand is inelastic—once a government decides it needs the metal, price becomes irrelevant. It is also gaining use in the solar sector, though military applications remain the largest.

Mining and geology

Antimony is mined using the same methods as gold and silver—open pit or underground operations. It is often associated with gold deposits. The NSJ Antimony Core project in New Brunswick targets a primary antimony deposit, with up to 90% antimony content, distinct from gold-rich deposits.

The project is located in the same geological package as the historic Lake George Mine, which was once North America's only primary antimony producer. The mine is now flooded with water and would require extensive treatment to reclaim, making a revival environmentally and economically challenging.

Project background and leadership

The company, NSJ Antimony Corp (NSJ.cn), acquired land from Dallas Davies, a geologist with direct experience in the Lake George feasibility study and prior work at Falconbridge. He now works for the company and brings deep antimony sector knowledge.

Rodney Stevens, CEO, has a background in mining finance and has raised $4 million in sponsorship, including support from Delbrook Asset Management ($5 billion fund) and Sprott. A group linked to the Almonte project also backs the initiative.

Key milestones and timeline

  • If an ore-grade intercept is found, the company will target that area for economic concentration.
  • Over the next 1–2 years: A major ore body could be drilled.

Investment and scale

A single deposit of 3,000 to 5,000 tons per year could generate $50 to $100 million in annual cash flow. While this would not fully satisfy the 30,000-ton supply gap, it could represent one of many deposits to be developed.

Multiple deposits across the property could be fed from a single processing facility, enabling scalable production.

Key consumers

The U.S. is the most aggressive consumer, followed by Canada and Japan. Japan may pursue joint ventures with U.S. or Canadian operators.

Final insight

The project is positioned at the frontier of a potential new primary antimony deposit. If large enough and located in one of the safest mining environments, it could become one of the rarest and most strategically significant antimony mines in the world.
