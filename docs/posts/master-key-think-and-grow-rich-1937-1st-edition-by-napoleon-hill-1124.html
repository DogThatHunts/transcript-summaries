Master Key | Think And Grow Rich! (1937 - 1st Edition) by Napoleon Hill

Napoleon Hill's classic guide outlines thirteen principles of personal achievement, demonstrating how burning desire, structured planning, and mental discipline can transmute thoughts into material wealth.

Publisher's and author's prefaces

Napoleon Hill introduces the core premise of the book: a secret formula for wealth and achievement originally passed to him by industrialist Andrew Carnegie. Carnegie challenged Hill to spend over twenty years researching and analyzing more than 500 of America's most successful self-made men—including Henry Ford, Thomas Edison, and Charles M. Schwab—to codify their methods into a universal philosophy. Hill emphasizes that this secret cannot be simply given away or bought; it must be discovered by those who are ready and actively searching for it.

Chapter 1: introduction

Thoughts are things

Thoughts possess immense power when mixed with a definiteness of purpose, persistence, and a burning desire to translate them into material riches.

  • The partnership of Edwin C. Barnes and Thomas Edison: Barnes arrived at Edison's laboratory looking like a tramp but carrying a singular, unwavering desire to become Edison's business partner, not just his employee. He accepted menial work to get his foot in the door. When Edison's sales force failed to see the potential in the new Edison Dictating Machine, Barnes recognized his opportunity, successfully marketed the device, and secured a nationwide distribution contract, proving that one can literally "think and grow rich."
  • Three feet from gold: R.U. Darby's uncle abandoned a promising Colorado gold mine after the vein disappeared, selling his machinery to a junkman for a pittance. The junkman consulted a mining engineer, who calculated that the vein would resume exactly three feet from where the Darbys had stopped drilling. The junkman made millions because he sought expert counsel before quitting. Darby later used this lesson to become a highly successful life insurance salesman, refusing to accept "no" as a final answer.
  • The fifty-cent lesson in persistence: A small child, the daughter of a tenant farmer, defied Darby's uncle in his own mill, refusing to leave without fifty cents her mother needed. Despite the uncle threatening her with a barrel stave, the child stood her ground and screamed her demand until the uncle capitulated, demonstrating the irresistible power of absolute determination.

Chapter 2: desire

The starting point of all achievement

Desire is not a mere wish or hope; it must be an all-consuming obsession backed by definite plans and relentless persistence.

Six steps to transmute desire into riches

  1. Fix the exact amount: Determine the precise sum of money you desire.
  2. Determine the exchange: Decide exactly what you intend to give in return for this money.
  3. Establish a date: Set a definite date by which you intend to possess the money.
  4. Create a plan: Formulate a practical plan and begin executing it immediately, whether you feel ready or not.
  5. Write it down: Write a clear, concise statement of the amount, the time limit, the exchange, and the plan.
  6. Read it aloud: Read your written statement aloud twice daily—once before bed and once upon waking—visualizing yourself already in possession of the money.

Overcoming physical limitations

Hill recounts the story of his son, Blair, who was born without any physical ears or natural hearing equipment. Refusing to accept the doctor's prognosis that the child would be a deaf-mute, Hill relentlessly planted the desire to hear and speak into his son's mind. Through bedtime stories designed to frame his handicap as a unique asset, Blair developed immense self-reliance. He eventually discovered that clamping his teeth onto a Victrola allowed him to hear music via bone conduction. Years later, Blair tried a newly developed electrical hearing aid that miraculously restored his hearing to near-normal levels. He then partnered with the manufacturer to dedicate his life to helping other deafened individuals, successfully converting his physical handicap into a highly lucrative and fulfilling career.

Chapter 3: faith

Visualization of, and belief in attainment of desire

Faith is a state of mind induced or created by repeated affirmations and instructions to the subconscious mind through auto-suggestion. It is the only known antidote for failure and the element that transforms ordinary thought vibrations into their spiritual equivalent.

The self-confidence formula

  • First: Acknowledge your ability to achieve your definite purpose, and demand persistent, continuous action from yourself.
  • Second: Realize that your dominating thoughts will eventually reproduce themselves in outward physical action; dedicate thirty minutes daily to visualizing the person you intend to become.
  • Third: Spend ten minutes daily demanding the development of self-confidence through auto-suggestion.
  • Fourth: Write down a clear description of your definite chief aim in life and never stop trying to attain it.
  • Fifth: Engage only in transactions that benefit all participants, eliminating hatred, envy, and selfishness by developing a love for humanity.

The creation of united states steel

Charles M. Schwab converted a simple idea into a $600 million transaction when he delivered a historic speech at the University Club in New York in 1900. Schwab envisioned a massive consolidation of the steel industry based on efficiency, specialization, and expanding the market by lowering costs, rather than raising prices to create a monopoly. His eloquence and structured plan convinced J.P. Morgan to finance the merger, which included purchasing Andrew Carnegie's massive steel holdings for $400 million, proving that a single, well-marketed idea backed by faith can generate immense wealth.

Chapter 4: autosuggestion

The medium for influencing the subconscious mind

Auto-suggestion is self-suggestion. It acts as the communication link between the conscious thinking mind and the subconscious. The conscious mind serves as a guard, filtering the sensory impressions that reach the subconscious.

  • Emotionalizing thoughts: Plain, unemotional words do not influence the subconscious mind. To achieve results, you must learn to mix your spoken words and desires with deep feeling, belief, and absolute faith.
  • The price of influence: Reaching and influencing your subconscious requires persistent, daily practice. You must concentrate on your visualized desire with closed eyes until you can physically "see" the money you intend to accumulate, expecting your subconscious to hand over the practical plans to acquire it.

Chapter 5: specialized knowledge

Personal experiences or observations

Knowledge is only potential power. It becomes actual power only when organized into definite plans of action and directed to a definite end. General academic knowledge, such as that held by university professors, rarely attracts great wealth because it is unorganized and undirected.

  • Henry Ford's push-button logic: During a libel suit, a newspaper's attorneys tried to prove Ford was ignorant by asking him obscure historical questions. Ford famously replied that he had a row of electric push-buttons on his desk that could instantly summon specialists to answer any question, freeing his own mind to focus on the business of manufacturing.
  • Acquiring and marketing knowledge: You do not need to personally possess all the specialized knowledge required to build a fortune; you can bridge this gap by allying yourself with a Mastermind group. Hill highlights the success of Stuart Austin Weir, who went back to school at age forty to become a highly successful corporation lawyer, and an unemployed grocery clerk who studied accounting and established a lucrative mobile bookkeeping business for small merchants.

Chapter 6: imagination

The workshop of the mind

The imaginative faculty functions in two forms: * Synthetic imagination: Reorganizes existing concepts, ideas, or plans into new combinations. This is the primary tool used by inventors and planners. * Creative imagination: Connects the finite mind of man directly with Infinite Intelligence. It is the faculty through which hunches, inspirations, and basic new ideas are received.

  • The enchanted kettle: Asa Candler, a young drug clerk, bought an old brass kettle, a wooden paddle, and a secret recipe from an old country doctor for his life savings of $500. The doctor was glad to be rid of it, but Candler added the missing ingredient—imagination. By developing a brilliant marketing plan, Candler turned that simple recipe into the global Coca-Cola empire.
  • Gunsaulus's million-dollar sermon: Frank W. Gunsaulus, a young clergyman, spent nearly two years visualizing his dream of founding an educational institution where students could "learn by doing." On a Saturday afternoon, he made a firm decision to raise the necessary million dollars within a week. He delivered a sermon titled "What I Would Do If I Had a Million Dollars" with such conviction that industrialist Philip D. Armour stepped forward afterward and presented him with the money, establishing the Armour Institute of Technology.

Chapter 7: organized planning

The crystallization of desire into action

To translate desire into reality, you must build practical, faultless plans. If your first plan fails, you must replace it with a new one. Temporary defeat is not permanent failure; it is merely a signal that your plans were not sound.

The QQS formula for personal services

To market your personal services permanently and at a high price, you must apply the QQS formula: * Quality: Render every detail of your position with the highest possible efficiency. * Quantity: Maintain the habit of rendering all the service of which you are capable. * Spirit: Maintain an agreeable, harmonious, and cooperative attitude to foster teamwork with your associates.

The 30 major causes of failure

Hill lists the primary reasons why ninety-eight percent of people fail to achieve success, which include a lack of a well-defined purpose, lack of ambition, insufficient self-discipline, ill health, procrastination, lack of persistence, a negative personality, indecision, overcaution, wrong selection of a marriage partner or business associates, and intolerance.

Chapter 8: decision

The mastery of procrastination

An analysis of over 25,000 men and women who experienced failure revealed that lack of decision was near the head of the list. Conversely, every person who accumulated a fortune beyond the million-dollar mark shared the habit of reaching decisions promptly and changing them very slowly.

  • The danger of opinions: People who fail are easily influenced by the opinions of others, permitting gossip and newspapers to do their thinking. Keep your own counsel and make your own decisions.
  • The courage of the founders: The signing of the Declaration of Independence on July 4, 1776, was one of the most courageous decisions in human history. The fifty-six signers knew that their decision would either bring freedom to America or result in their execution for high treason. Their prompt, definite decision magnetized the hearts of the colonists and ensured the eventual victory of George Washington's armies.

Chapter 9: persistence

The sustained effort necessary to induce faith

Persistence is to the character of man what carbon is to steel. The ease with which you can maintain persistence depends entirely on the intensity of your desire.

  • Fanny Hurst's triumph: Author Fanny Hurst arrived in New York in 1915 to convert her writing into riches. She received thirty-six rejection slips from The Saturday Evening Post before she finally broke through. Her relentless persistence eventually forced publishers to seek her out, resulting in massive book sales and film rights.
  • The four steps to cultivate persistence: 1. A definite purpose backed by a burning desire. 2. A definite plan expressed in continuous action. 3. A mind closed tightly against all negative and discouraging influences. 4. A friendly alliance with one or more persons who will encourage you to follow through.

Chapter 10: power of the mastermind

The driving force

Power is organized and intelligently directed knowledge. No individual can accumulate great power or wealth without the cooperation of others.

  • The Mastermind defined: The coordination of knowledge and effort, in a spirit of harmony, between two or more people for the attainment of a definite purpose.
  • The psychic phase: When two minds coordinate in harmony, they create a third, invisible, intangible force that functions like a third mind.
  • Henry Ford's alliances: Ford overcame poverty, illiteracy, and ignorance by allying himself with great minds like Thomas Edison, Harvey Firestone, John Burroughs, and Luther Burbank, absorbing their intelligence and spiritual forces into his own brain-power.

Chapter 11: the mystery of sex transmutation

The tenth step toward riches

Sex desire is the most powerful of all human desires. When driven by this urge, men develop a keenness of imagination, courage, and willpower unknown to them at other times. Sex transmutation involves redirecting this immense energy from thoughts of physical expression into creative, artistic, or financial pursuits.

  • Why men seldom succeed before forty: Hill's analysis of over 25,000 people showed that men rarely achieve outstanding success before forty because they habitually dissipate their energies through overindulgence in the physical expression of sex. The capacity to transmute this energy into creative work typically matures between forty and sixty.
  • The power of personal magnetism: Personal magnetism is nothing more than sex energy. Highly sexed individuals possess a plentiful supply of this force, which can be communicated to others through the handshake, tone of voice, posture, and thought vibrations to highly influence people.

Chapter 12: the subconscious mind

The connecting link

The subconscious mind acts as a filing cabinet, recording every thought, sensory impression, and mental impulse that reaches the conscious mind. It works continuously, translating desires into their physical equivalent by drawing upon the forces of Infinite Intelligence.

  • The seven major positive emotions: Desire, faith, love, sex, enthusiasm, romance, and hope. These must dominate your mind to successfully influence your subconscious.
  • The seven major negative emotions: Fear, jealousy, hatred, revenge, greed, superstition, and anger. These must be actively avoided, as a single negative emotion can destroy your subconscious mind's capacity for constructive action.

Chapter 13: the brain

A broadcasting and receiving station for thought

The human brain is both a broadcasting and receiving station for the vibration of thought. When stimulated by strong emotions, the brain's vibration rate increases, making it highly receptive to thoughts released by other brains through the ether.

  • Professor Rhine's telepathy experiments: Hill cites the pioneering research of Dr. J.B. Rhine at Duke University, which provided scientific evidence for extrasensory perception, telepathy, and clairvoyance, demonstrating that the human mind can communicate across vast distances without physical sensory organs.

Chapter 14: the sixth sense

The door to the temple of wisdom

The thirteenth principle is the Sixth Sense, which is the creative imagination or the receiving set of the brain. It is the point at which the finite mind of man contacts the universal mind, allowing hunches, inspirations, and warnings of danger to flash into consciousness.

  • The Invisible Counselors: Hill describes his long-standing habit of holding nightly imaginary cabinet meetings with nine historical figures he admired: Ralph Waldo Emerson, Thomas Paine, Thomas Edison, Charles Darwin, Abraham Lincoln, Luther Burbank, Napoleon Bonaparte, Henry Ford, and Andrew Carnegie. By visualizing these meetings, Hill successfully rebuilt his own character, absorbing the distinct virtues and wisdom of his counselors into his subconscious mind.

Chapter 15: how to outwit the six ghosts of fear

Preparing the mind for success

Before you can apply this philosophy, you must rid your mind of three closely related negatives: indecision, doubt, and fear. Indecision is the seedling of fear, which crystallizes into doubt, eventually blending to become fear.

The six basic fears

  1. The fear of poverty: The most destructive fear, arising from man's inherited tendency to prey upon his fellow man economically. It paralyzes reason, destroys ambition, and invites failure.
  2. The fear of criticism: Destroys initiative and prevents the execution of plans due to worry over what others will think or say.
  3. The fear of ill health: Often produces the actual physical symptoms of the disease feared through negative suggestion and hypochondria.
  4. The fear of loss of love: The most painful fear, often leading to extreme jealousy and mental distress.
  5. The fear of old age: Closely associated with the fear of poverty and the dread of losing physical and economic freedom.
  6. The fear of death: Often caused by religious fanaticism and the dread of eternal punishment.

The devil's workshop

The seventh basic evil is susceptibility to negative influences. To protect yourself, you must exercise your willpower to build a wall of immunity in your mind, close your doors to negative people, and take absolute control of your thoughts. You have control over only one thing on earth—your mind—and this divine prerogative is the sole means by which you can shape your destiny.
