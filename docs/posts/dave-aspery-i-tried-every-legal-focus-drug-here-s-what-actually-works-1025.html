The biological basis of focus

Dave Asprey argues that cognitive performance and focus are not matters of willpower or discipline, but are downstream of physical energy production in the brain. Rather than blaming oneself for a lack of motivation, Asprey suggests addressing the biological systems—specifically mitochondrial function—that generate cellular energy.

Caffeine and L-theanine: smoothing the stimulant curve

While coffee is a powerful performance and longevity tool, Asprey notes that caffeine does not create new energy; instead, it "borrows" energy by temporarily blocking adenosine receptors. When the caffeine wears off, the accumulated adenosine floods the brain, causing an afternoon crash.

  • The fix: Asprey recommends combining coffee with L-theanine, an amino acid found in green tea. He claims this combination eliminates jitters, prevents cortisol spikes, and averts the afternoon crash.
  • Quality matters: Asprey advocates for mold-free coffee (promoting his brand, Danger Coffee) to avoid performance-sapping toxins.

Adaptogens: buffering background stress

Chronic stress and elevated cortisol levels act as a heavy weight on cognitive function. Asprey highlights adaptogens like ashwagandha and rhodiola as tools to help the nervous system recover quickly from stress.

  • The dopamine connection: Asprey points out that rhodiola is particularly effective because the biological building blocks for cortisol are also used to produce dopamine, which drives motivation.

Medium-chain triglycerides (MCT) oil: alternative brain fuel

While the brain can run on glucose, Asprey argues that ketones derived from high-quality MCT oil provide a more stable and efficient fuel source for neurons. Consuming MCT oil helps maintain steady energy levels and focus throughout the morning, even if dietary carbohydrates are present.

Methylene blue: direct mitochondrial enhancement

Unlike stimulants that force existing cellular machinery to work harder, methylene blue acts directly inside the mitochondria to facilitate electron transport and increase ATP production. Asprey describes it as physically expanding cellular energy capacity. Because of its potency, he advises reserving methylene blue for demanding days rather than daily use.

Creatine: the misunderstood cognitive battery

Asprey asserts that creatine is highly underrated as a brain supplement due to its reputation as a bodybuilding aid. The prefrontal cortex is incredibly energy-hungry, and creatine directly supports the replenishment of cellular energy.

  • Cognitive benefits: Asprey cites double-blind research showing that creatine improves working memory and can completely eliminate cognitive deficits caused by sleep deprivation.
  • Dosing and preparation: For cognitive benefits, Asprey recommends a daily maintenance dose of 10 to 15 grams (significantly higher than the standard 3 to 5 grams used for muscle growth). He emphasizes dissolving creatine completely in a hot liquid (such as hot coffee or tea) to maximize absorption, prevent stomach irritation, and help it cross the blood-brain barrier.

The ultimate focus stack

To achieve sustained, frictionless focus, Asprey suggests stacking these compounds systematically:

  1. Base: Coffee combined with L-theanine to smoothly stimulate the nervous system.
  2. Fuel: MCT oil added to the coffee to stabilize brain fuel.
  3. Backup battery: A high dose of creatine dissolved in the hot beverage to maximize cellular energy reserves.
