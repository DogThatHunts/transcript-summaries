The mechanics of the global diesel and crude oil markets

In analyzing global energy, Doomberg explains that the global seaborne "jump ball" market for diesel is roughly 7 million barrels per day (out of a total 28 million barrels produced and consumed globally). However, Doomberg advises that the high prices for diesel may soon peak. High margins are incentivizing global refiners to maximize diesel output, which will eventually lead to a supply glut. Furthermore, Doomberg explains the apparent paradox of crude oil prices falling despite refinery attacks: because refineries are the only natural buyers of crude oil, destroying refining capacity reduces the immediate demand for crude, forcing Brent and WTI prices down.

In this environment, Doomberg advises investors to focus on acquiring real, physical assets and utilizing fixed-rate debt to purchase productive infrastructure.
