Market dynamics and regulatory shifts

RE-V emphasizes that corporate investors dominated single-family rentals for years, but regulatory pressure is now forcing an exit. The executive order and Senate bill represent a policy shift away from institutional ownership. RE-V argues this is "great news if you're a potential buyer" because competition from deep-pocketed firms is ending.

However, RE-V notes that overall U.S. housing prices remain at inflation-adjusted all-time highs on a national basis, with year-over-year values flat. The correction is uneven: some regions like Buckhead and Sandy Springs in Atlanta are forecast to appreciate 3–4%, while others are projected to fall 5–10%.

The accidental landlord trap

RE-V highlights a Zillow report showing a surge in "accidental landlords"—homeowners who cannot sell and are renting instead. This is presented as a warning sign. Years ago, owners with sub-3% mortgage rates could profit from rentals, but now over 20% of U.S. mortgage holders carry 6%+ rates. Rising property taxes, insurance, and mortgage costs make it uneconomical for these owners to rent out properties, especially if rental rates stagnate or decline.

RE-V's own purchase was from a seller in this situation: the owner had cut rent and could not achieve positive cash flow, so they sold at a steep discount. RE-V predicts more forced sales as operating costs rise and rents fail to keep pace.

Buying strategy for 2026

RE-V outlines a four-step approach to securing discounts:

  1. Target areas with downward price forecasts. Use tools like ReVenture's 12-month forecast to identify neighborhoods where sellers will be most willing to negotiate.

  2. Focus on long-listed properties. Properties on the market for six months or more (measured from initial listing, not Zillow reset dates) indicate motivated sellers.

  3. Signal seriousness early. Provide mortgage pre-approval documentation or proof of cash liquidity to the listing agent immediately, demonstrating intent to transact.

  4. Make aggressive offers without shame. RE-V emphasizes not feeling awkward about offering 25% below list price in declining markets. If the seller rejects, wait and let the property sit; desperation kills deals. Discipline and willingness to walk away are essential.

RE-V stresses that list price is negotiable and many sellers will not advertise willingness to discount. The key is targeting the right neighborhoods and properties, then making a serious, low offer and holding firm.
