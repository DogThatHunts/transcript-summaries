Master Key | The Edinburgh Lectures on Mental Science (1904) by Thomas Troward

An exploration of early New Thought philosophy, detailing how the interaction between the objective and subjective minds allows individuals to harness universal spiritual laws for physical and mental transformation.

The relationship between spirit and matter

Thomas Troward begins by establishing the fundamental distinction between spirit and matter. While physical science reveals that even seemingly inert matter (such as a solid lump of steel) is in a state of intense atomic vibration, Troward argues that the true distinction lies in the degree of intelligence.

  • Spirit is thought: The distinctive quality of spirit is thought, which is free from the limitations of time and space. It exists in a universal "here" and an everlasting "now."
  • Matter is form: The distinctive quality of matter is form, which inherently implies extension in space and limitation within boundaries.

Realizing spirit as pure thought allows individuals to conceive of it as existing independently of physical conditions. Troward cautions that extreme idealism (ignoring the relative) and extreme materialism (ignoring the absolute) are both erroneous; true reality exists in the combination of both the inside (spirit) and the outside (form).

The higher mode of intelligence controls the lower

Troward outlines a cosmic evolutionary scheme that progresses from simple, automated existence to highly individualized self-recognition.

  • The law of averages: In lower stages of evolution, nature operates by a law of averages to preserve the species, showing little regard for the single life.
  • Individual selection: As intelligence advances, the individual is increasingly liberated from the law of averages, gaining the power to control the conditions of their own survival through individual volition.
  • Conscious cooperation: Because humans represent the highest product of this evolutionary law, further human evolution cannot happen automatically. It requires conscious cooperation and individual effort to align with the universal law.

Troward asserts that a paramount cosmic intelligence underlies the universe. Because nature obeys us in proportion to how we first obey nature, understanding these spiritual laws allows the individual's mind to control lower, less self-conscious modes of spirit.

The mathematical unity of the spirit

Because the originating life principle is infinite, Troward reasons mathematically that it must be a single, indivisible unity.

  • Omnipresence: Since the infinite cannot be divided into fractions or limited by another entity, the entirety of the universal spirit must be present at every point in space simultaneously.
  • Impersonal susceptibility: In its undifferentiated state, this universal spirit is pure, impersonal intelligence. Because it is highly susceptible and responsive, humans can impress their own recognition of personality upon it to produce outward, visible results.

The dual nature of the human mind

Drawing on the contemporary scientific acceptance of hypnotic phenomena, Troward models the human mind as a dual mechanism consisting of the objective and subjective minds.

  • The objective mind: The intellectual, volitional outer mind that perceives the physical world through the senses. It is capable of both inductive reasoning (inferring general principles from observed facts) and deductive reasoning.
  • The subjective mind: The inner mind that reasons only deductively. It is incapable of inductive selection or comparison and will accept any suggestion impressed upon it by the objective mind as absolute truth, working it out to its most rigorous logical consequences.

The subjective mind as the body builder

Troward asserts that the subjective mind is the actual builder and maintainer of the physical body. Because it accepts the beliefs of the objective mind without question, physical health or sickness is ultimately the externalization of the aggregate of an individual's beliefs. If an individual firmly believes they are subject to uncontrollable external diseases, the subjective mind will fashion the body to reflect that vulnerability.

The mechanics of mental creation and growth

To bridge the gap between the universal absolute (the region of causes) and the particular relative (the region of conditions), Troward instructs students to practice "thinking in the absolute."

  • The spiritual prototype: Every material form begins as a spiritual prototype or archetypal idea. To manifest a desire, one must form its spiritual prototype in the subjective mind, treating the desire as an already accomplished fact in the present moment.
  • The law of attraction: Once this mental nucleus is established, it begins to exercise an attractive force, drawing to itself the material and conditions necessary for its physical manifestation.
  • The law of growth: Troward emphasizes that growth is a natural, quiet process inherent in the spiritual seed. Attempting to force results through anxiety, hurry, or willpower reverses the positive cause, planting a negative prototype of doubt that neutralizes the original desire.

Receptivity and reciprocal action

Troward describes the universal mind as an ocean of undifferentiated, highly responsive life that permeates all space. It possesses "personalness" (the qualities of personality, such as intelligence and volition, without separate individuality) and reflects back to us exactly what we project onto it:

  • If we view the world as governed by blind chance, it presents itself as chaos.
  • If we recognize universal law, we find a reliable, calculable order.
  • If we recognize its personal, responsive nature, it enters into a reciprocal relationship of companionship and guidance with us.

To utilize this, Troward advises alternating mental states. When seeking guidance, the individual should make their own mind receptive to the superior wisdom of the universal mind. When seeking to manifest a condition, the individual must direct the universal mind by impressing a specific personal desire upon its impersonal, creative energy.

Primary causes versus secondary conditions

Troward warns against judging solely by the outward senses, which only perceive secondary causes (the endless chain of existing conditions).

  • Conditions are not causes: Conditions are merely reflections of past thoughts.
  • Operating in the absolute: To escape limiting environments, one must rise above secondary conditions and operate in the region of primary causes within the self, maintaining the ideal image without making its fulfillment contingent on any past, present, or future circumstances.

Intuition, healing, and the will

Intuition

Because the subjective mind exists on a plane free from time and space, it can access information removed from the objective senses. Troward notes that an individual's very first impression of a situation is usually the most accurate reflection of this intuitive faculty before the objective mind begins to argue from external appearances.

Healing

Mental healing relies on substituting the healer's active, healthy mental suggestions for the patient's false beliefs in sickness. By establishing a "rapport"—temporarily removing the barriers of separate personality—the healer speaks directly to the patient's subjective mind. Troward notes that absent treatment (such as during sleep) is highly effective because it bypasses the patient's conscious, intellectual opposition.

The will

Troward clarifies that the will is not the creative faculty itself; rather, the imagination creates, while the will acts as a centralizing tool (like a tool holder in a lathe) to keep the imagination focused in the right direction. True willpower is not nervous strain, but a calm, peaceful determination to maintain a chosen mental attitude.

The physical, mental, and spiritual connection

Troward provides physiological correspondences to demonstrate the connection between the visible and invisible worlds.

  • The dual nervous system: The cerebrospinal system (centered in the brain) is the organ of the conscious mind, while the sympathetic system (centered in the solar plexus) is the organ of the subconscious mind. The vagus nerve physically connects these two systems, making the human being a single, integrated entity.
  • The biometer experiments: Troward cites Dr. Hippolyte Baraduke's biometer experiments as concrete proof that changing one's mental attitude directly modifies the polarization and flow of vital physical currents.
  • The ultimate of evolution: The supreme, originating spirit is characterized by the eternal interaction of love (its active polarity) and beauty (its passive polarity). The ultimate goal of evolution is to develop individual wills that act as conscious, harmonious vehicles for this outflowing universal spirit.
