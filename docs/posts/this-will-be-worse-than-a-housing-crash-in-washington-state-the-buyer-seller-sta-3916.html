This Will Be WORSE Than a Housing Crash in Washington State | The Buyer-Seller Standoff Explained

Washington’s housing market is caught in a buyer‑seller standoff where soaring mortgage payments and stagnant prices leave most families unable to afford a home.

Summary

Leah Courage explains that Washington state is experiencing a buyer‑seller standoff: home prices have risen only modestly since 2021, but mortgage rates have more than doubled, pushing monthly payments from about $1,900 to $3,470. With inventory up 24% and buyer activity down 40%, many sellers cannot find buyers, and many buyers cannot afford the higher payments, creating a prolonged impasse that could last years.

Key insights

  • Payment disparity – A median home now costs $625,000, but at a 7.43% rate the monthly principal‑and‑interest is $3,470, almost double the 2021 payment (Leah Courage).
  • Supply vs demand – Listings are up 24.5% year‑over‑year while contracts are down 40%, leaving many homes on the market (Leah Courage).
  • Equity buffer prevents crash – Most Washington owners hold significant equity, so even job loss rarely forces a forced sale, removing the key ingredient of a traditional crash (Leah Courage).
  • Affordability gap – To afford the median home today a household needs roughly $177,000 annual income, about 78% more than the state median of $99,389 (Leah Courage).
  • Policy uncertainty – Senate Bill 5798 could raise property‑tax growth from 1% to inflation‑plus‑population rates, adding thousands to owners’ yearly costs (Leah Courage).

The payment gap

In September 2021 a buyer with 20% down on a $570,000 home paid about $1,900/month at sub‑3% rates. Today the same down‑payment on a $625,000 home at 7.43% costs roughly $3,470/month – an 83% increase despite only a 10% rise in home price.

Inventory surge and demand drop

The Northwest MLS listed 24,965 homes in September 2026, 24.5% more than a year earlier, while the number of contracts fell about 40% from the 2021 peak. More homes and fewer buyers normally push prices down, but Washington’s equity cushion has stalled that effect.

Why a crash hasn't happened

A classic crash requires sellers forced to accept any price. In Washington, most owners sit on “theoretical equity,” allowing them to list instead of foreclose, so the market lacks the panic‑selling pressure seen in 2008.

Who can afford now

  • Minimum household income needed: ~$177,000/year (including $125,000 down‑payment).
  • Typical Washington household earns $99,389, leaving a large affordability gap.
  • Two buyer types remain: (1) cash‑rich owners with equity who can move without selling, and (2) high‑income dual‑income households near the top of the pay scale.

Ongoing costs for sellers

Even owners who have paid off their mortgage face rising expenses: insurance premiums rose 54% from 2023‑2025, property taxes in King County jumped ~10% in 2026, and inflation adds 3‑4% yearly to living costs, eroding real equity.

Policy and legislative backdrop

Washington caps property‑tax growth at 1% annually. Senate Bill 5798 (introduced by Senate Majority Leader Jamie Peterson) would tie the cap to inflation + population growth, potentially adding $1,700/year per $625,000 home over a decade. The bill died in the 2025 session but may be revived in 2027.
