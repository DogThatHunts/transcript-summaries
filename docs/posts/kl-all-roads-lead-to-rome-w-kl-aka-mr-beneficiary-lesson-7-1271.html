The historical roots of feudalism and land trusts

KL describes how feudalism arose in Europe as a decentralized system of protection. Individuals surrendered personal liberties to powerful lords in exchange for safety. This relationship was formalized through commendation ceremonies, establishing vassals and "fiefs" (land grants).

KL emphasizes that vassals never held true legal title to their land; the lord retained the superior right to seize or "escheat" the property if the vassal proved unfaithful. He draws a direct parallel to modern property taxes and eminent domain, asserting that modern landowners only hold equitable title while the state retains legal title.

A central pillar of KL's thesis is the private contract of 1213 between King John of England and Pope Innocent III. To redeem his soul, King John granted all of his present and future land holdings to the Catholic Church in perpetuity, receiving it back as a feudal tenant.
