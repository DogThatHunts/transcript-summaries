The linguistic trap of legal definitions

Sayles opens the discussion by referencing James 1:8 ("a double-minded man is unstable in all his ways"), arguing that the modern legal system deliberately induces double-mindedness through "equivocation." This technique involves assigning legal definitions to common words that are 180 degrees opposite to their colloquial meanings.

For example, Sayles explains that a "person" in legal terms is not merely a human being, but an entity to whom the law ascribes rights and duties. Under the 14th Amendment, a "person" is ascribed civil rights but also owes correlative duties like income tax. Similarly, "property" is legally defined as a right of ownership rather than a physical object, and "resident" originates from ambassadorial law, signifying which system of law an individual is under rather than where they physically live. By answering "yes" to questions regarding citizenship and residency without understanding these legal definitions, Sayles claims that individuals unknowingly give their consent to be governed under federal administrative jurisdiction.

The historical roots of modern federal servitude

According to Sayles, the Civil War was primarily fought to introduce the 13th and 14th Amendments, which laid the groundwork for a modern feudal system. He notes that while the 13th Amendment outlawed "involuntary servitude," it left "voluntary servitude" legally permissible. The 14th Amendment then established a secondary, federal political status tied to birthright citizenship—a concept Sayles traces directly to the European feudal system brought to England by William the Conqueror in 1066.

In the feudal system, jurisdiction was based on a reciprocal agreement: protection for allegiance. Sayles argues that the federal government acts as the "lord of the manor," offering civil rights and protection in exchange for the citizen's allegiance and submission to administrative public policy. By accepting this relationship, individuals become "voluntary serfs" whose political status is inherited generationally.

The financial matrix: collateralizing human labor

In this system, Sayles explains that gold and silver were replaced by debt notes. Because "property cannot own property," citizens in voluntary servitude cannot truly own assets; instead, they receive certificates of title while the state retains ultimate ownership. Furthermore, Sayles claims that birth certificates function as commercial "warehouse receipts" representing the individual's future labor. This labor is then collateralized to back the national debt, with the IRS acting as an extraction mechanism to pay coupon payments to international bondholders.
