Bond yield math and portfolio risk

Bianco details his transition from a bond bear in 2020 to a buyer as yields reached 5% to 5.6%. In 2020, with 10-year Treasury yields under 1%, modified duration created severe downside risk where a 100-basis-point yield increase produced an 8% capital loss against a meager 1% coupon. Today, with the Bloomberg US Aggregate Index yielding around 5.5% with a duration of 5.6 years, a 100-basis-point yield spike results in a near break-even return. Conversely, an economic slowdown cutting yields by 100 basis points would yield double-digit total returns, offering a favorable risk-reward profile for the first time in six years.

Bianco notes that energy cost increases remain inflationary until demand destruction forces consumers to cut consumption.
