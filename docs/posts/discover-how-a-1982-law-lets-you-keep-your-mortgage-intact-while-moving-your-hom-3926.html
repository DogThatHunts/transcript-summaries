Discover how a 1982 law lets you keep your mortgage intact while moving your home into a revocable living trust and bypass probate for your heirs.

Overview

A 1982 law— the Garn‑St. Germain Act— stops lenders from triggering due‑on‑sale clauses when a home is transferred into a revocable living trust. As long as the owners remain beneficiaries, continue living in the property, and the home has fewer than five units, the mortgage stays unchanged. Setting up the trust and recording a new deed lets the house pass to heirs without probate while keeping the original loan terms.

Key takeaways

  • The Garn‑St. Germain Act protects borrowers from having to pay off the mortgage when the property is placed in a revocable living trust.
  • Requirements: stay as trust beneficiaries, occupy the home, and the property must be a single‑family residence (fewer than five units).
  • The existing mortgage remains in place with the same interest rate and payment schedule.
  • After death, the trust allows the house to transfer to children without going through probate court.
