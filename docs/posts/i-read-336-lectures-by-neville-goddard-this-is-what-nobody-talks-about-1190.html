The early life and training of a mystic

Neville Lancelot Goddard was born in Barbados in 1905 and moved to New York City at age 17 to study drama. He worked as a dancer and actor, touring Europe and performing in vaudeville. His trajectory shifted in 1925 during a tour in England, where a Scotsman introduced him to books on the power of the mind. Shortly after, Goddard met Abdullah, an Ethiopian rabbi living in Harlem, who spent seven years teaching him Hebrew, the Kabbalah, and the psychological meaning of scripture.

Occultist Israel Regardie, who served as Aleister Crowley's secretary, attended Goddard's early lectures in the late 1930s. Regardie wrote about Goddard in The Romance of Metaphysics, describing him as transitioning from a highly disciplined, fanatical vegetarian ascetic into a deeply human, charismatic speaker. Regardie called Goddard's approach the "most magical of all metaphysical systems" he had ever encountered.

Extraordinary personal claims and the New Yorker profile

Goddard frequently used his own life to illustrate his teachings. In 1942, after being drafted into the U.S. Army, he claimed to have secured an honorable discharge solely through sustained imagination. By visualizing himself back in his New York apartment night after night, he was discharged within months. This unusual story caught the attention of The New Yorker, which profiled him in 1943 in an article titled "A Blue Flame on the Forehead."

In another lesser-known account, Goddard claimed to have taken a maximum dose of LSD with a doctor friend in an experiment originally intended to be observed by author Aldous Huxley. Goddard reported that while the chemical experience produced shared hallucinations, it was "nothing" compared to the natural visionary states he regularly accessed through his own consciousness.

The transition from the law to the promise

For his first two decades of teaching, Goddard focused on "the law"—the practical use of imagination to change physical circumstances. However, in 1959, he experienced a series of internal mystical revelations that he identified as the fulfillment of biblical prophecy. This marked his shift to "the promise."

Goddard argued that while the law could be used to acquire temporary earthly wealth and success, the true purpose of human life was to fulfill the promise: to awaken to one's true identity as God. This shift in focus from material manifestation to radical biblical mysticism reportedly cost him half of his lecture audience, who were primarily interested in manifesting wealth and relationships.

A radical model of the afterlife and recursion

Goddard's view of death was highly unconventional. He taught that if an individual dies before fully experiencing the promise, they immediately pass through a "door" and are restored to a physical body on a similar timeline. * Instant restoration: The deceased person is restored to a healthy, young body (around age 20), regardless of their age or physical condition at death. * Total amnesia: The individual has no memory of their previous life and continues to live, marry, struggle, and eventually die again. * The recursive loop: This cycle of recursion repeats across various timelines until the promise unfolds within their consciousness, allowing them to permanently graduate from the earthly cycle.

Scott notes that this model shares structural similarities with the Tibetan Buddhist concept of the bardo, the Gnostic return to the pleroma, and the Hindu concept of moksha (liberation from samsara). However, Goddard's model is distinct in its lack of traditional karma and its emphasis on physical recursion with complete amnesia.

The death of Neville Goddard and the Judas connection

Goddard died on October 1st, 1972, at age 67. While initially reported as a heart attack, historian Mitch Horowitz later corrected the record using Goddard's death certificate, which cited an esophageal rupture.

Frank Carter, a close friend and student of Goddard, believed Goddard's death fulfilled biblical prophecy. Carter recalled a disturbing vision he had years prior of Goddard falling backward and bursting open. He later connected this to the description of Judas's death in Acts 1. When Carter shared this with Goddard, Goddard agreed, stating that Judas represented the betrayal of the "messianic secret"—the act of making hidden spiritual knowledge public. Following Goddard's passing, his wife described his death as his "ascension" and later claimed to have "fused" with him on the other side.
