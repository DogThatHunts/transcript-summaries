Something Strange is Happening in the Housing Market, And No One Is Talking About It

The U.S. housing market is seeing a surge in unsold inventory and slowing home sales, challenging the idea of a housing shortage despite high prices and rising interest rates.

The Housing Market Is experiencing unusual inventory growth

Active housing inventory in the United States has risen to 1.83 million homes, up 7.5% from the same time last year, according to Realtor.com. This marks the fastest annual growth in eight months. Meanwhile, new listings are down 7.5% year-over-year, indicating that much of the current inventory consists of homes that have been on the market for months and remain unsold.

Historical trends show a Market that’s No longer short

Data from the ReVenture app reveals that inventory was 1.2 million homes in October 2019 and 1.3 million in October 2018. Current levels are at 1.18 million, showing that the long-standing narrative of a housing shortage has been over for some time. The speaker argues that media, real estate agents, and mortgage brokers have perpetuated the idea of a tight market to maintain demand and FOMO.

Home prices are growing slowly, and inflation Is outpacing them

The median home price has increased from $383,000 in 2022 to $417,500 today. However, when adjusted for inflation, a $410,000 home in 2023 would be worth $453,000 today. This means home price growth has not kept up with inflation, resulting in negative real growth. The speaker emphasizes that homebuyers should aim to earn more than inflation to stay ahead in this economy.

Mortgage rates are not the only factor behind slow sales

While high mortgage rates are often cited as the primary reason for sluggish home sales, the speaker argues this is a partial explanation. Home sales have been depressed since 2022, with annualized sales hovering between 3.8 and 4.2 million — a level not seen since 2024. Even when rates dropped below 6%, buyer activity did not increase, disproving the claim that mortgage rates alone drive market activity.

Mortgage rate increases have significantly reduced purchasing power. A buyer with a $2,528 monthly budget at a 6.5% rate could afford a $400,000 loan. The job Market and inflation are major economic headwinds

The speaker notes that the job market is weak, wages are falling behind inflation, and economic conditions are not improving. These factors contribute to buyer hesitation. Zillow’s stock has dropped 50–55% over the past year, reflecting investor skepticism about the housing market.

Florida’s Amendment 3 and property tax reforms

Florida’s Amendment 3, passed in 2024, increases the homestead exemption from $50,000 to $150,000 in 2027 and to $250,000 in 2028, applying only to homesteaded homeowners who have lived in the state for at least five years. Non-homesteaded properties are not eligible. The amendment also reduces the annual property assessment cap from 10% to 5%, improving affordability for many.

A critical gap in property tax treatment

The speaker highlights a flaw in the current system: when a homeowner rents out a property, they lose the homestead exemption and face higher taxes. When they return to live in the property, the assessed value does not reset, resulting in permanently higher taxes. This creates a disincentive to rent out a primary residence and may force owners to sell, reducing housing supply.

Conclusion: the Market Is not in a rush

The speaker concludes that the housing market is not in a FOMO-driven phase. Instead, it is facing structural challenges — high inflation, weak job growth, and economic uncertainty — that are slowing buyer activity. Buyers should focus on earning above inflation and assessing long-term affordability.
