The administrative bank levy vs. constitutional due process

Dave Champion opens by highlighting a perceived contradiction: while the government generally requires a court order to seize property from citizens, the IRS can administratively levy bank accounts. He asserts that this practice is part of a "scam" understood by only a few hundred people in the country. Champion, author of "Income Tax Shattering the Myths," claims he has not filed an income tax return or paid income tax in 32 years, and despite the DOJ possessing copies of his book, he remains "unmolested." He argues that ordinary Americans are not subject to the income tax and that ignorance of tax law leads to unnecessary payments or bank account seizures.

Due process for citizens vs. aliens

Champion explains the distinction between the "full-throated due process" constitutionally guaranteed to American citizens and the "minimalistic or scaled down" administrative due process afforded to aliens. He notes that federal courts, including the Supreme Court, have affirmed aliens' right to due process, albeit a truncated version before agency tribunals (Article I courts), not judicial branch courts (Article III courts). This distinction is crucial to understanding bank levies, according to Champion.

The legal implications of form W-9

According to Champion, signing an IRS Form W-9 is an attestation, under penalty of perjury, that the signer is acting as a U.S. agent for a non-resident alien or foreign corporation, and the money received belongs to that foreign entity. He claims that every financial institution in America unlawfully demands a W-9 to open an account, thereby coercing individuals into committing perjury. From the government's perspective, any account opened with a W-9 means the funds belong to a foreign person, thus subjecting them to the truncated administrative due process applicable to aliens.

Tax returns and declared status

Champion argues that signing tax returns, such as Form 1040, not only certifies the factual accuracy of numbers but also declares one's "status." Citing the U.S. Secretary of the Treasury, he claims Form 1040 is exclusively for non-resident aliens with U.S. source income or their domestic agents. Therefore, by signing a 1040, an American citizen is, under penalty of perjury, declaring themselves a non-resident alien or an agent thereof. This declaration, he asserts, then limits their due process rights to the administrative version when the IRS pursues collection.

The misuse of taxpayer identification numbers (TINs)

Champion states that only three types of entities are legally required to provide a TIN when opening an account: non-resident aliens, foreign corporations, and U.S. persons acting as domestic agents for foreign persons, all receiving U.S. source income. He contends that if an individual does not fall into one of these categories, they are not a "taxpayer" for income tax purposes. Banks, he claims, unlawfully demand TINs from everyone, creating a legal presumption that all funds in such accounts are U.S. source income belonging to a foreign person. This presumption allows the IRS to seize funds with only administrative due process.

Bank's unlawful practices and employee firings

Champion presents an example of a bank's response to an account holder who challenged the W-9 requirement. The bank acknowledged the account holder was not a foreign agent but still insisted on the W-9 for "tax residency" documentation. He claims that bank employees who understand and follow the law by not requiring W-9s from ordinary Americans would be "promptly fired," while those at the top knowingly violate the law.

Stopping the IRS
