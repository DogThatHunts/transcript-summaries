Housing market roundup — executive summary for homebuilders

One page for a homebuilder audience, distilled from 228 transcribed videos on the U.S. housing market (Queue rows 1319–2626, August 2026 → September 2026). Each finding cites its source videos; where a claim has been independently verified against primary sources, the verified marker links to that group in the fact-check & verification report. The full picture is in the housing market roundup and the homebuilder briefing.

Builders are now the price setters in U.S. housing: new homes are cheaper than existing homes for the first time on record, builder months' supply sits near 9–10 months, and demand is stuck at roughly 4 million annualised existing sales (rows 1359, 1366, 1774, 2098, 2419). Price cuts and rate buydowns are working at the volume level but at severe margin cost, and the captive-lender channel that makes them work is concentrated in FHA/VA credit that is deteriorating (rows 1319, 1333, 1359). The buyer geography has flipped: Sun Belt and Mountain West inventory surpluses sit alongside Midwest and Northeast scarcity and price growth (rows 1325, 1343, 1355). Institutional demand is being legislated and priced out simultaneously (rows 1361, 1518, 2589).

Key findings

  • Builder inventory is at recession-grade levels and is the single biggest pricing constraint. Builder lots carried 9.1–10.3 months of supply through 2026 versus a 5.8-month long-term average, with 21 months of supply on homes not yet started (rows 1319, 1329, 1333, 1359, 1621) (verified (c), verified (ad)).
  • The two largest builders have already repriced hard, resetting the whole comp set. Lennar cut net prices 25% from the 2022 peak — $491,000 to $370,000 — with construction costs down 12% and cycle time at 122 days, while D.R. Horton is down 12% and reporting 20% cancellations (rows 1319, 1333, 1359, 1774) (verified (b)).
  • New homes now undercut existing homes, an inversion without precedent. Builder median $394,000 versus existing $434,000 in July 2026, a $40,000 gap against a 25-year norm of builders being 17% more expensive (rows 1366, 1774, 2420) (verified (d)).
  • The captive-mortgage engine carries real credit risk. D.R. Horton's capture rate hit 82% in Q3 2026 with 63% FHA/VA at 0–5% down, while FHA delinquency reached 11.88% in Q1 2026 (rows 1319, 1600) (verified (a), verified (e)).
  • Rate relief will not rescue affordability; price does the work. Mortgage rates held 6.5–7% for four years, and a $100,000 cut on a $480,000 house saves $770/month versus $200 for a 1% rate cut (rows 1327, 1485, 2023); Morgan Stanley concludes affordability will not return to pre-2022 levels (row 1328) (verified (w)).
  • Land risk is now regional, not national. Fourteen states hold inventory above 2019 levels and Tennessee, Colorado and Washington show 50–60% surpluses, while Midwest and Northeast markets such as Milwaukee (+5.2%) and Albany (+5.5%) lead price growth (rows 1343, 1355, 1404, 1643, 2098).
  • Institutional single-family demand is exiting as law and math both turn. The 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act bans buyers owning 350+ homes effective January 2027, and cap rates of 4.8% sit below mortgage rates (rows 1336, 1361, 1518) (verified (g), verified (h)).
  • Sun Belt migration, the demand thesis behind most land banks, has collapsed. Florida net migration was 22,000 in 2025, down 93% from peak; Texas hit a 20-year low (rows 1347, 1367, 2419) (verified (i)).

What it implies for a builder

  • Lead with headline price, not buydowns, where supply exceeds nine months — the payment math favours price cuts roughly four-to-one (rows 1327, 1333).
  • Cap FHA/VA share and down-payment-assistance volume in your captive lender; 63% government-backed at 0–5% down is the exposure that converts a price decline into cancellations and repurchase risk (rows 1319, 1600).
  • Reprice or walk option contracts in Sun Belt land where surpluses run 50–60% and migration is down 70–93%; take takedowns in Midwest/Northeast scarcity markets instead (rows 1343, 1355, 1347, 2419).
  • Shift mix toward smaller, lower-price product: median new-home size is back to ~2,100 sq ft and sub-$250,000 resale sales fell 10% while $1M+ grew 4% (rows 1333, 2589).
  • Treat build-to-rent as a sale-to-operator channel with a seven-year clock, not a bulk-buyer backstop, given the 350-home ban and Wall Street net selling of 3,000+ homes in 2026 (rows 1361, 1518).

Watch list

  • Census new-home sales and months' supply: a move below 9 months would confirm the price cuts are clearing inventory (rows 1329, 1359).
  • D.R. Horton and Lennar quarterly capture rate, cancellations and incentive load — the earliest read on whether discounting is still buying volume (rows 1319, 1774).
  • FHA and ICE delinquency/foreclosure prints; Florida already leads at 1 in 2,300 units (rows 1332, 1600).
  • January 2027 implementation of the investor ban, plus any 50-year mortgage legislation, which would require changes to Dodd-Frank (rows 1518, 1845).
